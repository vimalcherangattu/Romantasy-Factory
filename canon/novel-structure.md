# Novel structure

From Appendix A, Part 8, Part 2 (H) and Regis's eight elements.

## Genre contract: Regis's eight elements [EE, via summaries]

Society defined, meeting, barrier, attraction, declaration, point of ritual
death, recognition, betrothal, in any order. Every book's outline places all
eight.

## Beat map (Regis merged with common romance beats) [CI]

| Phase | Romance beats | Fantasy beats | Engagement goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–10% | Society defined; meeting | World rule shown at cost | Liking + shaped gap |
| 10–25% | Attraction; barrier | Inciting threat | Probable-but-uncertain stakes |
| 25–50% | Disclosure ladder; first intimacy | Investigation, training | Half-answers; responsiveness |
| 50% | False declaration or first real choice | Major reversal | Midpoint Mirror (17) |
| 50–75% | Cost ledger rises | Antagonist pressure | Dramatic irony |
| 75–85% | Point of ritual death | All seems lost | Choice Under Pressure (18) |
| 85–95% | Recognition; declaration | Confrontation | Fair twist; transformation proof (24) |
| 95–100% | Betrothal (HEA/HFN) | New balance | Series Hinge (26) |

For a 32-chapter book: chapters 1–3, 4–8, 9–15, 16, 17–24, 25–27, 28–30,
31–32.

## Book-level requirements (checked before review)

- The book answers its own central question; only the series hinge stays open.
- Every planted clue in the outline pays off or is logged as a series thread.
- A midpoint reversal exists and changes the romance.
- The heroine changes a belief, shown by a choice she couldn't have made in
  chapter 1.
- At least one real loss that stays lost.
- The love interest has a goal unrelated to the heroine.

## Order, surprise and callbacks [EE → CI]

- Decide per reveal whether the reader should feel suspense (knows the
  danger), curiosity (knows a gap) or surprise (knows neither). Order the
  scenes accordingly.
- Fair surprise: the clue was present, memorable, and ambiguous in meaning.
- Callbacks gain meaning when the situation around a repeated line has
  flipped. Don't signpost them.
- Surprising yet inevitable endings come from a choice the character's values
  set up all along.

## Series level

- Each book answers its own question and completes one romantic phase (or one
  couple's whole arc, per the bible).
- Long-term secrets are planted in book 1 and pay off in books 2 and 3.
- Loyalties change on a planned arc (the bible's secret map).
- The world expands from the leads' choices.
- Each book ends with a hinge that opens the next book's question and a
  teaser for the next book.
- Release a series in a short burst, one book every 2 to 7 days.

## Development prompts (use in S2 and S3)

1. Interview the love interest about the moment he decided to trust her.
2. Three ways the world rule makes the first kiss dangerous.
3. List the clues for the midpoint twist and where each is planted.
4. Write the scene from the antagonist's POV.
5. What would a reader complain about in this outline?
6. Test this chapter against the scene checklist.
7. Five ways to raise the cost of their alliance.
8. The theme's two legitimate answers, assigned to characters.
9. What does the reader know that she doesn't, act by act?
10. Which book-one detail opens book two?
