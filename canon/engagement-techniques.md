# The 26 engagement techniques

From Appendix A, Part 3, with the psychology from Part 2. The outline agent
tags every chapter with the numbers it uses; the critic checks they landed.

## Mechanisms behind them

- **Curiosity** comes from awareness of a specific gap (Loewenstein 1994),
  peaks at intermediate confidence, and engages reward and memory (Kang 2009;
  Gruber 2014). Specific beats vague. A little knowledge raises the itch.
  Making a prediction raises curiosity. Clues placed while the reader burns
  with a question are remembered. Test: can a reader state the question in one
  sentence?
- **Suspense** needs a liked character and probable but uncertain harm
  (Zillmann; Comisky and Bryant 1982). A 2025 study found delay predicted
  suspense better than the probability curve. Earn liking before threatening.
- **Anomalous suspense:** readers feel suspense about known outcomes (Gerrig
  1989). Suspense about how and at what cost survives knowing what.
- **Zeigarnik is folklore:** interrupted tasks are not remembered better
  (Ghibellini and Meier 2025), but people reliably want to resume them
  (Ovsiankina). Cliffhangers work through the urge to resume: interrupt
  actions in progress.
- **Intimacy:** disclosure met with a response that leaves the discloser
  feeling understood, validated and cared for (Reis and Shaver; Laurenceau
  1998). Escalating mutual disclosure builds closeness (Aron 1997).
- **Order:** the same events produce suspense, curiosity or surprise depending
  on order; surprise improves recall of earlier events.
- **Dopamine** relates to wanting and prediction error, not pleasure. Use
  unpredictability in plot and revelation, never as the love interest's policy
  toward the heroine.
- **Danger is a revelation engine, not a love potion.** Under threat,
  characters choose, and choices reveal values.
- **Productive vs repetitive tension:** productive tension changes state each
  time. Test: is the reason they can't be together new, or stronger?
- **Humour** resets arousal so the next threat lands harder.
- **Romance needs concern; mystery can run on curiosity.**

The examples illustrate the mechanism. Never reuse them verbatim in a book.

## Multi-function scene example [CH]

He dresses her wound (9); she sees the traitor's brand under his collar (11);
she now holds his life (16); she must decide whether to report it, the thing
she fears most because her father died for not reporting (18). One scene moves
romance, mystery, power and character. Aim for scenes that do at least two jobs.

## Usage rules

- Every chapter uses at least 2 techniques; tag them by number in the outline.
- Technique 7 (Interrupted Action) at most every third chapter, and never by
  coincidence.
- Technique 17 at the midpoint, technique 26 in the final chapter.
- Technique 19: losses stay lost. If death or broken vows can be undone,
  readers discount future stakes.
- Fair surprise: the clue was present, memorable, and ambiguous in meaning.
  Cheating: a POV character knew and hid it, or the clue appeared after the
  fact.
