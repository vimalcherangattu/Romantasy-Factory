# Desi romantasy: rules and folklore engines

Direction set by the owner on 19 Oct 2026, after market research: **desi characters and
folklore inside a package US romantasy readers already buy.** Desi readers on amazon.in are a
second market. The evidence: fully desi-world romantasy is a small niche (*The Jasmine Throne*,
about 39k Goodreads ratings in 5 years; indie titles in the hundreds), while a desi heroine in a
familiar package can reach hundreds of thousands of readers (Sangu Mandanna's *The Very Secret
Society of Irregular Witches*, roughly 330k to 386k ratings). [RO]

## Packaging rules

- Lead with the tropes and the danger (canon/first-look.md). The desi element is the twist and
  the texture, never the barrier to entry.
- Titles in English. Series names may carry one evocative Indic word if it's easy to say.
- At most about 10 non-English terms per book, each clear from context on first use. No
  glossary needed to follow the plot. Don't italicise every Indic word.
- Names short and easy for a US reader to say (Asha, Kiran, Devan, Meera, Arjun-style length).
  Avoid names of gods and of famous epic characters for leads.
- Inspired-by settings: invented kingdoms built on real regional history and geography, not
  real places named as fantasy kingdoms.

## Respect lines (hard rules)

- No major deities (Shiva, Vishnu, Krishna, Rama, Ganesha, Durga, Kali, Lakshmi, Parvati and
  the like) or epic heroes (Mahabharata, Ramayana) as romantic or sexual characters, and none
  as villains. Gods may exist offstage, as in the epics' own spirit, or be invented.
- No living religious practice (Tibetan Buddhism, Sikh, Islamic or Christian sacred figures and
  rites) used as magic or romance.
- Caste is never romantic spice or decoration. If it appears, it's treated as harm.
- Folk spirits and beings (yakshi, naga, odiyan, vetala, rakshasa, devta-oracles) are fair game,
  written with their own logic, not as Western monsters in costume.

## Kerala engines

- **Yakshi:** a beautiful, dangerous spirit of the palm and pala trees who lures men at night.
  Gothic romance engine: she can't be loved without being fed, or the man who loves her must
  be bound. Iron nails bind a yakshi.
- **Odiyan:** Malabar's shapeshifting assassins, who become animals at night using a secret
  preparation. Assassin-romance engine: the heroine is an odiyan sent to kill the hero, or he is.
- **Theyyam:** for one night, a performer *becomes* the god or ancestor and speaks with its
  authority. Engine: a lover who is someone else at night; truth that can only be said in the
  god's voice. (Use invented ancestor-spirits, not major deities.)
- **Sarpa kavu:** sacred serpent groves of the naga; to cut one is to invite a curse. Engine:
  a land-and-inheritance war fought over a grove.
- **Kalaripayattu and marma:** the martial art and its pressure points that can heal or kill.
  Engine: a healer-fighter whose touch is lethal or saving.
- **Tharavad matriliny:** large households where property passes through women. Engine: the
  heroine is the heir; the man must marry *into* her house.
- **Spice ports:** pepper as gold, foreign traders, coastal kingdoms (built on the Zamorin and
  Chera ports). Engine: political marriage and trade war.
- **Monsoon:** the season that closes the sea and the roads. Engine: forced proximity.

## Himachal engines

- **Devta courts:** every village has a devta who rules through a human oracle (the *gur*),
  holds court, settles disputes, votes and travels in procession to great gatherings.
  Engine: a court-intrigue romance where the gods are politicians and the oracle can't lie.
- **Jogini and mountain spirits:** spirits of high meadows and passes. Engine: a bargain with
  the mountain.
- **Passes and snow:** passes close for winter and cut valleys off. Engine: forced proximity,
  smuggling, border politics.
- **Dev procession:** gods carried in palanquins meet at a great festival, where alliances and
  feuds play out. Engine: a tournament or summit.

## Wider South Asian folk beings (use with their own rules)

Naga (serpent people, guardians of water and treasure), yaksha (nature guardians and treasure
keepers), vetala (a corpse-riding spirit that tells riddle stories), rakshasa (shapeshifting
demons: use as a people with their own culture, not pure villains), apsara (celestial dancer,
best as an invented order or title).

## The owner's check

The AI beta reader can't tell when a cultural detail is off. The owner reads each review sheet
for cultural accuracy first.
