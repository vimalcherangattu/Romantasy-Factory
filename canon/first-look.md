# First look: titles and premises that sell

A Kindle Unlimited reader decides from a thumbnail, a title and one line of the blurb. A concept
that fails at first look never gets to show its prose. The rule for every concept:
**familiar promise, fresh specifics.** The promise (tropes, danger, desire, a series to binge)
must be instantly recognisable. Only the specifics (world rule, folklore, characters, setting)
should be new. [CI]

## The five-second test

From the title and the hook line alone, a reader must be able to answer:

1. **Who falls for whom?** The two roles, in one phrase each ("a temple dancer and the
   assassin sent to kill her").
2. **Why is it dangerous?** The barrier or threat.
3. **What's the twist?** The one thing they haven't read before.
4. **Which tropes?** At least two of the tropes readers search for, visible in the tags.

If any answer needs a second sentence or a glossary, the concept fails first look.

## Titles

- 1 to 5 words. Short titles read at thumbnail size.
- Concrete nouns with charge: power, danger, desire, a bond, a wound, an oath, a crown, a
  blade, a night, a name, ash, salt, blood, thorns, smoke, a serpent.
- Proven shapes: "[Noun] of [Noun] and [Noun]", "The [Adjective] [Noun]", a single strong word.
  Never copy a famous title's exact frame ("A Court of ... and ...").
- English words only in the title, or a non-English word a US reader can say and guess at
  ("Yakshi" only with a subtitle that explains it). Indic words belong in the book, not the title.
- Must not collide with an existing title in fantasy or romance (checked by the market check).
- The series name carries the genre signal when the title alone can't.

## Hook line

One sentence, at most 30 words: **[heroine's role] must [goal], but [love interest] is
[why he's dangerous to her], and [the world rule's cost].** The stakes sit inside the sentence.
No names the reader doesn't know yet, except the love interest's if it's short.

## Tropes that sell (lead with two or three)

Enemies to lovers, forbidden love, forced proximity, arranged or forced marriage, fated or
magical bond, morally grey or villain love interest, "touch her and die" protector, slow burn,
he falls first, rivals or competition, bodyguard, captive and captor. [RO: reader surveys and
store categories] Avoid love triangles, fake dating and miscommunication plots
(canon/reader-appeal.md).

## Comps

Every concept names two well-known romantasy comps *for positioning only*: the shelf it sits
on. Never imitate their prose, names or invented terms.

## What "fresh" may change, and what it must not

| May be fresh | Must stay familiar |
|---|---|
| The world rule and its cost | The romance arc and HEA/HFN |
| The folklore and setting | The top tropes |
| The heroine's job and skills | A dangerous, desirable love interest |
| The institution or power structure | A series question that pulls to book 2 |
| One inverted convention | The spice level the pen name promises |
