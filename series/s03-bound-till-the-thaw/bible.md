# The Winter Passes: Series Bible

Pen name: Mira Sangla

## Series overview

- **Series title:** The Winter Passes
- **Book 1 title:** Bound till the Thaw
- **Structure:** four books, one couple per book. Each book completes its couple's whole arc and ends HEA. A series thread, the Old Bargain, runs under all four and pays off in Book 4.
- **Primary engine:** Arena. The pressure comes from survival: a closed winter pass, cold, avalanche, fever, pursuit and a hard deadline. Court politics (the regent's coup, the queen's seal, the hill council) sit underneath as stakes. They are never the engine.
- **Subgenre:** Romantasy (core), with romance and fantasy at equal weight. It is survival-adventure romantasy built on Himachal mountain-spirit folklore.
- **Tropes in front:** enemies to lovers (rooted in law against lives, not rudeness), forced proximity, magical bond, bodyguard, one fire, slow burn with a costed first kiss.
- **Inverted convention:** the heroine is the expert and the leader. The soldier is the dependent who has to take her orders on the mountain.
- **Honoured convention:** forced proximity, played straight. There is one fire, one rope and nowhere to go.
- **Heat:** open door, moderate. Each book has 2 to 3 explicit scenes, and each one follows a truth spoken across the rope that changes the bond. Under the world rule every declaration is tested against the cold, so desire on the page is always also a verified truth.
- **Darkness:** survival darkness. It includes frostbite, avalanche, a fever village, graves and a hanging law. Real losses stay lost: Meera's two fingers, Jassi, Dilan and the dead of Ranu. There is no sexual violence. The love interest's danger points outward at the law's enemies and is disciplined toward the heroine.
- **Ending promise per book:**
  - Book 1 *Bound till the Thaw*: HEA for Meera and Rohan.
  - Book 2 *The Winter Oath*: HEA for Ila and Kunal.
  - Book 3 *Salt and Snow*: HEA for Lata and Tej.
  - Book 4 *The Last Crossing*: HEA for Kiran and Asha. All series couples are reunited, and the Old Bargain is paid with a promise, not a life.
- **POV plan:** dual close third person, past tense, alternating between the book's heroine and hero. There is one POV per scene, and a switch is marked with `* * *`. The heroine carries about 60% of chapters. Book 1 POVs are Meera and Rohan only.
- **Calendar:** years are counted in winters of Queen Sarala's reign. Book 1 takes place in the 30th winter. Each later book is the next winter (31st, 32nd, 33rd).
- **Non-English terms:** about six per book, each clear from context on first use and not italicised: jogini, kothi, pattu, chulha, sattu, redbark is English. See Glossary.

## World rules

### Rule 1: The Rope-Bond (the jogini of the high passes)

The jogini are the spirits of the high meadows and passes. They are never seen as people and never take part in romance. They are felt as wind in the stacked stones, cairn-light and the cold. In winter they decide who may cross.

- **What people can do:** a pair may cross a closed winter pass alive if they are rope-bound. The binding works like this:
  - Both partners tie the same knot on each other's wrist with one rope of hand-spun wool, at a jogini cairn.
  - Each partner speaks their name and destination to the stones.
  - The bound pair then sees the winter line. Faint blue cairn-light marks the safe path at night, and the mountain will not take them by whiteout or lost path.
- **What they can never do:**
  - Cross alone in winter. A lone crosser loses the path within an hour and dies.
  - Separate beyond the rope's length before the first thaw, whether the rope is tied or not. Past that length both partners lose heat fast. At about twice the length the path vanishes for them both.
  - Use a rope longer than twelve arm-spans. The spirits refuse it and the knot will not hold. Meera's red rope is eight arm-spans.
  - End the bond early. Cutting or untying the rope ends nothing. The bond lasts until meltwater first runs at the cairn where it was tied, wherever the pair has gone. The rope can be untied for washing, climbing and sleep, but the distance still binds.
- **The cost:**
  - Every lie told to your bound partner costs body heat in proportion to its size. A lie is a statement you believe false and say in order to be believed.
  - Size is measured by how much the truth would change the partner's choices:
    - A small lie whitens the fingertips for an hour.
    - A middling lie brings frostnip and a night of shaking cold.
    - A great lie kills flesh (frostbite, lost fingers).
    - A lie about the partner's life or death kills the liar.
  - The cold lands in the hands first, then the feet and ears.
  - Fire does not warm lie-cold. Only time or the loophole does.
- **What is free:** silence, evasion, refusing to answer, a joke both know to be a joke, lies told to anyone else, and the truth.
- **Promises:** a promise made while bound is a statement of intent. If it is broken while the bond holds, the breaker is struck as by a great lie.
- **Who benefits:**
  - Pairs who trust each other.
  - The smuggler crews of Kothi Shilak, who learned the rope-craft over generations. The crown's law tried to bury that knowledge.
  - The cut-off far valley, which gets medicine and salt only through them.
- **Who is excluded:**
  - The lone and the deceitful.
  - Armies. Columns cannot cross because only pairs bind, and two pairs cannot share one rope. Large groups must cross as separate pairs, each at the spirits' pleasure.
  - Anyone who cannot or will not speak their true name at the cairn.
- **What happens to lovers:** a declaration made while bound is tested against the cold. "I love you" spoken across the rope is either true or it freezes the speaker's hands, so a bound lover can verify love by watching the other's fingers. Courting while bound means every compliment, promise and refusal is on the record of the body.
- **The intimate act that becomes dangerous:** confession and promise.
  - A confession is evidence. Once spoken, it cannot be unsaid.
  - A promise is a contract with the mountain until the thaw.
- **The recurring dilemma:** tell the truth that convicts someone you love, or lie and lose part of your body. In Book 1, Meera can admit what her crew and brother have done or freeze for them.
- **The loophole:** the partner who was lied to can take the cold onto their own skin by touching the liar's skin. The lie-cold passes to them through the touch.
- **What the loophole costs:**
  - The one who takes the cold knows at once that a lie was told and how great it was. They do not learn what the truth is.
  - They pay for the lie in their own flesh.
  - Taking the cold is therefore an act of love and an act of exposure at once. To spare the liar, you must learn that they lied to you.
- **Never suspended:** the rule holds at every climax. No one may lie to their partner for free in a crisis. Each book's point of ritual death is built on this rule biting.

### Rule 2: The Closing Law (the crown of Hemkot)

The Closing Law is a human law, written 60 winters before Book 1, and it is as hard as the mountain.

- **What people can do:** pass-wardens may arrest, seize goods from and hang anyone who crosses a high pass between first snow and first thaw. Only a crown writ of passage, signed by the queen's own hand, makes a winter crossing lawful.
- **What they can never do:**
  - A warden can never pardon. Only the queen can.
  - No one may lawfully shelter, feed or share a fire with an unlawful crosser. Doing so is "harbouring" and carries the same rope.
- **The cost:**
  - The far valley of Neshar is cut off for four to five months every year.
  - Every fever winter kills there, because lowland medicine cannot reach it.
  - Smugglers hang. Wardens die chasing them in storms.
- **Who benefits:**
  - The crown, which controls Neshar's trade through the summer passes.
  - The Taravan Salt Guild, whose monopoly the law protects.
  - The regent of Neshar, who sells winter grain at famine prices.
- **Who is excluded:** Neshar's poor, the sick, the Kothi Shilak crews, and any family with someone on the far side.
- **What happens to lovers:** a warden who loves a smuggler must report them or hang with them. A smuggler who loves a warden is loving the man who carries the noose.
- **The intimate act that becomes dangerous:** sharing a fire, which is harbouring. Under the law, the one fire of the Rope-Hut is a crime.
- **The recurring dilemma:** report the person in front of you, or keep the law that protects the many from the strong.
- **The loophole:** a crown writ of passage can name additional crossers. Rohan's writ can name Meera as his guide.
- **What the loophole costs:** a person named on a writ is entered in the Closing Roll for life. They owe the crown winter service whenever summoned and can never refuse a warden's call. For a smuggler, this means selling her freedom to her enemy.

### How the rules interlock

- The spirit rule forces truth.
- The human law makes truth deadly.
- Together they make every honest word between Meera and Rohan both an intimacy and a possible conviction.
- Book 1's ending, the Winter Crossing Law, changes Rule 2 and never Rule 1. People can change their laws. They cannot change the mountain.

## Places

- **Hemkot:** the invented mountain kingdom of the series, on a salt-and-wool trade route modelled on the upper Sutlej valleys. Its terraced near side faces the lowlands, and the high passes lead to its far valley, Neshar.
  - Sensory signature: everywhere smells faintly of deodar resin and cold stone.
- **Taravan:** the queen's capital on the lower river. It has slate roofs, apricot orchards and the Salt Guild halls. The Closing Roll and the royal archive are kept here.
  - Sensory signature: apricot-wood smoke and rain on wet slate.
- **Kothi Shilak:** the last village below the pass, home of Meera's crew. Their base is the old salt house, a three-storey timber-and-stone kothi with a goat pen underneath.
  - Sensory signature: goat bells, and salt dust that you taste on your lips before you smell it.
- **The Shilak Cairn:** the jogini cairn at the foot of the pass. It is a chest-high ring of stacked flat stones. On the inner face, older carvings show two hands bound by one rope.
  - Sensory signature: a low hum of wind through the stones, which rises to a ringing when a lie is told within the ring.
- **The Long Saddle:** the high snowfield of the Shilak Pass, two days across, wind-scoured and blinding.
  - Sensory signature: the squeak of dry snow under boots, and glare that aches behind the eyes.
- **The Rope-Hut:** a crew-built stone shelter just below the Long Saddle on the far side. It has one hearth (chulha), one sleeping shelf and a juniper woodpile that has to last.
  - Sensory signature: burning juniper and wet wool drying.
- **Ranu:** the fever village in lower Neshar, a cluster of timber houses on a walnut slope.
  - Sensory signature: boiled walnut leaf steeping in every pot, and coughing behind wooden walls.
- **Garh Neshar:** the regent's fortified seat, built of grey stone with a brass assembly bell.
  - Sensory signature: the brass bell's long hum, which you feel in your teeth.
- **The Snow Fair ground:** a frozen river meadow below Garh Neshar where the Neshar assembly meets at midwinter.
  - Sensory signature: roasting barley and drums under a white sky.
- **The Glass Lakes (Book 2):** three frozen tarns on Kunal's hidden route.
  - Sensory signature: ice that booms like a struck drum at night.
- **Lata's wool house, Taravan (Book 3):** combing rooms and a dye yard.
  - Sensory signature: lanolin on the hands and the iron smell of madder-red dye.
- **The Under-Cairn (Books 2 to 4):** a hollow inside the mountain beneath the Shilak Cairn, holding the original carving of the Old Bargain.
  - Sensory signature: the tick of meltwater on stone in total dark.

## Cast

Ages are given at Book 1 (30th winter) unless noted. All romantic leads are adults.

### Meera (29), Book 1 heroine
- **Role:** smuggler-guide of the Shilak Pass and secret head of the crew known to the wardens as the Salt Fox.
- **Want:** to get twelve packets of redbark powder to Ranu before the grey cough kills her brother and the village, and to keep her crew off the gallows.
- **Need:** to rely on someone else's strength instead of carrying everyone alone.
- **False belief:** "The law is only ever a noose for people like us."
- **Contradiction habit:** she scorns the law as paper, yet keeps ledgers as exact as a clerk's. She records every person she has carried, every packet and every price, in a waxed notebook she calls "the count."
- **Voice notes:**
  - Dry, short and practical. She gives orders in imperatives and jokes when frightened.
  - She counts things out loud: steps, fingers, packets.
  - She never says "please" to authority.
  - She calls Rohan "Captain" until she stops.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - The winter line over Shilak.
  - The rope-craft and the lie-cold rule.
  - That her redbark came through a lowland middleman she has never met.
  - That Rohan has hunted her crew for three winters.
  - That Kiran froze an ear "on a bad crossing" two winters ago.
- **Does not know:** that Kiran was on Dilan's rope; that her supplier is Varun; that Rohan carries the queen's seal.

### Rohan (35), Book 1 hero
- **Role:** captain of the queen's pass-wardens on the Shilak side, a commoner who rose by competence.
- **Want:** to deliver the queen's seal to the Snow Fair in Neshar within twenty days and stop Regent Pritam's coup. His second want is to bring in the Salt Fox crew.
- **Need:** to admit that the law he enforces has killed people he could have saved.
- **False belief:** "If I bend once, nothing holds."
- **Contradiction habit:** he says rules cannot bend, yet he secretly pays the seizure fines of poor families from his own wage. He also carries the clean-cut end of a dead man's rope in his coat.
- **Voice notes:**
  - Measured and formal, with full sentences.
  - He asks precise questions and goes silent instead of lying, even before the bond.
  - His humour is rare and bone-dry.
  - He calls Meera "Guide" because he refuses the crew name.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - The Closing Law to the letter.
  - The regent's plan to be invested at the Snow Fair on the claim that the queen is dying.
  - That his lieutenant Varun chose the approach route.
  - That a Kothi smuggler was on the rope with Dilan the night Dilan died.
  - His writ orders him to deliver the Salt Fox at the thaw.
- **Does not know:** that the Salt Fox is Meera; that Varun is the regent's man; that the smuggler on Dilan's rope was Kiran.

### Kiran (24 in Book 1; 27 in Book 4), Meera's brother, Book 4 hero
- **Role:** crew runner. He is in Ranu nursing the sick when Book 1 opens and catches the grey cough.
- **Want (Book 1):** to keep Ranu alive until his sister comes.
- **Want (Book 4):** to pay the Old Bargain himself.
- **Need:** to forgive himself for Dilan without needing to die for it.
- **False belief:** "I owe a life, so mine is the one to spend."
- **Contradiction habit:** he jokes constantly and cannot stand silence, yet he has never once said Dilan's name aloud.
- **Voice notes:** quick and teasing, with deflecting questions. He calls Meera "Didi" when he is scared.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - He was on Dilan's rope.
  - Dilan cut the rope himself to save him.
  - He kept Dilan's warden badge.

### Asha (28 in Book 1; 31 in Book 4), Book 4 heroine
- **Role:** boss of a rival smuggler crew from the lower salt road. In Book 1 she is hired to track the seal.
- **Want (Book 1):** a crown pardon for her crew, bought by delivering the seal-carrier to the regent's people.
- **Want (Book 4):** to stop Kiran from walking out alone.
- **Need:** to tie herself to people instead of keeping every bond cuttable.
- **False belief:** "Nobody stays, so tie nothing you can't cut."
- **Contradiction habit:** she claims to belong to no one, yet she ties every knot in an old family pattern her grandmother taught her and refuses to learn any other.
- **Voice notes:** brash and mercantile. She prices everything, swears inventively with invented oaths, and goes very quiet when moved.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - That Varun pays her.
  - That the seal exists.
  - Her family knot, though not that it is Hemi's knot.

### Ila (30 in Book 1; 31 in Book 2), Rohan's sister, Book 2 heroine
- **Role:** crown pass-surveyor. Winter closed while she was surveying Neshar, so she is stuck there at the start of Book 1.
- **Want:** to finish the first true map of the high passes and be named Chief Surveyor in her own right.
- **Need:** to see that a true map is still a choice about who gets power.
- **False belief:** "A map is only true or false, never good or bad."
- **Contradiction habit:** she claims to be neutral, yet she names every unnamed peak after people she likes.
- **Voice notes:** brisk and exact, a measurer. She corrects other people's distances, and laughs loudly and suddenly.
- **Knows at series start:** surveying, the regent's court gossip, and that her grandfather "died on the pass" 60 winters ago, though not how.

### Kunal (32 in Book 1; 33 in Book 2), Book 2 hero
- **Role:** prince of Neshar and nephew of Regent Pritam. He secretly leads the hill council's rebels. In Book 1 he is the masked buyer who pays for Ranu's medicine.
- **Want:** self-rule for Neshar at the spring council, and to keep its hidden ways unmapped.
- **Need:** to stop treating every truth as a weapon.
- **False belief:** "Whoever knows the way owns the people."
- **Contradiction habit:** he preaches openness to the hill council, yet he keeps every secret route in his head and shares none.
- **Voice notes:** charming and courtly with sharp edges. He answers questions with questions and softens only with children.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - His uncle's coup plan.
  - Three hidden summer routes.
  - That someone in Hemkot's wardens is selling crown medicine.

### Lata (40 in Book 1; 42 in Book 3), Book 3 heroine
- **Role:** widowed wool trader of Taravan. She supplies Meera's crew with wool, and the red rope of Book 1 was spun in her house.
- **Want (Book 3):** to get her winter wool convoy and her late husband's ledgers to Neshar, and keep her house solvent.
- **Need:** to believe that a broken thing can be mended rather than only written off.
- **False belief:** "What's broken stays broken; you only keep the accounts."
- **Contradiction habit:** she claims to have forgotten Tej entirely, yet she still dyes one skein a season in the exact red he once called his favourite.
- **Voice notes:** warm, tart and unhurried. She uses trade sayings and is the funniest adult in any room.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - Wool, prices and the Salt Guild's books.
  - That Tej's soldiers burned her family's wool sheds 22 winters ago (counted from Book 1, 20 winters before).
  - That her husband Mohit died of fever in a closed winter.

### Tej (43 in Book 1; 45 in Book 3), Book 3 hero
- **Role:** Regent Pritam's general. In Book 1 he quietly refuses the order to fire Ranu. He is exiled after the Snow Fair as the regent's man, and no one knows about his mercy.
- **Want (Book 3):** to cross back into Hemkot and warn the queen of Pritam's mercenary army before spring.
- **Need:** to accept a second chance he did not earn by suffering.
- **False belief:** "I chose wrong once; men like me don't get a second."
- **Contradiction habit:** he insists he deserves nothing, yet he keeps exact soldierly care of everything he is given: boots, horses, a borrowed shawl.
- **Voice notes:** few words and a low register. He speaks in plain soldier's orders and goes oddly gentle with animals.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - The regent's forces.
  - That he burned Lata's sheds under orders in the Neshar Rising.
  - That he still loves her.

### Queen Sarala (61)
- **Role:** queen of Hemkot, ill but alive.
- **Want:** to keep the kingdom whole past her death.
- **Need:** to admit that her ancestors' law hid a debt.
- **False belief:** "A crown survives by never confessing."
- **Contradiction habit:** she claims to be ruthless, yet she signs every winter pardon her clerks bring her.
- **Voice notes:** short and dry, with royal plurals she drops in private.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - The true story of the Old Bargain from the royal archive.
  - That the last payer, 60 winters ago, was Rohan and Ila's grandfather.

### Regent Pritam (51)
- **Role:** regent of Neshar for the crown and Kunal's uncle. He plans to be invested as ruler at the Snow Fair.
- **Want:** Neshar for himself.
- **Need:** none the story grants him; he is a pressure, not the central conflict.
- **False belief:** "Scarcity is loyalty."
- **Contradiction habit:** he speaks of protecting the valley while he hoards its grain.
- **Voice notes:** silken, with numbers in every sentence.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - His coup plan.
  - That Varun sabotaged the approach to the pass.

### Varun (38)
- **Role:** Rohan's lieutenant and secretly the regent's man. He sells stolen crown redbark to the Kothi crew through a middleman, chose Rohan's approach route and set off the avalanche.
- **Want:** a lordship in Neshar.
- **Need:** n/a.
- **False belief:** "Loyalty is a price nobody's met yet."
- **Contradiction habit:** he is fastidious about uniform rules while he breaks every oath.
- **Voice notes:** friendly, and calls Rohan "Captain-ji" with fond mockery.
- **Knows at series start:** everything about his own scheme. He does not know that Meera is the Salt Fox.

### Dilan (died aged 23, two winters before Book 1)
- **Role:** Rohan's youngest warden, whom Rohan loved like a brother. He died on the pass chasing a smuggler in a storm, after cutting his own rope to keep Kiran from being dragged off the ice.
- Appears only in memory and through objects.

- **Want:** to retire to his sister's orchard.
- **Need:** n/a.

- **Contradiction habit:** he grumbles about every crossing and volunteers for all of them.
- **Voice notes:** gruff, and names every goat.
- **Knows at series start:** the crew's routes.
- **Fate:** dies in the avalanche in Book 1, chapter 3. This loss stays lost.

### Bindu (34)
- **Role:** the crew's ropemaker at Kothi Shilak.
- **Want:** a lawful trade.
- **Need:** n/a.
- **False belief:** "Hands that work don't need names."
- **Contradiction habit:** she is shy with people and loud with rope.
- **Voice notes:** sings knot-counts under her breath; all her songs are invented.

### Nimmi (50)

- **Want:** redbark before the children die.

## Relationship worksheet

### Couple 1: Meera and Rohan (Book 1)

1. **What each consciously wants, visible in chapter 1.**
   - Meera wants to cross Shilak with twelve packets of redbark for Ranu, where her brother's note says the grey cough has started, and to keep Rohan's wardens from catching her crew at the salt house.
   - Rohan wants to reach the Snow Fair with the queen's seal in twenty days. In chapter 1 he rides into Kothi Shilak hunting the Salt Fox crew on the way.
2. **What each secretly needs.**
   - Meera needs to let someone carry part of the load, to be the competent one who is finally relied upon and who relies back.
   - Rohan needs to say aloud that the Closing Law killed people at Ranu, and Dilan, and that he signed the blockades.
3. **The belief that holds each back, as a sentence they would say.**
   - Meera: "The law is only ever a noose for people like us."
   - Rohan: "If I bend once, nothing holds."
4. **What each misunderstands about the other.**
   - Meera believes Rohan hangs smugglers for promotion and enjoys it. He has hanged two lowland smugglers in three winters and seized her crew's loads twice.
   - Rohan believes Meera smuggles for profit. Taravan market prices for Kothi salt are high, and he has never seen that she charges the rich double so the poor cross free.
5. **What each can offer that nobody else can.**
   - Meera is the only living guide who knows the Long Saddle's winter line, reads avalanche snow and knows the rope-craft. Without her the seal never reaches the Snow Fair.
   - Rohan carries the seal and the crown's authority. He can get medicine into Ranu past the regent's men and enter evidence before the Neshar assembly. He is the only person placed to change the Closing Law.
6. **What makes loving the other costly, in world-rule terms.**
   - Every true answer Meera gives is evidence against her crew under the Closing Law.
   - Every true answer Rohan gives about his writ tells her he must deliver her at the thaw.
   - Any promise either makes to protect the other binds them to the mountain until the thaw.
   - Sharing the Rope-Hut's one fire is harbouring, a hanging crime for him.
7. **Three observable choices that make trust rational.**
   - Chapter 6: Meera takes Rohan's lie-cold by touch when he claims his ribs are fine. She learns he lied and pays for it herself instead of letting him slow them both.
   - Chapter 11: while Rohan is fevered, Meera holds the seal and could walk it to the regent's bounty. She puts it back in his coat with the cord retied his way.
   - Chapter 26: Meera lies about Kiran and her fingers whiten. Rohan could take her hand and know for certain. He chooses not to reach for her.
8. **What changes, in them and the world, because they fall in love.**
   - Meera trusts a law enough to write one, and puts her name and her ledgers into the crown's record.
   - Rohan bends publicly. He admits the law's victims before the assembly and declines to arrest Kiran.
   - The world gets the Winter Crossing Law: licensed bound guides, medicine always passes, and no one hangs for a death on a bound crossing. The jogini notice the law and speak at the thaw.
9. **Why these two are uniquely suited to challenge each other.** Both are exact accountants of life: she counts the people carried, he counts the law kept. The rope strips each of their usual armour. She cannot bluff, and he cannot hide behind regulation, because only true answers are free. Each sees precisely where the other's ledger is missing names.
10. **What each refuses to give up, even for the other.**
    - Meera will not give up her crew's names or her brother.
    - Rohan will not give up the seal or the mission. Even arrested and in chains, he insists the seal reaches the Snow Fair before anything else.
11. **What the reader knows before either character.**
    - From Rohan's POV in chapter 9, the reader knows he carries Dilan's clean-cut rope end and has sworn to find the smuggler.
    - From Meera's POV in chapter 22, the reader learns that the smuggler was Kiran, seven chapters before Rohan does.
    - From Rohan's POV, the reader knows his writ names the Salt Fox before Meera knows she has been named.

### Couple 2: Ila and Kunal (Book 2)

1. **Want, visible in chapter 1.**
   - Ila wants to complete the crown's map of Shilak's hidden routes by spring and be named Chief Surveyor.
   - Kunal wants to keep those routes secret and win Neshar's self-rule at the spring council.
2. **Secret need.**
   - Ila needs to own that her maps are choices.
   - Kunal needs to share a truth without first calculating its use.
3. **Holding belief.**
   - Ila: "A map is only true or false, never good or bad."
   - Kunal: "Whoever knows the way owns the people."
4. **Misunderstanding.**
   - Ila thinks Kunal is his uncle's kind of prince, an aristocrat playing at rebellion for a throne, because he is Pritam's nephew and heir.
   - Kunal thinks Ila is the queen's spy-cartographer sent to open Neshar to garrisons, because that is what her commission literally says.
5. **Unique offer.**
   - Ila can draw a map that both courts would accept as true. She is Rohan's sister, so a warden-captain will back her.
   - Kunal knows the hidden ways and the Glass Lakes route to the Under-Cairn, which no crown map has.
6. **Cost in world-rule terms.**
   - Bound for a winter survey, every true answer Kunal gives about the hidden ways becomes a line on a crown map.
   - Every true answer Ila gives about her orders tells him she is mapping his people's defences.
   - A promise to hide a route binds her against her surveyor's oath.
7. **Three trust choices.**
   - Ila leaves a hidden route off her field sheet the night he shows it to her, and tells him so truthfully.
   - Kunal takes her lie-cold at the Glass Lakes rather than let her freeze, and learns she lied about her orders.
   - Kunal gives her the Under-Cairn route before he knows what she will do with it.
8. **What changes.** Ila breaks crown exclusivity: she co-signs one Shared Map with Neshar's own place names and gives copies to the queen and the hill council alike. Kunal shares his routes. Neshar becomes a self-governing valley under the crown, with a council seat at Taravan. The Under-Cairn is found.
9. **Why uniquely suited.** Both hoard knowledge as power: she in ink, he in memory. The rope forces each to share at the other's pace.
10. **What each refuses to give up.**
    - Ila will not draw a false line, even to protect him.
    - Kunal will not let a crown garrison into the hill villages.
11. **What the reader knows first.** Through a scene in the queen's POV-free archive letter, delivered to Ila midbook and read by the reader in Ila's POV, the reader learns that the queen wants the Under-Cairn found because the Old Bargain once took Ila's grandfather. Kunal does not know this. Ila withholds it as a free silence, never as a lie.

### Couple 3: Lata and Tej (Book 3)

1. **Want, chapter 1.**
   - Lata wants to get her winter wool convoy and Mohit's ledgers over Shilak to the hill council before Pritam's spring war.
   - Tej wants to cross back into Hemkot from exile, which is a hanging offence, to warn the queen of Pritam's mercenaries.
2. **Secret need.**
   - Lata needs to mend rather than write off.
   - Tej needs to accept mercy that he has not paid for in pain.
3. **Holding belief.**
   - Lata: "What's broken stays broken; you only keep the accounts."
   - Tej: "I chose wrong once; men like me don't get a second."
4. **Misunderstanding.**
   - Lata thinks Tej chose the regent's side 22 winters ago for rank. He did take the promotion.
   - Tej thinks Lata married Mohit out of hatred for him, because she married within the year.
5. **Unique offer.**
   - Lata's ledgers prove Pritam's grain-hoarding.
   - Tej knows Pritam's army, and he is the only witness to who ordered Ranu burned.
6. **Cost in world-rule terms.**
   - Bound, Tej cannot deny the sheds. Any softening lie would freeze him.
   - Under the Winter Crossing Law, exiles may not cross, so harbouring him puts Lata's house and licence at risk.
7. **Three trust choices.**
   - Tej confesses the shed-burning in full before she asks.
   - Lata takes his cold when he lies that he is not afraid to hang.
   - Tej hands her the evidence that could buy his pardon and lets her decide whether to use it.
8. **What changes.** Lata stands surety for his pardon, pledging her house as bond. Tej testifies before the queen. Pritam's war collapses before it starts, and the queen's archive is opened.
9. **Why uniquely suited.** They loved each other at twenty, so each knows the other's young self. Each can measure exactly how far the other has changed.
10. **What each refuses to give up.**
    - Lata will not give up Mohit's memory. She loved her husband and will not pretend otherwise.
    - Tej will not ask for forgiveness.
11. **What the reader knows first.** The reader saw Tej lower the torches at Ranu in Book 1, chapter 27. Lata does not know it until Book 3's midpoint.

### Couple 4: Kiran and Asha (Book 4)

1. **Want, chapter 1.**
   - Kiran wants to reach the Under-Cairn alone before first snow and pay the Old Bargain with his life.
   - Asha wants to stop him, and claims the motive is a debt she owes Meera's crew.
2. **Secret need.**
   - Kiran needs to forgive himself without dying.
   - Asha needs to tie a knot she cannot cut.
3. **Holding belief.**
   - Kiran: "I owe a life, so mine is the one to spend."
   - Asha: "Nobody stays, so tie nothing you can't cut."
4. **Misunderstanding.**
   - Kiran thinks Asha follows him for the bounty the Salt Guild has posted on anyone who interferes with the Bargain. She did take Varun's money once.
   - Asha thinks Kiran wants to die. He wants to repay a debt and believes the only coin is himself.
5. **Unique offer.**
   - Asha's family knot is Hemi's knot, the only knot the Under-Cairn accepts.
   - Kiran's guilt makes him the one person who will not lie to the spirits about what is owed.
6. **Cost in world-rule terms.**
   - Bound, Asha cannot pretend she does not care. Kiran cannot pretend he plans to come back.
   - A promise to come home would freeze him if he still means to walk out alone.
7. **Three trust choices.**
   - Kiran says Dilan's name aloud to Asha for the first time.
   - Asha tells the truth about Varun's money.
   - Kiran unties the rope at the cliff edge and asks rather than makes her come.
8. **What changes.** They pay the Old Bargain with the founders' true promise:
   - Every winter of their lives they will be rope-bound and carry whoever needs to cross.
   - They will never lie to each other.
   - The bond becomes lifelong in winter.
   - The pass stays open forever.
9. **Why uniquely suited.** Each wants to leave first. She leaves people, and he plans to leave life. Each can see the other's exit door.
10. **What each refuses to give up.**
    - Asha will not give up her crew's freedom; they are not bound by her promise.
    - Kiran will not give up the debt; he insists it is paid, not forgiven.
11. **What the reader knows first.** The reader knows from Book 3's hinge, through Lata's POV, that the Bargain's original price was a promise and not a death. Kiran does not learn this until the Under-Cairn.

## Book arcs

### Book 1: Bound till the Thaw (Meera and Rohan)
- **Central question:** will Meera get the medicine through to Ranu, and will Rohan arrest her?
- **Answer:**
  - Yes, the medicine reaches Ranu.
  - No, he does not arrest her. Instead they write the Winter Crossing Law together.
- **Romantic phase completed:** the whole arc, from enemies to bound allies to lovers to a chosen bond at the thaw.
- **Beat placement (32 chapters):**
  - Chapters 1 to 3: Kothi Shilak and the fever note. Rohan arrives and the chase begins.
    - Chapter 3: the avalanche kills Jassi and buries the approach. The jogini bind Meera and Rohan at the Shilak Cairn.
  - Chapters 4 to 8: attraction and barrier.
    - Chapter 6: Meera takes Rohan's lie-cold, the first use of the loophole.
    - Chapter 7: Asha's crew is spotted on their trail.
  - Chapters 9 to 15: the disclosure ladder.
    - A nightly firewood question: one juniper stick burned per refused question, so evasion has a cost.
    - Chapter 11: Meera returns the seal.
    - The regent's hunters close in.
  - Chapter 16 is the midpoint, in the Rope-Hut during a storm. See below.
  - Chapters 17 to 24: descent to Ranu.
    - Kiran is sick, and Rohan sees the graves.
    - Chapter 20: first explicit scene.
    - Chapter 22: Kiran tells Meera he was on Dilan's rope.
    - Chapter 24: the masked buyer, Kunal.
  - Chapters 25 to 27: Varun arrives, Rohan is arrested, and Ranu is threatened with fire.
  - Chapters 28 to 30:
    - Asha turns on Varun.
    - Kiran confesses to Rohan.
    - At the Snow Fair the seal is pressed, followed by the second explicit scene.
  - Chapters 31 and 32:
    - Drafting the law.
    - Chapter 31: optional third explicit scene.
    - The thaw.
- **Midpoint reversal (chapter 16, Rope-Hut, storm):**
  - Meera admits truthfully that she is the Salt Fox.
  - Rohan admits his writ orders him to deliver the Salt Fox at the thaw.
  - Together they work out that the avalanche was set and that Varun chose the route. Meera realises her redbark comes from crown stores stolen by Varun, so she has been funding the coup Rohan is fighting.
  - Both are complicit, and the first half rereads: her lifeline was his enemy's purse.
  - The first kiss follows, and it is costed. Rohan promises, "I will not let you fall on this mountain." That promise is now a contract with the cold.
- **Point of ritual death (chapters 25 to 27):**
  - Varun arrests Rohan as a seal-thief and traitor, and names Kiran as Dilan's killer to break him.
  - Rohan asks Meera, "Was Kiran on Dilan's rope?" She says, "No."
  - It is a great lie. Her left ring and little fingers die white in her glove.
  - She keeps the hand from him, and he chooses not to reach.
  - That night the regent's men prepare to fire Ranu. Meera nearly dies of cold and grief.
- **Heroine's belief change and the choice that proves it:**
  - Belief changes from "The law is only ever a noose for people like us" to "A law is a rope; it depends who ties it."
  - The proof comes at the Snow Fair, chapter 30. Meera stands before the Neshar assembly and enters "the count," her own ledgers, into the crown's record under her own name, as evidence against Varun's stolen-medicine trade.
  - This is the act a smuggler never commits. It incriminates her and trusts the law to judge fairly, and she could not have done it in chapter 1.
- **Hero's change:**
  - Rohan tells the assembly that the Closing Law's blockade killed Ranu's people, and that he signed it.
  - He declines to arrest Kiran after learning the truth.
- **Real losses that stay lost:** Jassi; Meera's two fingers; the fever dead of Ranu; Dilan.
- **Memorable moments planned:**
  - First act of protection: chapter 3. Rohan shields Meera from the avalanche's second slide with his own body, then lets her give every order after.
  - Private vulnerability: chapter 9. Rohan alone with Dilan's rope end, retying and untying the clean-cut fibres.
  - Reframing line: "Watch my hands," said first as a threat in chapter 4, when Rohan means he will see if she lies. In chapter 30 he says it as his declaration: he says "I love you" and holds up his bare hands, and they stay warm.
  - Final sacrifice: the two fingers.
- **Series hinge:**
  - At the thaw, meltwater runs at the Shilak Cairn and the bond ends. Meera and Rohan retie an ordinary rope by choice, which is their betrothal.
  - Then the stones ring, and for the first time in living memory the jogini speak in the cold itself. Every person at the cairn feels the words in their hands: the Old Bargain is unpaid. The pass will close forever after the fourth winter unless someone pays.

### Book 2: The Winter Oath (Ila and Kunal)
- **Central question:** will Ila map the hidden passes for the queen or hide them for Neshar?
- **Answer:** she does neither alone. She maps them truly and shares the map with both sides.
- **Romantic phase completed:** the whole arc, from mutual suspicion to forced bond to lovers to a joint oath.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Ila learns why the queen wants the Under-Cairn found. The last Bargain payment, 60 winters ago, was a life: her own grandfather, a warden, who walked into the snow. The Closing Law was written the next spring to make sure no one crossed in winter and asked questions. Her neutral map is suddenly a family grave map, and Kunal's secrecy is suddenly protecting the debt.
- **Point of ritual death:** Pritam's loyalists capture Kunal's route-keepers. To protect Kunal, Ila lies to him about where she has hidden her field sheets. The lie is great, and the cold takes her feet. Kunal takes the cold through the loophole, learns she lied, and believes she has given the routes to the queen.
- **Heroine's belief change and choice:**
  - Belief changes from "A map is only true or false" to "A true map still says whose it is."
  - Proof: she breaks her surveyor's oath of crown exclusivity. She co-signs the Shared Map with Kunal, using Neshar's own names for every peak, and hands identical copies to the queen and the hill council at the same hour.
- **Real loss:** a hill route-keeper, Kunal's oldest friend, dies under capture.
- **Series hinge:** in the Under-Cairn, Ila and Kunal find the original carving: two hands on one rope and a line of old script, "carried till no thaw." In the same winter Pritam escapes Garh Neshar.

### Book 3: Salt and Snow (Lata and Tej)
- **Central question:** can Lata and Tej take their second chance across a war? Concretely, will Lata stand surety for the exiled general whose soldiers burned her family's sheds?
- **Answer:** yes, and the war ends before it begins.
- **Romantic phase completed:** the whole second-chance arc.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Lata learns from Ranu's healer Nimmi that Tej refused the order to fire Ranu in Book 1, so he was exiled for mercy. In the same scene she learns he did burn her sheds, exactly as she always believed. Both things are true, and her accounts no longer balance.
- **Point of ritual death:** at the border cairn the wardens move to hang Tej under the exile clause. To spare Lata's house, he lies to her that he never loved her. The lie is great, and the cold nearly stops his heart. Lata takes the cold by touch, learns it was a lie, and loses feeling in her own hands for the rest of the winter.
- **Heroine's belief change and choice:**
  - Belief changes from "What's broken stays broken" to "Some things mend, and the mend is stronger."
  - Proof: Lata pledges her wool house as surety for Tej's pardon before the queen. It is the first unbalanced account of her life.
- **Real loss:** Lata's convoy oxen and half her wool are lost in a slide, and her house's finances never fully recover.
- **Series hinge:** in Mohit's ledgers Lata finds an archive copy of Hemi's original words. The Bargain's first price was a promise, and the crown changed it to a death to keep winter crossing forbidden. That same night Kiran leaves a note: he is going to pay with his life.

### Book 4: The Last Crossing (Kiran and Asha)
- **Central question:** who pays the spirits' Old Bargain, and with what?
- **Answer:** Kiran and Asha pay it with a lifelong promise, not a life.
- **Romantic phase completed:** the whole arc, from former rivals to a forced bond to lovers to a permanent bond.
- **Series couples unite:** Meera and Rohan, Ila and Kunal, and Lata and Tej each cross as bound pairs to the Under-Cairn behind them.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Asha's family knot opens the Under-Cairn, which reveals she is Hemi's descendant. Her "tie nothing you can't cut" was her family's inherited fear after Hemi's death.
- **Point of ritual death:** Kiran unties the rope at the Under-Cairn's edge and walks into the dark to pay alone. The cold takes him toward death.
- **Heroine's belief change and choice:**
  - Belief changes from "Tie nothing you can't cut" to "Some knots are the point."
  - Proof: Asha ties Hemi's knot to Kiran and speaks the founders' true promise. She binds herself to him and to the pass every winter for life, a knot that can never be cut.
- **Real loss:** Queen Sarala dies at the thaw, having confessed the crown's alteration of the Bargain in her last writ.
- **Series close (final hinge):** the pass will never close again. Kiran and Asha will be bound every winter for life. The final image is the four couples at the Shilak Cairn at the first thaw, and a stranger at the stones asking to be carried across.

## Theme

**Question:** when a law fails the people in front of you, do you keep it for the people it protects, or break it for the ones it is killing?

- **Answer one: keep the law.** Rohan holds this answer in Book 1, and Ila holds it in Book 2.
  - Without the Closing Law, the strong would take the passes. Grain merchants, warlords and the regent would all use them.
  - Every bent rule becomes a price someone can pay. Law is the only protection the weak have against the strong.
- **Answer two: break the law.** Meera holds this answer in Book 1, Kunal in Book 2 and Asha in Book 4.
  - A law that hangs the people saving lives has no claim on anyone. Obedience while children die is complicity.
- **Resolution pattern:** neither lead converts. Each book builds a third thing that keeps the protective purpose and repairs the harm:
  - Book 1: the Winter Crossing Law.
  - Book 2: the Shared Map.
  - Book 3: a pardon with surety.
  - Book 4: a promise in place of a death.
- **Counterpoint:** the mountain is the one law no one can bend. Its rule is cruel and fair, and that makes human laws look negotiable by contrast.

## Cover style

- **Series look:** each cover has one object at the centre, in tight focus, on a cold, wide mountain ground. The object is always touched by the same red wool rope, so the four books read as a set at thumbnail size.
- **Palette (4 colours):**
  - snow white, with a warm-grey shadow
  - glacier blue
  - deep slate ink
  - wool red, a madder red, used only on the rope and the title's single accent line
- **Central motif per book:**
  1. *Bound till the Thaw*: two gloved hands bound wrist to wrist by the red wool rope, dusted with snow.
  2. *The Winter Oath*: a brass surveyor's compass hanging from the red rope over a map etched in frost.
  3. *Salt and Snow*: a skein of red wool unspooling across a snowfield, crossing a fallen soldier's sword hilt.
  4. *The Last Crossing*: a stacked-stone cairn with the red rope wound around its top stone, and light breaking through a gap in the stones.
- **Typography:**
  - Title: tall, condensed serif capitals with sharp chiselled wedge serifs, widely letter-spaced, in snow white with a faint frost-crack texture.
  - Accent: a thin red horizontal rule under the title, echoing the rope.
  - Pen name "Mira Sangla": clean geometric sans-serif small capitals, very widely tracked, at the bottom in white.
  - Series line "The Winter Passes · Book N": the same sans-serif at small size above the title.
- **Composition:**
  - The motif sits slightly below centre.
  - A jagged blue-white ridge line runs across the lower third.
  - The title occupies the top third over a pale sky.
  - The pen name is at the bottom edge.
  - The vertical spacing is the same on every book.
- **Mood:** cold, intimate and high-stakes, like breath held in frozen air. Warmth comes only from the red rope.
- **Restrictions:** no faces and no likeness of any real person. There are no figures beyond hands. No religious symbols, prayer flags, monasteries or real shrines. No trademarked imagery or invented terms from other works.

## Glossary

- **jogini:** spirits of the high meadows and passes. In this series they are felt as wind in the cairn stones, cairn-light and cold, and they rule winter crossings.
- **kothi:** a tall timber-and-stone house of the high villages. Meera's crew uses the old salt kothi.
- **pattu:** a thick woven woollen shawl worn in the high valleys.
- **chulha:** a clay or stone hearth. The Rope-Hut has one.
- **sattu:** roasted barley flour mixed with water or salt tea, the crossers' trail food.
- **Didi:** "elder sister." Kiran's name for Meera when he is afraid.
- **rope-bond / bound pair:** the jogini's binding of two people, tied at a cairn and lasting until the first thaw.
- **lie-cold:** body heat lost to a lie told to one's bound partner. It cannot be warmed by fire.
- **taking the cold:** the loophole in which the partner who was lied to draws lie-cold onto their own skin by touch, and learns that a lie was told.
- **cairn-light:** faint blue glow on the stones that marks the winter line for bound pairs at night.
- **winter line:** the safe path over a closed pass, visible only to the bound.
- **first thaw:** the day meltwater first runs at the cairn where a bond was tied. It ends the bond.
- **the Closing Law:** Hemkot's law forbidding winter crossings on pain of hanging.
- **harbouring:** the crime of sheltering, feeding or sharing a fire with an unlawful crosser.
- **writ of passage:** a crown document signed by the queen's own hand that makes a winter crossing lawful.
- **the Closing Roll:** the register of everyone named on a writ, who owe winter service for life.
- **the Salt Fox:** the wardens' name for Meera's crew and its unknown leader.
- **the count:** Meera's waxed ledgers recording every person and packet she has carried.
- **redbark:** a lowland bark powder that treats the grey cough.
- **the grey cough:** a winter fever of Neshar, deadly to children and the old.
- **the Snow Fair:** Neshar's midwinter assembly and fair, held on the frozen river meadow.
- **the Old Bargain:** the founders' pact with the jogini, which opened the pass and is now owed.
- **the Under-Cairn:** the hollow beneath the Shilak Cairn where the Old Bargain is carved.
- **the Winter Crossing Law:** the law Meera and Rohan write at the end of Book 1. Licensed bound guides may cross in winter, medicine always passes, and no one hangs for a death on a bound crossing.
- **the Shared Map:** the map of Book 2, co-signed by Ila and Kunal and given to both crown and council.
