## Keep

- **Chapter 1's hook and ending.** "Ila had never been wrong about a distance she had walked herself, so when the tower came up short she walked it again" is a first line I'd read on from. The ending laugh ("a bray that had been getting her sent out of dinner tables since she was eleven") followed by "Good night, Highness. It's forty" is where I decided to keep going. The void pays off in ch32 as Pritam's stair, which is the most satisfying clue in the book.
- **Ch2's turn.** Kunal realises on the page that she wanted him on the rope, and that his own slogan ("Nothing in Neshar should be hidden from Neshar") is the hook in his mouth. This is opposition rooted in values, which is what readers want.
- **Chemistry you can feel.** In ch16: "The laugh came out against his mouth and he felt it, and she felt him smile." His nails "had not gone one shade whiter" when he says "I'd show you anyway." The rule does the work, and nobody has to tell us they have chemistry.
- **The "I like you" callback in ch30.** She reverses her ch3 demonstration lie, with warm hands and a specific list (pencil leads, *forty-one*, making her laugh when she's furious). It's earned, and it's the line readers will quote.
- **Raghu caught by his own rope in ch30.** Pasang reads the tallow fingertips. The rule is used as a weapon by the people it binds, and it's fair because ch26 planted it.
- **Ch31's proof.** The gloves folded on the table, "My hands are warm. That tells you nothing," and "I'd rather break the oath I swore than the one I chose." She pays for the belief change with the commission. The new pace ("One and three-quarter spans") and the double scale bar (spans for the crown, ropes for the hills) are concrete and moving. Widow's Knuckle replacing Nimmi's Knuckle is a quiet, perfect beat.
- **Ch32's oath that doesn't hum.** "It didn't hum." / "I wanted it to." The walnut tied with his teeth. "Then I'll know when you're coming." The "we bind again, and my fingers can't show you a lie" setup is a strong series hook for this couple's world.

## Fix

1. **Ch30: Gauri is Dhanu's sister, not his wife.** Kunal says "my keeper's widow" and Ila says "His wife laid stones over him at the fold." The outline (ch7) makes Gauri his sister and Mini his daughter. Fix both lines, and check chs 26–28 and 31 for the same slip. Readers who loved Ewe's Rest will notice.

2. **Chs 16, 29, 31, 32: the account of Harish's death contradicts itself.** This is the emotional core of the book and readers can't tell what happened.
   - Ch16 has him "found at the thaw, sitting whole against a flat stone on the slope above the third bend," with his gloves on the stone.
   - Ch29 has a shelf of folded gloves inside the Under-Cairn, with his name last.
   - Ch31 has "He walked out again. He laid his gloves on a stone on the hillside," and Meera writes "went in... and came out."
   - Ch32 has "carried down at the thaw."

   Decide one sequence: did he go in, pay, walk out and die on the slope? Are his gloves on the shelf, on the hillside, or both? Then state it once, plainly, in ch29 (where she sees the shelf) and have Ila say it the same way to Rohan in ch31. If "he came out alive and died anyway" is the point, because it supports the hill reading of "carried", let Ila notice that out loud. It would strengthen ch32's two readings.

3. **Ch30 or ch32: Raghu's purpose is never answered (C10).** Ch5 asks "why does Raghu want it?" and the book never says. In ch30, Kunal's offer to Pasang ("I'll ask you where they're held") also opens a hostage-keeper thread that goes nowhere. Pay off both in ch30:
   - Pasang, once he sets the rope down, says whom Raghu was taking the way to and where the keepers are held.
   - Alternatively, the ch32 rider reports Raghu's loyalists' bells on the east tracks, which ties the hunt to Pritam's escape.

   One or two lines would do it.

4. **Ch31 end / ch32 opening: the cliffhanger is defused offstage.** Ch31 ends on "Whichever way she moved, she would be breaking faith with one of them." Ch32 resolves it in the past perfect ("'Not alone,' Ila had told the queen"). It reads as a cheat. Either:
   - end ch31 on her refusal, spoken live to the queen, or
   - open ch32 in the room and play the exchange in scene.

   Also make clear which promise to Kunal is at stake. Right now the dilemma reads as being only about Kiran.

5. **Ch16: the stretch between the letter and the first "Ila" runs long.** The "Endless monologue" risk peaks here. The weighing section after it (oath, crime or mercy, "Silence was free") restates the same point three times.
   - Cut both by about a third and keep the sharpest lines: "It was not neat. It was a receipt." / "His closed hand was a hand over a grave."
   - Cut the told summary "It was the most generous thing she had ever watched anyone do." The beat before it, setting down the mountain "as a man puts a sleeping child on a bed", already shows it.
   - Fix two lines that read as errors: "She had held it for an hour at nineteen fingers' depth, in a manner of speaking" and "one man's grandfather's grave-field". I couldn't parse either.

6. **Ch1: lore dump and book-1 baggage for a book a new reader might start with.**
   - Ila's speech beginning "Rohan wrote that the stones at the Shilak Cairn spoke at the thaw..." packs the Bargain, the fourth winter and the archive into one speech, and that is too much at once. Split it: give Kiran one line between the parts, and let his ear-rub carry the weight.
   - Varun, "his sister had tied a rope on Ila's brother", and Kiran's having "stopped looking like a man who expected to be arrested" each need a half-clause of context, or should be cut. The blurb says each book is its own couple's story, so the opening has to stand alone.

7. **Ch30, the pillow talk: "The tower, thirty-nine steps" and "At Ewe's Rest, for the lambing. The north wall needs checking" is muddled.** Why does Kunal sleep in his uncle's prison tower, and why is Garh Neshar's north wall at Ewe's Rest? Make it a clean plant instead. He jokes that she can come and check his crooked north wall in spring, she says "I'd want a new pace," and ch32's rider lands harder.

8. **Pattern, all chapters read: word tics.**
   - "Plain/plainly/the plain thing" appears in every chapter I read, often several times. Ch1 alone uses it for Ila, Kunal and the narration.
   - Fingertips "the colour of tallow" appears in ch16 and twice in ch30.
   - The "He did not X. He did Y." construction stacks up in emotional beats (ch16, ch30, ch31).

   Thin these in the line edit. Ila's habit of turning feelings into measurements is her voice, so keep it. But by ch30–31 ("I'll tell you the size of the hurt, in spans") it should land once per scene, not every few lines.

## Blurb

- **Factual error and spoiler: "hunting the same way for the escaped regent."** Pritam is imprisoned until ch32, and his escape is the book's final hinge. Change it to "for the imprisoned regent's loyalists" or "for the regent in his tower."
- **"Rivals to lovers" will be audited.** They aren't competing for anything. They're political opponents who disagree over values. Use "Sworn opponents to lovers" in the copy, and lead the tags with forced proximity and magical bond, which the book delivers fully. Don't swap in "enemies to lovers". He's kind to her from ch4, and readers will flag the label.
- **Darkness isn't signalled.** A beloved friend dies under interrogation and the heroine loses toes to frostbite. Add one clause, for example "and the mountain takes its price in flesh and friends," so grief-averse readers aren't blindsided and dark-leaning readers know it's for them.
- Heat ("open-door"), the HEA-for-this-couple promise, the 18+ label and the AI disclosure all match the book. The bold hook line is strong. Keep it.
