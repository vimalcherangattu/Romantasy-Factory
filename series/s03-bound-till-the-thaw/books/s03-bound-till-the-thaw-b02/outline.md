# The Winter Oath

**Series:** The Winter Passes, Book 2. Pen name Mira Sangla.

**Logline:** A crown surveyor who believes a map is only ever true or false is rope-bound for one closed winter to the rebel prince sworn to keep Neshar's hidden ways off every map. On the mountain every lie costs body heat, and every true answer he gives her becomes a line on the queen's map.

**Central question:** Will Ila map the hidden passes for the queen or hide them for Neshar?

**Answer:** Neither alone. She maps them truly, with Neshar's own names, co-signs the Shared Map with Kunal, and hands identical copies to the queen and the hill council at the same hour.

**Heroine's belief:** "A map is only true or false, never good or bad" becomes "A true map still says whose it is." The proof comes in chapter 31.

**Hero's change:** "Whoever knows the way owns the people" becomes giving his ways away before he knows what she will do with them (chapter 23), then naming them all on a map anyone can read (chapter 31).

**Love interest's own goal:** self-rule for Neshar at the spring council on Shilak's thaw day, and the safety of his route-keepers from Captain Raghu.

**Clock:** the pair are bound at the low Meadow Cairn below Garh Neshar the morning after the Snow Fair. Its meltwater runs about thirty-five days later, a month before Shilak's. The bond ends then wherever they are. If they are still high in the hidden ways when it ends, the line goes dark and the mountain takes them.

**Real losses that stay lost:** Dhanu, Kunal's oldest friend and route-keeper of the Glass Lakes, who dies under capture (ch 24, confirmed ch 26). Ila's two smallest left toes, and with them the exact pace she has used as her ruler all her life (ch 26).

**Explicit scenes:** chapter 20, after Kunal tells the ridge story he has never told and Ila says "I want you" with warm hands. Chapter 30, after Ila's verified "I love you" and her promise to give Neshar the same map as the queen.

**Regis's elements:** society defined (ch 1–2); meeting (ch 1, re-meeting after the drafting table); barrier (ch 2–5: her oath of crown exclusivity against his secrecy); attraction (ch 4–13); declaration (Kunal ch 29, Ila ch 30); point of ritual death (ch 25–27); recognition (ch 28); betrothal (ch 32, the winter oath sworn without a rope).

**Forms of address:** Ila calls him "Highness" with irony until chapter 20. He calls her "Surveyor".

## Chapter 1: The North Wall

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Finish measuring Garh Neshar's tower yard before the council sits on her winter licence tomorrow.
- **Value shift:** confident neutrality -> wary (the prince will fight her licence, and the regent's tower is lying about its size)
- **Beats:**
  - Hook: the eve of the Snow Fair, the 31st winter of Queen Sarala's reign. Ila (31, crown pass-surveyor) paces the yard under the tower where Regent Pritam is held. Her pace is exactly two and a half spans, the ruler she trusts more than brass. She finds the tower's inner room a full pace shorter than its outer wall allows on the north side.
  - She writes "north wall, two spans out of true, void?" in her field book. Kiran, her summer chain-man, jokes that it's where the regent keeps his conscience. When she mentions the cairn's warning, he goes quiet and touches his torn ear.
  - Society shown at a walk: the Winter Crossing Law nailed on the gate with two seals, licensed pairs bringing redbark down the Neshar road, hill-council guards on Pritam's grain stores, and the news that Varun went to Taravan in chains in summer.
  - Her want, shown in her sheets: the queen's commission, which came with the summer post, orders her to map every winter way. She has drawn the summer passes and named unnamed peaks after people she likes (Tamar's Shoulder, Kiran's Elbow). Without the winter ways she will never be Chief Surveyor.
  - Meeting: Prince Kunal (33), heir of Neshar and an open hill-council member since the Fair, finds her on the tower stair with her chain. They spar. He asks why the crown wants a prison measured, she corrects his count of the stair, and he answers every question with a question.
  - She tells him about the void. He says a drunk mason built Garh Neshar crooked and she can add it to her demands at council.
  - End: he tells her plainly that he will speak against her licence tomorrow. Ila laughs, loud and sudden, and it startles him more than an argument would.
- **Reader question out:** What is hidden in the north wall, and how will Kunal stop her crossing?
- **Techniques:** 1, 16, 11
- **Romance change:** From wary acquaintances who argued over a rope length at the drafting table to declared opponents. He will vote against her, and she laughs at him for it.
- **Clues planted:** C01, C12

## Chapter 2: The Council's Price

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Stop the hill council from sealing a licence that lets a crown surveyor map Neshar's hidden ways.
- **Value shift:** in control -> trapped by his own public principles
- **Beats:**
  - Snow Fair morning: roasting barley, drums under a white sky. Kunal (33) carries the council's petition for self-rule, which the queen will hear at the Shilak Cairn on Shilak's thaw day. He pays a route-keeper's runner in cut-corner silver.
  - In council he reads aloud the commission's outer sheet: "every way by which a person may cross between Neshar and Hemkot in winter, so that the crown may hold every way, and the way beneath the Shilak Cairn." The last clause stops him cold. Only his family knows the princes' way exists.
  - Old Bhim wants to refuse the licence. Chetu of Upper Kuhl wants to sell it for grain. Kunal has preached openness at this table for a year and cannot argue for secrecy aloud. Ila argues that a true map serves whoever reads it.
  - Dhanu's runner reports that Captain Raghu, the red-moustached patrol captain who fled the Fair with a dozen loyalists, has been in the high hamlets asking for "the princes' way".
  - Kunal proposes the condition: the council seals her licence only if a council member is bound to her for the survey. He names himself. Bhim: "So you can choose what she sees." Kunal: "Would you rather Chetu chose?"
  - Private face: after council he sits in the kitchen passage teaching a scullery girl the Shepherd's Stair route-song as a counting rhyme. He stops singing when Ila passes the door.
  - End: in front of the council Ila accepts. "Then you'll walk where I measure, Highness." He realises she wanted him on the rope.
- **Reader question out:** What does the crown know about the princes' way, and what will Kunal let her see?
- **Techniques:** 13, 15, 25
- **Romance change:** From opponents to forced partners. Each believes the other is a hostile court's instrument, and each has chosen the rope for that reason.
- **Clues planted:** C10

## Chapter 3: Names to the Stones

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Collect the sealed licence and bind before the snow sets hard.
- **Value shift:** certain of her task -> unsettled by a way the mountain lights that she can't explain
- **Beats:**
  - The clerk hands her the inner letter from the commission packet. It bears the royal archive's seal, with a thread of red in the wax like the Fair seal, and the queen's own hand: "Open when you stand at the Glass Lakes. Not before." Ila has never heard of the lakes. She doesn't open it.
  - Kiran asks to come. Pairs only, she says, and he is crossing home over the Saddle with a licensed pair. He asks her to send word if she finds where the Bargain is paid. She reads it as worry.
  - Kunal names the clock. The Meadow Cairn's meltwater runs about a month before Shilak's, and the bond ends there wherever they stand. Ila calculates thirty-five days in a soft year.
  - Dawn binding at the Meadow Cairn above the Fair meadow, with her eight-span rope of Lata's undyed wool. Names: "Ila, of the Shilak post's family, surveyor" and "Kunal of Neshar." Both say "the Shilak Cairn". She notices he says it a breath late.
  - To show she knows the rule, she tells him a lie on purpose: "I like you." Her fingertips whiten. He laughs, unguarded for the first time.
  - Dusk cairn-light: the winter line runs bright and west up the Neshar road toward the Long Saddle, and also faint and broken east into a valley she has never drawn. Kunal goes very still.
  - End: she asks which way. Kunal: "Which does your queen want?" He turns east.
- **Reader question out:** Why does the mountain still light a way no one walks?
- **Techniques:** 1, 22, 16
- **Romance change:** Her demonstration lie becomes their first shared joke and sets the terms. Each knows the other can't lie for free.
- **Clues planted:** C02, C11, C14, C15, C16

## Chapter 4: The Walnut Man

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Reach Ranu's terraces where the salt road begins and take her first fixed bearings.
- **Value shift:** contempt for a throne-playing prince -> confusion (he is the man who paid for Ranu's medicine)
- **Beats:**
  - Hook: Ranu a day down the Garh road. Nimmi's chalk tallies sit on the lintel by the old ash handprint, and Roop and Chand are unloading redbark at noon, legally. Ila has never seen the law working before.
  - Pinku, seven now, runs at Kunal shouting "Walnut man!" Kunal gives him a walnut tied with red thread. Ila connects it to Meera's story at the drafting table: the masked buyer, the three-and-tail cord on Nimmi's latch, the cut-corner silver.
  - On the terrace she confronts him. He paid for two winters of Ranu's medicine with silver from his uncle's own stores. He asks, "Would you have mapped the back road if you'd known?" She would have, and says so, and her hands stay warm.
  - Nimmi shows Ila the thirty-two graves. She refuses to name the grey-bearded officer who lowered the torches: "That's not mine to spend."
  - Kunal explains the walnut: route-keepers send true word with a walnut tied in red thread, and a message without one is not from his people.
  - Ila revises her picture of him. This is not his uncle's kind of prince. She draws the back road behind the terraces anyway, and he watches her draw it.
  - End: at the first faint cairn above the terraces he tells her the salt road is the last way he'll show her that every smuggler already knows. After that she earns them. Ila: "Price first."
- **Reader question out:** What will each hidden way cost her, and what will she do with them?
- **Techniques:** 2, 3, 25
- **Romance change:** She sees him gentle with a child and generous in secret, and her misjudgement cracks. He sees she will draw even a lifeline if it is true.
- **Clues planted:** C13, C18

## Chapter 5: The Burned Hut

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Get Ila up the salt road and onto the Shepherd's Stair before Raghu's pairs find it.
- **Value shift:** confident guide -> afraid for his people
- **Beats:**
  - The salt road climbs over the two spurs. At the Stair's foot the route-keeper Hari's hut is burned out and empty, with bootprints in the ash and a regent's brass shoulder-bell trodden into it.
  - Kunal reads the ground as a threat to his people. Ila reads it as numbers: two bound pairs, a day ahead, heavily loaded, going up. She is right about the count. He hadn't checked.
  - His interior: the ways are the hamlets' only defence against garrisons and his uncle's men. If she draws them and the crown holds them, Neshar stops being a valley and becomes a road.
  - She asks whether Raghu wants the ways to free Pritam. He says Raghu wants a way over the mountain for something, and refuses to say what he fears. Refusal is free. She writes down that he refused.
  - The Stair's lower ice steps are rotten. He leads and she belays. She corrects his count ("forty-one, not forty") and he lets her lead the next pitch.
  - End: at the Stair head a flat stone carries a route-keeper's chalk warning in old hill script. She asks him to read it. He does, aloud, before he can decide not to: "They ask for the princes' way."
- **Reader question out:** What is the princes' way, and why does Raghu want it?
- **Techniques:** 1, 6, 10
- **Romance change:** First competence duet. He lets her count and lead, and she sees him frightened for others rather than for a throne.

## Chapter 6: Whiteout on the Stair

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Cross the Stair plateau between faint cairns to Ewe's Rest before the cloud comes down.
- **Value shift:** sure of her instruments alone -> relying on him, and relied upon
- **Beats:**
  - Cloud drops. Between the old faint cairns the line goes dark. Her compass and her pace are set against his memory of the route-song.
  - He sings the song under his breath, and she realises its names are directions. "Where the Ewe Lay Down" means a shelter three hundred paces past a split boulder. Neshar names are routes.
  - She names a crag Nimmi's Knuckle. He tells her it already has a name and he won't say it, because a name is a way.
  - The song's boulder is buried, so he takes her bearing instead and she is right. Then a slab cracks under her and he holds her on the rope with a hip belay he learned from goats. Each saves the other.
  - They reach a faint cairn at dark. The stones hum and the light comes back.
  - He proposes the name trade. Each night he gives her one true Neshar name for a feature she has measured, and she answers one question about her orders truthfully or refuses for free.
  - End: the first trade. He gives "Old Woman's Tooth, water under it in spring." He asks, "Does your commission say garrisons?" She answers, "It says the crown may hold every way. I don't know what holding means to them." Her hands stay warm.
- **Reader question out:** How much will each name cost them both?
- **Techniques:** 6, 10, 16
- **Romance change:** The name trade begins. Their intimacy becomes an exchange of costly truths on a schedule.
- **Clues planted:** C03

## Chapter 7: Ewe's Rest

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Rest and resupply at Dhanu's hamlet and survey its fold.
- **Value shift:** outsider -> welcomed, and caught out
- **Beats:**
  - Ewe's Rest is three stone houses under a cliff, with goats and juniper smoke. Dhanu (34, Kunal's oldest friend, limping from a boyhood fall, route-keeper of the Glass Lakes way) lives here with his sister Gauri (38) and his daughter Mini (6).
  - Dhanu teases Kunal mercilessly in front of her. Ila sees a Kunal she hasn't met: laughing, cuffed round the ear, losing arguments.
  - Gauri wants to know if her new pen wall will hold. Ila measures it for her and finds the footing a hand short, and Gauri's wariness turns to trust.
  - Mini asks Ila to name a star, and Ila names it Mini. Kunal asks why someone so neutral names things after people she likes. She has no answer.
  - Dhanu says Kunal first walked these ways at nine "all alone, the first time." Kunal changes the subject too fast.
  - End: at night Dhanu tells Kunal that Raghu's pairs passed the upper fold two days ago, heading for the Hollow Moon.
- **Reader question out:** What happened to Kunal at nine, and how close is Raghu?
- **Techniques:** 1, 5, 23
- **Romance change:** She sees him loved by people who know him. He sees that her naming habit gives the lie to her neutrality.

## Chapter 8: A Cold Man's Whistle

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Decide with Dhanu whether to go on or turn back to guard the hamlets.
- **Value shift:** certain of his secrecy -> doubting it
- **Beats:**
  - By the chulha Dhanu argues for the bright line: take her over the Saddle and keep the ways. Kunal says the commission names the way beneath Shilak, and he must learn what the crown knows.
  - Dhanu shows his goat-horn whistle, the Glass Lakes way's signal. He jokes: "If they ever take me, I'll swallow the route, and they'll get my whistle off a cold man."
  - Kunal's own goal, spoken aloud to Dhanu: no garrisons in the hill villages, and a council seat at Taravan, won at the thaw.
  - Dhanu and Gauri will go ahead by the short way to stock the lake huts and watch for Raghu.
  - Kunal hears Ila teaching Mini to pace. Later she asks what Dhanu meant. He gives half: "My uncle taught me the ways."
  - End: at dawn on the cliff edge he starts, unprompted, "When I was nine my uncle—". Ila cuts across him. She has been reading the slopes as surveyors do, and she has caught a spyglass glint on the Hollow Moon. Someone is watching the hamlet.
- **Reader question out:** Who is watching, and what did Pritam do to him?
- **Techniques:** 2, 7, 15
- **Romance change:** He nearly gives her his oldest wound without calculating its use, which is the first sign he can.
- **Clues planted:** C04

## Chapter 9: Between the Cairns

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Get past the watchers on the Hollow Moon by the faint line at night.
- **Value shift:** competent alone -> competent together
- **Beats:**
  - A night crossing. Cairn-light shows only at the old cairns, with dark between. She steers by star bearings and paces, he by song. The watchers' fire burns on the bowl's west lip.
  - Competence duet on an avalanche slope: she reads its angle and loading, he knows where summer streams undercut the snow.
  - She corrects his distance aloud and he laughs without sound. Her pencil leads keep snapping in the cold (she mentioned it in chapter 6). He carries them inside his coat against his chest and hands them to her warm, one at a time.
  - Name trade: "Hollow Moon, the shepherds' summer way to Kuhl." His question: does she report to the queen alone? "To the queen and the archive. I don't know who reads the archive."
  - She must choose whether to draw the watchers' camp. It is true, so she draws it, and tells him.
  - End: at dawn the Hollow Moon's route-keeper cairn lies toppled, its old stones scratched with a bell, Raghu's mark.
- **Reader question out:** How far ahead are Raghu's men, and what are they digging for?
- **Techniques:** 6, 9, 10
- **Romance change:** His care is specific and she lets it land. She lets him carry something of hers.

## Chapter 10: The Princes' Way

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Learn from the toppled cairn how much Raghu knows, and keep Ila off the east fork.
- **Value shift:** guarded -> exposed
- **Beats:**
  - Raghu's men dug at the cairn's base, hunting a carved marker that isn't there. They are hunting the start of the princes' way, and it starts somewhere else.
  - Ila asks early: "What is the princes' way?" He answers truly in part. It is the way his family keeps, never walked in winter by family rule, and it begins at the Glass Lakes.
  - She asks why the rule exists. He tells the family story. His grandfather Prince Udai walked it once in winter as a young man with a guest, came down alone, and never went up again. No one says who the guest was.
  - Kunal realises he has named the lakes. He doesn't know her sealed letter names them too. The reader does.
  - The faint line forks in cloud. He leads her the long way round the bowl's lip, away from Raghu's tracks, and she notices he is choosing what she sees.
  - End: "You're walking me around something." "Yes." It is true. She writes "K. avoids NE fork" on her sheet and shows him.
- **Reader question out:** Who was Udai's guest, and now that she knows where the lakes are, will she open the letter?
- **Techniques:** 2, 13, 16
- **Romance change:** His first true answer about his family's secret, met by her open record of his evasion. She is honest even about his dishonesty.
- **Clues planted:** C06

## Chapter 11: Folded Gloves

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Survive a two-night storm in a snow cave on the Hollow Moon.
- **Value shift:** professional distance -> mutual disclosure, then a wound
- **Beats:**
  - The storm pins them. They dig a snow cave with axe and pot. There is no fire, only sattu wet with snow.
  - She tells him her grandfather, a Shilak warden, died sixty winters ago "checking cairns for a lost party." He was found at the thaw with his gloves folded one on the other on a flat stone, and no one in the family knows why.
  - He asks exactly how the gloves were folded: left on top? palms in? No one has ever asked. She feels understood.
  - Why she wants Chief Surveyor: her father copied other men's maps for thirty years and his name is on none of them.
  - He gives her half of nine. His father died, his uncle took him up a ridge, "and I came home." Then he stops.
  - They share body warmth, carefully. She says it isn't harbouring any more under the new law. He says it's worse, and they both laugh.
  - End: his name-trade question on the second night: "If you found a way that let my uncle's men into every hamlet, would you draw it?" "Yes." Her hands stay warm, and he turns his face to the snow.
- **Reader question out:** Can he trust a woman who would draw anything true?
- **Techniques:** 9, 10, 15
- **Romance change:** Their first real mutual disclosure, then her honesty wounds him. The values collision is now spoken.
- **Clues planted:** C07

## Chapter 12: The Widow's Ladder

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Climb down the Widow's Ladder into the lakes basin.
- **Value shift:** oath-bound -> first omission by choice
- **Beats:**
  - Kunal shows her the Widow's Ladder: ice steps cut under a frozen fall. It is the escape way the Ewe's Rest families use when soldiers come, and no one outside the hamlets knows it.
  - The descent. She counts steps on the hard pitch and he anchors.
  - The Ladder's route-song says "the widow was carried down," meaning guided across. Ila notes that in Hemkot's chronicles "carried" means carried off dead.
  - That night she inks her sheet and leaves the Ladder off. She tells him, "I didn't draw it." Her hands stay warm.
  - He asks her to promise she never will. She refuses: a broken promise strikes as a great lie, and she can't promise against her oath. She adds that it isn't properly measured yet. That is true, and an evasion, and he hears both.
  - End: he gives her a name without asking a question in return. The basin below is the Glass Lakes.
- **Reader question out:** What will she do when she stands at the lakes with that letter?
- **Techniques:** 11, 14, 15
- **Romance change:** She gives him something that costs her oath. He gives her a name for nothing, the first gift without a price.
- **Clues planted:** C09

## Chapter 13: The Ringing Stone

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Reach the lakes before the next storm front.
- **Value shift:** hope -> self-doubt
- **Beats:**
  - They race the storm along the basin. His desire is tied to watching her work, and above all to how she laughs when she proves him wrong.
  - By a lakeside cairn they nearly kiss. He starts to say "It doesn't matter to me what you draw," which is a lie. The cairn's hum climbs before he finishes. She hears it and he stops.
  - "Finish it," she says. He won't. He says the truth instead: "It matters more than anything."
  - His uncle's lesson, "whoever knows the way owns the people," sits in his mouth like a stone.
  - The storm front hits as they reach the first lake, the Mirror, frozen. She wants to sound the ice before crossing. There is no time.
  - End: halfway across, the ice booms under them like a struck drum. Ila stops dead, listening with her whole body: "Don't move."
- **Reader question out:** Will the ice hold them?
- **Techniques:** 7, 8, 22
- **Romance change:** The rule stops a false first kiss, and a true sentence takes its place.

## Chapter 14: Orders

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Get off the Mirror and decide whether to open the letter.
- **Value shift:** in control -> hurt by her own lie
- **Beats:**
  - She reads the boom. It runs ahead along a pressure line, and she steers them to the line's thick edge. They cross.
  - The upper lake hut is a stone hut Dhanu has stocked, with one chulha and one shelf.
  - These are the Glass Lakes. The letter sits in her pattu. She doesn't open it yet, because she wants to see where his way goes first.
  - Night question: "Do your orders name these lakes?" She is afraid he'll turn her back, so she says, "No."
  - It is a middling lie. Frostnip bites her fingers and ears, and the fire does nothing. She shakes and says she is only cold.
  - End: he sees her hands in the firelight, white to the second knuckle, and sets down the pot.
- **Reader question out:** Will he take her cold, and what will he learn?
- **Techniques:** 13, 14, 22
- **Romance change:** Her first lie to him breaks trust exactly where it had grown strongest.

## Chapter 15: Taking the Cold

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Keep her from freezing through the night.
- **Value shift:** suspicion -> chosen trust
- **Beats:**
  - He knows the loophole, as every hill child does. To take her cold is to know she lied.
  - He takes her hands. The cold floods into his, and he knows it was a middling lie about her orders, the only thing he asked.
  - He could keep his ignorance clean and let her freeze. He takes all of it, and shakes until dawn while she holds him.
  - At dawn she tells him the truth. Her orders name the lakes, and a letter must be opened here. He asks why she lied. "Because I was afraid you'd turn back, and I wanted to see where your way goes." Her hands stay warm.
  - She sounds the second tarn, the Drum, with an iron spike and his knife. At each boom line she counts thickness and marks where the ice holds a pair but won't hold three. The numbers steady her.
  - End: "Open it," he says. She breaks the seal.
- **Reader question out:** What does the queen want at the Glass Lakes?
- **Techniques:** 9, 14, 22
- **Romance change:** Trust becomes rational. He could have refused the cost and paid it knowing what it would tell him.
- **Clues planted:** C05

## Chapter 16: The Grave Map

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Read the letter and decide what it means for the survey and for him.
- **Value shift:** neutral surveyor -> granddaughter of the debt
- **Beats:**
  - Before she reads, she kisses him first, in thanks and in more than thanks. It is costed: she promises nothing. He says something true without weighing it: "I don't know what you'll do with any of it, and I'd show you anyway."
  - She reads the letter on the shore, inside the rope's span. Sixty winters ago the pass began to close, and the archive holds that the price is a life paid beneath the Shilak Cairn. Warden Harish of the Shilak post, her grandfather, was guided there by Prince Udai of Neshar by the way that begins at these lakes, and he did not come back. The Closing Law was written that spring so no one would walk that way in winter and ask what it cost. "Draw the way and read the price before the fourth winter. Tell no one Neshar's part until it is found." Signed Sarala.
  - Midpoint mirror. The folded gloves were a payment, not an accident. Udai's guest was her grandfather. Kunal's family secret is the grave, and her neutral map is a family grave map.
  - She weighs telling him. Her commission's archive oath binds her silence, and the truth accuses his grandfather of walking hers to his death. Under the rope, silence is free.
  - He asks what it says. She answers truthfully: "That I'm to find where your way goes and read what's written there. The rest I can't tell you yet." Her hands stay warm. He accepts it, and she watches what that costs him.
  - End: "Then I'll take you." She knows what his family did and he doesn't, and he has just offered to walk her where his grandfather walked hers.
- **Reader question out:** Will she tell him before they get there, and what is owed beneath the cairn?
- **Techniques:** 17, 3, 13
- **Romance change:** The first kiss, and then a reversal that recasts him as heir to the secret that killed her grandfather. His secrecy now protects the debt.

## Chapter 17: The Shape of a Silence

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Wait out the storm in the upper hut and win the rest of her trust.
- **Value shift:** closeness -> a wall he can see but not name
- **Beats:**
  - The storm holds them two days. Her new silence has a shape: she watches his hands and won't meet his eyes.
  - He thinks she regrets the kiss and offers her an exit: "If it was the cold talking—" "It wasn't." Her hands stay warm, so it's something else.
  - She asks carefully about Udai. He died when Kunal was four. Pritam told the story as a lesson, "the way kills those who share it," and made the family rule himself.
  - Name trade: "Where the Drum Answers: lie still till morning." His question: "Is my family in your letter?" She refuses, which is free. The reader knows the answer is yes.
  - He works on the petition and counts days: day twenty of thirty-five, with Shilak's thaw council after that.
  - End: as the storm breaks he tells her the princes' way enters a cleft at the head of the third lake, the Eye. He has never been past the first turn, because his uncle never took him further.
- **Reader question out:** What lies past the first turn, and what will he do when he learns what she knows?
- **Techniques:** 1, 13, 14
- **Romance change:** Closeness meets a silence he chooses to respect. His respect survives not knowing.

## Chapter 18: The Eye

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Survey the cleft mouth at the Eye before going down to the lower huts.
- **Value shift:** protected -> protecting
- **Beats:**
  - At the Eye's head the cleft is a black slot under blue ice, with meltwater ticking somewhere deep. Faint cairn-light glows inside. The mountain marks it.
  - She draws the lakes and the cleft mouth truly and tells him. "Draw it true," he says.
  - An ice lip collapses under her inside the mouth. He holds her on the rope with hands still blistered from her cold, and she climbs out on her own axe.
  - She dresses his hands with goat fat. She tells him Rohan sends her the post's numbers every summer "like love letters," and he laughs.
  - She imagines the queen's garrisons at the Eye, and for the first time a true line feels like a weapon in her hand.
  - End: from the cleft they see smoke at the lower lake huts. Dhanu and Gauri have come.
- **Reader question out:** What news does Dhanu bring of Raghu?
- **Techniques:** 6, 9, 15

## Chapter 19: Knucklebones

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Rest with Dhanu and Gauri and learn where Raghu is.
- **Value shift:** dread -> warmth -> threat
- **Beats:**
  - The lower lake huts. Gauri has dried apricots, and Dhanu teaches Ila knucklebones. She cheats with exact throws, and he is delighted and accuses her of surveying the stones.
  - Dhanu's report: Raghu has eight pairs in the Hollow Moon bowl. They took Hari's goats and still haven't found the lakes basin.
  - Ila tells him she named a star for Mini, and his eyes fill.
  - Teasing, Dhanu starts, "Ask him what his uncle did on the ridge when he was—" and Kunal cuts him off flat.

## Chapter 20: The Ridge

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Tell her the truth without calculating what it buys.
- **Value shift:** armoured -> known
- **Beats:**
  - On the shore, with the Drum booming, he tells her. At nine, a month after his father died, Pritam walked him up the Kuhl ridge in autumn and left him with a knife and no route, saying "whoever knows the way owns the people." He got home in two days by following goats, and he has kept every way in his head since, because what is in your head can't be taken. He is afraid that makes him his uncle.
  - He has never said it. He doesn't know what it buys him, and that is the point.
  - She answers him: "You found the way. He owned nothing." She tells him a man who funded Ranu's medicine in secret is not Pritam.
  - She says "I want you" and holds up her warm hands, then "Kunal," the first time without "Highness."
  - Explicit scene in the upper hut. Both are adults, consent is spoken, and the rope lies loose between them. She stops counting. He warms her cold feet in his hands, which plants her feet as the vulnerable place.
  - Afterwards he says he'll give her one name a night and ask nothing back.
  - End: he wakes before dawn to find her awake and watching him, with the letter's corner showing in her pattu.
- **Reader question out:** How long can she keep the letter from her lover?
- **Techniques:** 9, 10, 25
- **Romance change:** They become lovers on a fully met disclosure. His gift of names stops being a trade, and her silence turns heavy.

## Chapter 21: A Promise with a Date

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Survey the basin's exits against Raghu and ease her conscience.
- **Value shift:** keeper of a secret -> bound by a promise
- **Beats:**
  - Morning. Gauri reports two loyalist pairs on the basin's far shore, with tracks along the Eye's west side.
  - Ila draws the exits knowing every line could guide Raghu. She sews the sheets into her pattu's lining.
  - Kunal asks, "What did the letter say?" She refuses, which is free. His face. She can't bear it.
  - She promises: "When we're through the mountain, I'll tell you everything in it." The rope hums low and level. Now she must tell before the thaw or be struck as by a great lie.
  - The cost ledger: if they aren't through before the meadow runs, the promise and the line both run out together.
  - End: Dhanu asks her privately what she'll do with the map. "Draw it true." "True for who?" She has no answer.
- **Reader question out:** Will she keep the promise in time, and what will Kunal do when he hears?
- **Techniques:** 14, 15, 22
- **Romance change:** Her silence becomes a debt with a date, owed to him by the mountain's own law.

## Chapter 22: Walnuts

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Decide whether to give her the whole princes' way.
- **Value shift:** hoarder -> giver
- **Beats:**
  - At night over juniper, Dhanu repeats the walnut rule, "word from me without a walnut isn't from me," and gives Kunal two red-thread walnuts "for when you're stupid."
  - Dhanu argues against giving the way: the crown will hold it. Kunal answers that the queen's letter already knows it exists, so secrecy has already failed.
  - Kunal rehearses the petition: no garrisons in the hill villages, a council seat at Taravan. Dhanu warns that the council will ask what he gave the crown for it.
  - Dhanu tells him he has never given anyone anything he couldn't take back.
  - Private face: alone, Kunal ties red thread on a walnut for Mini, badly, three times over.
  - End: he tells Ila he will take her into the cleft tonight, the whole way he knows and past it.
- **Reader question out:** What will she do with the princes' way once she has it?
- **Techniques:** 3, 15, 25
- **Romance change:** He decides to give her everything before he knows her answer. This is the change he needed.

## Chapter 23: Seven Breaths

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Walk the princes' way past Kunal's first turn and fix it in her head.
- **Value shift:** taker of ways -> receiver of a gift she can't repay
- **Beats:**
  - Into the cleft by lamp: blue ice walls, the meltwater tick, the first turn. Then the "seven breaths" squeeze, which you cross holding your breath, and the meltwater step, while he sings the verse Pritam taught him.
  - Past his furthest point, faint cairn-light shows on stacked stones inside the mountain.
  - He gives her the whole way, every turn and count, before she says what she'll do with it. She asks why. "Because I want to know what you do with it more than I want to own it."
  - They stop where the way drops toward deeper water. It is too far to reach without stores, so they must come back.
  - By lamplight she draws the way and says, "I drew it. I don't know yet whose it is." It is true.
  - She almost tells him about the letter and stops. Her promise said "through the mountain."
  - End: they come out at dawn and the lower huts' smoke is gone.
- **Reader question out:** What has happened at the huts?
- **Techniques:** 3, 6, 14
- **Romance change:** His gift is whole and her answer is still unsaid. She owes him now in a way no trade can settle.

## Chapter 24: Proof of Life

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Find Dhanu and Gauri.
- **Value shift:** hope -> grief-driven resolve
- **Beats:**
  - The huts are empty, the door broken, blood on the snow, Gauri's apricots trodden in, and drag marks to the Drum's shore.
  - A message is pinned to the door with Dhanu's goat-horn whistle on its thong: "The surveyor's sheets, carried by the prince, to the Drum fold by moonrise tomorrow, or the two go under the ice." There is no walnut.
  - Kunal reads it as two living hostages and decides to trade the routes for Dhanu. His whole creed reverses in a breath for one friend.
  - Ila says aloud that there's no walnut. "They didn't give him time."
  - His interior: he has become his uncle's lesson. Whoever knows the way owns the people, and he will sell the way to buy one man.
  - End: he turns to her. "Where are the sheets?" She opens her mouth.
- **Reader question out:** Will she give them up?
- **Techniques:** 6, 7, 11
- **Romance change:** For the first time he asks her to hand him everything, and their two non-negotiables meet head on.

## Chapter 25: Burned

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Keep Kunal alive and the ways out of Raghu's hands.
- **Value shift:** truthful -> a liar who pays in flesh
- **Beats:**
  - Hook: the question still hangs. She answers with a truth first. The fold sits under a cornice ledge with sightlines from three sides; she measured it from the Eye. They'll take the sheets and kill him.
  - He says to draw them a false set. She won't. Raghu's men would test it on a hostage, and a false line is the one thing she will not make.
  - He says he'll go anyway, with the real sheets.
  - Choice under pressure. She lies: "I burned them last night in the hut fire. They're gone." It is a great lie, because it changes everything he will do.
  - The cold drops into her feet. She laces her boots tight and hides it while he searches the hut's ash and finds no proof either way.
  - He decides to go to the fold with nothing but himself to offer, and the rope means she goes too.
  - End: on the Drum's shore at dusk her feet stop answering and she falls on the ice.
- **Reader question out:** What will he do when he knows she lied?
- **Techniques:** 14, 18, 22
- **Romance change:** She chooses his life over his trust and pays for it in her body without telling him.

## Chapter 26: The Drum Fold

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Get Dhanu back.
- **Value shift:** hope -> loss
- **Beats:**
  - He unlaces her boots. Her toes are white to the instep. He takes the cold through his palms and knows it was a great lie about the sheets, though not what the truth is. Her silence since the letter and her oath tell him she has kept them for the queen.
  - "You'd let Dhanu die for your queen's map." She answers truthfully: "They're not for the queen. Or Raghu. And they're not for trading you." Her hands stay warm, which is no comfort to either of them.
  - Her two smallest left toes are already dead. The cold reached them before he did.
  - He carries her to the fold. Raghu waits, bound to the young soldier Pasang, with pairs on the ledge above. Dhanu lies on the ice, still. Gauri lies bound beside him.
  - Raghu demands the sheets and Kunal offers himself. Raghu tells Pasang loudly, "The limping one went home this morning," and his fingertips whiten. Ila sees it.
  - Ila calls the escape from his arms: "Eleven paces left of the boom line, then straight." He runs her across on her soundings. The pursuing pair goes through the thin ice. In the chaos he cuts Gauri loose and she runs for the huts.
  - End: they reach the Eye's cleft with no fire and no stores. Her feet are dead, his hands are blistering, and Dhanu's body lies behind them on the ice.
- **Reader question out:** Will they live through the night, and can he forgive her?
- **Techniques:** 6, 13, 19
- **Romance change:** She made the right choice alone, and it breaks them. Grief makes him blame her.
- **Clues planted:** C17

## Chapter 27: Past the Rope

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Survive the night in the cleft.
- **Value shift:** alive and loved -> nearly dead and alone
- **Beats:**
  - The cleft mouth has no fire. She can't feel her feet. Kunal sits silent, with raw hands.
  - He gets up and walks back toward the Drum, toward Raghu and Dhanu's body, past the rope's eight spans. The separation cold hits them both, and she feels the faint line dim.
  - She could pull him back with one long pull. She doesn't. She lets him choose and calls out the truth: "I lied to keep you alive. I'd do it again."
  - Near twice the rope's length he stops. Vengeance alone would kill them both. Her life is behind him.
  - He comes back. He doesn't forgive her. He lies down and holds her feet against his belly all night.
  - She thinks of her grandfather. She takes off her gloves and almost folds them one on the other on the flat stone beside her, then puts them back on.
  - End: near dawn Gauri appears at the cleft mouth with a pot of embers and says, "He was dead before they wrote it."
- **Reader question out:** Was Dhanu already dead, and what does that change?
- **Techniques:** 18, 20, 22
- **Romance change:** He chooses her over vengeance without forgiving her. Love is proven while trust is still broken.

## Chapter 28: Off a Cold Man

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Get Ila warm and learn what happened to Dhanu.
- **Value shift:** blame -> recognition
- **Beats:**
  - Gauri's embers and juniper. Dhanu refused to give a single name. Raghu's men held him in the Drum's open water to make him speak, and he died the first night, before the message was written. They took the whistle off his body. There was no walnut because there was no Dhanu.
  - Fair twist: the message was bait from the start and the fold was set to kill Kunal. Ila's lie cost her toes and saved his life. It didn't cost Dhanu his.
  - Kunal recognises what she did and how she did it: alone, because she couldn't trust him to listen. He also admits he wouldn't have listened.
  - Ila keeps her promise early. She tells him everything in the letter: Harish, Udai, the gloves, the Closing Law. She tells him the sheets are sewn into her pattu. Her hands stay warm throughout.
  - He says it before she can soften it: his family walked her grandfather to his death and buried it for sixty winters.
  - Repair: he admits he accused her, and she admits she lied rather than argue it out. Neither excuses the other. He asks if she still wants to go under the mountain. She does, to read what is owed.
  - End: Gauri will stay at the huts until the thaw. Kunal promises to send her the first spring pair, and the rope hums. They go back into the cleft.
- **Reader question out:** What is written beneath the cairn?
- **Techniques:** 3, 9, 12
- **Romance change:** Recognition and repair, each acknowledged and owned, with full knowledge between them at last.

## Chapter 29: The Under-Cairn

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Reach the hollow beneath the Shilak Cairn and read the price.
- **Value shift:** grief -> awe and dread
- **Beats:**
  - Past the drop, the lamp gutters and the meltwater tick sounds in total dark. Ila goes on a crutch Kunal cut, and he counts paces for her because her own are no longer true.
  - The Under-Cairn: a hollow under stacked stones, with faint cairn-light. The door frame is carved with three nested loops. She knows it from Meera's count sketch marked "Same as cairn."
  - The original carving shows two hands on one rope, one broad and one long and narrow. Ila measures the narrow hand's span. It is a woman's. Under the hands runs a line of old hill script.
  - Beneath it is a column of scratched marks. The last is a warden's goat and a name: Harish. Beside it a flat stone shelf holds rotted gloves folded one on another, pair after pair. Every payer folded his gloves.
  - Kunal can read the old script in part, since the route-songs use it, but not the worn last word. Ila takes a rubbing on her field paper.
  - In the dark Kunal says, "I love you." She can't see his hands. She takes them, and says it isn't to check, it's to hold them. "I'm not counting."
  - End: lamplight moves in the crawl behind them. Someone has followed them in.
- **Reader question out:** Who has followed them, and can they get out?
- **Techniques:** 1, 11, 20
- **Romance change:** He declares himself in the one place where the cold's proof can't be seen. She chooses belief over verification.
- **Clues planted:** C08, C19

## Chapter 30: Two Hands

- **POV:** Kunal
- **Goal:** Get Ila and the rubbing out alive past Raghu.
- **Value shift:** hunted -> free and declared
- **Beats:**
  - Raghu and Pasang, bound, come up the crawl. Raghu wants the sheets and the way out, and he has a knife.
  - Kunal turns the rope rule on him. He asks Raghu, in front of Pasang, whether the limping man went home. Raghu says yes, and his fingers go white again. Pasang sees it, and Ila tells him what she saw at the Drum.
  - Pasang was promised the route-keepers would be freed, and he refuses to move. Raghu can't go past the rope's length or cross alone. He is stuck, and Kunal disarms him.

  - In the salt house, warm for the first time in days, Ila says across the rope, "I like you." Her hands stay warm, the reverse of chapter 3. Then, "I love you." Then a promise: "I'll give Neshar the same map I give the queen." The rope hums level.
  - Explicit scene in the salt house's top room. Her feet are bandaged and he is careful of them. For the first time they speak of a life after the thaw.

## Chapter 31: The Shared Map

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Draw two identical true maps and hand them to the queen and the hill council at once.
- **Value shift:** neutral surveyor -> a surveyor who signs whose map it is
- **Beats:**
  - Day thirty-five. Kunal walks past the herders' fold, sixteen spans out, and feels no cold. The bond has ended at the Meadow Cairn. Nothing they say can be checked now.
  - Twelve days in the salt house top room making two copies. Kunal names every peak and way in Neshar's own words: Old Woman's Tooth, Where the Drum Answers. The Widow's Ladder goes on the map because Gauri sent word that the hamlets want it known. He gives her one nameless crag to name, and she names it Dhanu's Rest.
  - She re-measures her pace with two toes gone while Kunal walks beside her counting. She takes a new unit.
  - In the post room she tells Rohan about Harish, Udai and the gloves. He sits with Dilan's rope end in his hands. Meera writes Harish into the count as a person carried.
  - Shilak's thaw. The spring council meets at the Shilak Cairn. The queen (62) arrives by litter from Taravan, and the hill council's delegates cross the Long Saddle as licensed bound pairs guided by Meera's crew.
  - The proof. Ila breaks her surveyor's oath of crown exclusivity. She co-signs the Shared Map with Kunal under Neshar's names and hands identical copies to the queen and to Old Bhim at the same hour, in front of both. She keeps a promise the mountain no longer enforces.
  - Kunal's petition: self-rule for Neshar under the crown, no garrisons in the hill villages, a council seat at Taravan, and every way on the map. The queen grants it, angry at the broken oath and keeping the map.
  - End: the queen asks Ila, alone, for the rubbing.
- **Reader question out:** What does the carving under the mountain say?
- **Techniques:** 15, 20, 24
- **Romance change:** Unverified faith. They keep their word with no rope to prove it. He gives away his ways and she gives away her exclusivity.

## Chapter 32: The Winter Oath

- **POV:** Ila
- **Goal:** Swear their own oath and read what is owed.
- **Value shift:** answered -> opened
- **Beats:**
  - Dusk at the cairn. Ila and Kunal swear the winter oath with no rope: to keep no way from each other, and to walk every winter together. The stones neither hum nor ring. They choose it unproven. Kunal ties a red-thread walnut to her surveyor's chain.
  - The queen brings out the rubbing. Kunal reads the old script with Bindu, who knows it from crew knot-songs: "carried till no thaw."
  - Two readings. In Hemkot's sense the dead are carried off. In Neshar's sense people are carried across. The queen says the archive holds it as a death owed each time the pass calls. Ila says the hill sense out loud. No one can prove either.
  - The queen confirms that Harish was the last payer, and orders the Under-Cairn guarded until the fourth winter. Kiran stares at the rubbing and says nothing.
  - A rider comes over the Saddle from Garh Neshar. Pritam has escaped the tower by a stair inside the north wall, the void Ila measured, and his tracks run east toward a way no map shows.
  - Final image: Ila adds a dotted line to her own copy, "unknown way, Pritam." Kunal says, "Then we'll find it."
  - Teaser: at the salt house door Lata's wool convoy arrives early for the next winter, and Lata asks Meera who the grey-bearded man was that Ranu won't name.
- **Reader question out:** Who will pay the Bargain, and with what? Where has Pritam gone, and with whose army?
- **Techniques:** 3, 20, 26
- **Romance change:** Betrothal by an oath that no mountain checks. Their trust no longer needs proof.
