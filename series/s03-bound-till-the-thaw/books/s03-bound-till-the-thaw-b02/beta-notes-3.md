## Keep

- **Chapter 1's last beat.** Ila's laugh on the stair ("Highness, I thought you were built entirely of questions") is the moment I'd have bought the book. It's surprising, it's in character, and it gives Kunal his first unguarded reaction. The closing line, "It's forty," is a perfect exit.
- **Chapter 2's reveal.** "Kunal of Neshar" pencilled in on her licence before the vote flips the whole chapter. It's fair, it's set up, and it shows she's playing the same game he is. The kitchen counting song ("Nine and thirty, and the ghosts go by") is a lovely private face, and it pays off in ch 32 with "The ghost was the stair." That callback works hard.
- **The kiss in ch 16 works.** "It's thanks. Some of it isn't thanks." / "Which part is which?" Then, after the letter, "The thanks was the smaller part." That is chemistry built from wit and a costed truth, not narration. Kunal's "I don't know what you'll do with any of it... And I'd show you anyway" lands because the rule verifies it.
- **The midpoint is real.** "It was not neat. It was a receipt." It recasts the gloves, his family and her silence in one line, and it changes how the romance moves from there.
- **Ch 30's "I like you" callback.** She shows warm hands, then says "I love you" where he can see them. The explicit scene is specific to these two (his dead fingertips, her feet, "Then use your mouth, and I'll tell you the shape"), it's plainly adult and consensual, and it ends on a life after the thaw. It changes the relationship, as it should.
- **Ch 31's proof of change.** "I'd rather break the oath I swore than the one I chose." She hands over identical copies at the same moment and pays with the chair. This is exactly the changed-heroine payoff readers say they want. *Dhanu's Rest* earns its silence.
- **Ch 32's unverified oath.** "Nothing happened." Then Kunal ties the walnut with his teeth. It's quiet, costly, and the best HEA image in the book.

## Fix

1. **Ch 31–32, the surety timeline.** The rider says Pritam left "six nights ago". By my count the thaw is four days old and the queen arrived three days after it. That puts the escape *before* Kunal staked his person as surety. As written, the queen holds him to a clause for an event that had already happened. Readers will catch this, and it makes Sarala look either careless or unjust without the book noticing. Fix: either move the escape after the signing ("two nights ago"), or make the timing a deliberate clue. For example, Ila does the sum aloud and realises someone knew to open the door before the surety existed. That fits the unexplained key nicely.

2. **Ch 16, continuity and doubles.** Kunal says "I'd stake the fingers I have left". The book-check puts his loss in ch 21, so here it reads as an error. Change it to something like "both my grey hands". Two exchanges also appear twice in one chapter, and the second time each one tips into cute: "That's true." / "I know. I could feel it." and "I'd rather you were angry at me honestly." Keep the first of each, or change the wording of the second so it works as a turn rather than an echo. Cut the closing narrator line, "He took her silence for what it was and did not press it." The scene has already shown it.

3. **Ch 1, the measurement paragraph.** "Taken off the thickness, which the window cut gave her to the finger. Now the figure closed from both sides" is the one place I had to reread in the opening, and it's the hook's key fact. Say it plainly in two short sentences: outside length, inside length, two spans missing. In the same chapter, trim or ground the drive-by references (Varun in chains, Kiran "waiting to be arrested for a rope", the cairn that "spoke at the thaw"). A first-time reader gets five unexplained histories in four pages. Keep the cairn warning and Kiran's ear, and cut one or two of the others.

4. **Ch 32, the final image.** The book's last paragraph is Ila alone on a stair, thinking about Pritam climbing in the dark. That's a strong hinge, but it leaves the couple off the page at the moment romance-first readers close the book. Keep the Lata teaser, then bring Kunal down the stair with tea, or end on the walnut knocking against her rule as he comes in. One line of them together after the hook is enough.

5. **Pattern, chs 16 and 30–32: the hands and the dead fingertips.** The hands-as-truth-gauge is the book's best device, but by the last third we get "dead fingers / dead tips / dead hand / grey tips" several times per chapter. Every truthful line also gets a hand-colour report. Cut about half of these in chs 30–32. Readers know the rule by now, so save it for lines where the reading matters (Raghu, the 35th day). The "I'll tell you the size of it, in spans" joke also appears in ch 30 and again in ch 31. Use it once.

6. **Ch 30, Raghu's motive.** His "wives and children shut out under your new law" arrives as a speech mid-knife-fight, and it's the first time the villain becomes a person. Fantasy-first readers will feel it bolted on. Plant it earlier, for instance a line from Pasang or a family bundle found at the burned hut or the Drum fold. Then this scene can confirm it in one sentence instead of explaining it.

7. **Ch 31, the day count and geography.** The chapter opens on "the seventeenth day of the rope" and then ticks 22, 26, 28, 31, 35, 40, 56. That's fine, but the "east shoulder above the Meadow Cairn" visit on day 31 confused me. They're in the salt house at Kothi Shilak, and the Meadow Cairn was above the Fair meadow at Garh Neshar. Clarify where she walked and how, or move the east-bearing margin note to a scene at the drafting table. Ch 2's Chetu (round, pink, sweating, wants to sell it for grain) is the one stock-type character in what I read. Give him one specific, non-venal reason, such as Upper Kuhl's forty sacks being for a named family.

## Blurb

The blurb mostly promises the book I read: forced proximity, the magical bond, slow burn (kiss at ch 16, sex at ch 20), open-door heat and an HEA. Three adjustments:

- **"Rebel prince" and "leads the rebel hill council."** In the book the hill council is the legitimate local government, and Kunal is an open member petitioning the queen. "Rebel" promises a darker, outlaw hero than this principled politician. Use "the hill council's prince" or "the prince fighting for his valley's self-rule".
- **"Enemies-to-lovers."** They are political opponents who like each other by ch 4. Readers audit this label. "Rivals" or "opponents-to-lovers", alongside forced proximity and the magical bond, is more honest and still searchable.
- **"The jogini's rope-bond."** This is an unexplained Indic term in the middle paragraph. Either gloss it ("the mountain spirit's rope-bond") or drop the word from the blurb. The first two sentences already sell the rule beautifully.
