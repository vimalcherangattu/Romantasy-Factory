## Keep

- **The first line and Ila's voice.** "Ila had never been wrong about a distance she had walked herself, so when the tower came up short she walked it again." I'd keep reading on that sentence alone. The void in the north wall is a great planted clue, and it pays off in ch 32 with no signposting.
- **The ch 1 laugh.** "I thought you were built entirely of questions" turns his evasion into the thing she notices about him. It is banter that proves she reads him.
- **The ch 2 ending.** His name is already pencilled on her licence. It is a fair, satisfying reversal, and it reframes the whole council scene.
- **The ch 2 counting song.** "Nine and thirty… and the ghosts go by" ties the route-songs to the stair count, and ch 32 cashes it in with "The ghost was the stair." This is the best callback in the book.
- **Chemistry that works (ch 16).** "I could feel you decide not to argue. It's a different thing from being agreed with. I'm learning the difference." It is specific, it responds to her, and it shows him changing. "I don't know what you'll do with any of it… And I'd show you anyway" lands as hard as it should.
- **The ch 30 "I like you" reversal.** It calls back the ch 3 demonstration lie, and her hands are now warm. Readers will remember it. The sex scene changes the relationship: he has dead fingertips, she gives instructions "in spans", and they talk about life after the thaw. It stays specific to these two.
- **The ch 31 proof.** She gives up the Chief Surveyor chair, and she says "Not alone, Majesty." This is exactly the changed-belief payoff the market wants, a choice her ch 1 self could not make. Writing Dhanu's Rest and Widow's Knuckle over her own pet names is a small, devastating detail.
- **The ch 32 walnut.** He ties it with his teeth because his fingers can't pinch the thread. It is costly tenderness and a betrothal token. Do not trim it.

## Fix

1. **The hidden east-line omission undercuts the proof (ch 31, 32).** Ch 31 has Ila present the Shared Map as "every way" with her hands bare. Ch 32 then reveals she knowingly left the east bearing off ("The omission had sat in her fingers like a stone all through the signing"). That reads as a retcon. It also makes her proof moment a half-lie that the reader learns about afterwards. Change: put the choice on the page in ch 31. She reaches the east shoulder, sees only a cairn-light bearing she never walked, and makes a stated surveyor's rule out loud, "I don't draw a way I haven't walked." That keeps her honest. In ch 32 the dotted line then becomes a planned next step, not a confession. Also remove "She had left the bearing out of her field book," which contradicts her ch 2 creed "I won't leave out what is."

2. **The deadline dies at the end of ch 30.** Meera's button, "Day sixteen. Nineteen to your meadow," closes the climax chapter with nineteen calm days. Nothing happens in them, and ch 31 skips straight to day 35. The book check accepted the count, but as a reader I felt the clock deflate. Change: either restore the outline's "Day thirty-two. Your meadow's running soon," or give those weeks a job, such as the mapping starting at once with the bond ending mid-copy. The ch 31 timeline also has Ila keeping the Under-Cairn news from Rohan for about a month before the post-room scene. Tighten that gap or explain it.

3. **The opening leans too hard on book 1 (ch 1).** A new reader meets Rohan, Varun, the new law, the Shilak post, Kiran's sister and Ila's brother, the Old Bargain, the fourth winter, Pell Orlan and Tamar in about 1,500 words. Meanwhile the book's sales hook, that lies cost body heat on the rope, never appears in chapter 1. Change: cut the Varun exchange to one line. Replace it with one concrete beat of the rule at the gate, such as a licensed pair with a whitened fingertip after a fib to the clerk. The reader then knows the game before Kunal arrives on the stair.

4. **The ch 16 recap monologue.** After the letter, two passages re-list the clues: "She went through it as she would have gone through a field sheet, point by point…" (route-keepers, nine holes, the Mirror line), and then "Her neutral map was a family grave map. And neither of them knew it, except the one holding the letter." This is the "painful monologue" complaint. The narration announces the midpoint instead of letting the reader feel it. Change: keep the gloves-as-receipt paragraph, which is the best beat. Cut the clue list to one image, and drop the two summary lines. The reader has already done that arithmetic.

5. **The "carried" debate is a rerun (ch 32).** The two readings of "carried" are already explained in ch 12 and stated again plainly in ch 16 ("In the hamlets it meant seen across by the hand. In the post's chronicle it meant dead"). In ch 32 the queen and Ila argue both again at length, and the only new information is "thaw." Change: cut the ch 16 restatement so ch 32 keeps its charge. In ch 32, compress the queen and Ila exchange to about half. Let Bindu's "This is *thaw*," and Kiran's white ear carry the weight.

6. **The teaser is long and points at the weakest thread (ch 32).** The Lata scene spends many lines on wool prices and convoy logic ("I've waited twenty years to be early for something"). It then hangs the book on the grey-bearded officer, who appears for one beat in ch 4 and whom most readers won't remember. The real pull to book 2 is Pritam heading east before the fourth winter. Change: cut the convoy business to two or three lines. Either tie Lata's question to Pritam's escape, for example through the key someone gave him, or say plainly whose book comes next so the teaser sells a new couple.

7. **Smaller line notes (ch 30, 31).** In ch 30, "It was no hour's white. The truth would have cost Pasang his obedience, and the mountain billed him for that" muddies the great-lie rule exactly when the reader needs it clear. State it simply: the lie changes what Pasang will do, so it bites hard. In ch 31, the queen's "Thirty-one winters, and no one has said no to me in that voice" is a stock line. Give her something specific to the archive drawer instead. Also check how often Ila turns or watches her hands across chapters 16, 30, 31 and 32. The image is the book's key image, but it is close to being a tic.

8. **Raghu stays a flat villain (ch 30).** He lies, lunges and is led away. Pasang gets the moral turn, but Raghu never gets one legitimate value on the page. Change: give him one line about what Pritam promised the loyalists, such as grain, pay or a winter way for their own families. Fantasy-first readers will then read the east-way threat as politics, not just villainy.

## Blurb

- **Heat isn't promised.** The book has two explicit, open-door scenes (ch 20, ch 30), but the blurb's only signal is "slow burn." Add a plain spice cue in the trope line (e.g. "open door") so the pen name keeps its promise and closed-door readers aren't ambushed.
- **"A rebel force is hunting the princes' way" is wrong.** Raghu's men are the deposed regent's fugitive loyalists, and the logline already calls Kunal the "rebel prince." Change it to "the fallen regent's loyalists are hunting the princes' way."
- **"Rope-bound to a guide chosen by the hill council" undersells the best setup.** He names himself, and she had already pencilled him in. The next paragraph half-says this, so tighten both into one line: "He spoke against her licence, then volunteered for her rope, so he could choose what she sees. She'd already written his name in."
- **The Old Bargain stake over-promises.** "The pass will close forever unless the spirits' Old Bargain is paid" reads as this book's question, but it stays open until a later book. Either cut it or frame it as the shadow over the series. Readers who expect it resolved will dock a star.
- **The blurb runs long, at five paragraphs.** The first bold line is a strong hook. Cut the fourth and fifth paragraphs to two sentences, and keep the closing question.
- **"Enemies to lovers" is a slight stretch.** They are declared opponents for chapters 1 and 2, and her judgement turns by ch 4. Readers who audit labels may call it "rivals." "Rivals to lovers" or "opposing sides" is safer and still searchable.
- **Book 2 positioning.** The blurb says each book follows a new couple. Add "Can be read as a standalone" only if note 3 is fixed. Right now chapter 1 assumes book 1.
