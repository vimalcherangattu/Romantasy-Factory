## Keep

- **Chapter 1 hooks.** I'd keep reading. The spoken count, the scraped lids, the button in the spine and Kiran's "Nobody here will kiss me. I've decided it's the ear" all set up questions and personality in the first two pages. Rohan's entrance is right: "It is a goat, and it has not been charged with anything." "Please costs extra. You've already paid double" tells us who Meera is in eight words. I never reached a line where I'd stop.
- **Chapter 2 earns the dual POV.** The bale he probes and doesn't cut, the Puja fine ("Especially not mine"), and the redbark grain he pockets give the reader something on him before Meera has it. That is the dramatic irony the genre runs on.
- **The chemistry is felt.** The moment that works best is in ch16: "That," said Meera, "was free." / "I know," he said. "I watched your hands." It is banter that only these two, under this rule, could have. The kiss is costed and chosen ("I will not move. You will tell me which.") and the promise humming in the rope turns care into stakes.
- **The midpoint changes the romance.** The double confession with warm hands, then the shared complicity ("We fed the same hand. Your silver. My faith."), rereads every earlier chapter. It moves them from adversaries to complicit lovers who know exactly what divides them.
- **Ch31's amputation count is the best scene in the book.** The 206 faces, "Two hundred and seven," and "I only wanted to watch you hear it." It is disclosure, response and being understood, all in one go. The felted glove is the right small act of care.
- **The heroine changes on the page.** In ch30 she writes her own name into the crown's record, and in ch31 she takes the Closing Roll. Chapter-1 Meera could not have done either. Rohan changes too: he gives up the veto ("I gave away the thing I was proudest of"), and he names his signature on the blockade in public.
- **Ch32's ending flips the rule.** "You'll have to take my word." / "I do." is a real callback: belief with nothing to prove it. The swapped knots (his double hitch on him, Bindu's reef on her) are lovely, and so is the walk past eight spans and back.
- **The ch30 assembly has real opposition.** Pritam's grain gambit, Chetu half rising and the woman by the braziers stop it reading as a walkover.

## Fix

1. **Ch31 to ch32: the name-drop is spent before the betrothal.** Ch32's betrothal beat is Meera dropping "Captain" ("Rohan. Not Captain. Not from me"). But ch31 ends with "Come on, Rohan," and ch30 has her say "his, ten different ways." By the cairn the payoff has already happened. Keep "Captain" (or no name at all) in her mouth through ch30 and ch31, including the sex scene ("she said *Captain*, and then didn't finish it"), so that ch32 is the first time she says Rohan aloud.

2. **Ch3, ch31 and ch32: Badami's bell.** This is the continuity slip book-check flagged. Ch3 has Badami too old for the run, with Jassi carrying her kid bell. Ch31 and ch32 then call the big bell worn "through twelve winters of crossings." Change it to something like "the bell Badami wore through twelve winters, before her knees gave out like Jassi's." That keeps the joke and fixes the timeline.

3. **Ch20 to ch32 (pattern): the "not a promise" disclaimer.** It is meaningful in ch6 and ch16, but it recurs in nearly every late chapter: ch23 ("intent and not a promise"), ch30 ("It was not a promise"), ch31 ("That isn't a promise" / "I will not dress a hope up as a vow") and ch32 ("a guess, not a promise"). By ch32 it reads as a tic. Cut it from ch30 and ch32 and keep it in ch31, where it carries the Roll decision. A related tic is narration of his speech style ("He said it as he said everything, the full sentence, the exact number" in ch30, "He said it like the rest of his sentences, whole" in the same chapter). Keep one.

4. **Ch25, ch30 and ch31: Varun is a stock villain at the payoff.** He was at Rohan's wedding and stood beside him at Dilan's death. In ch30 he gets "said Varun pleasantly," a late laugh and a run, which hits the "strawman villains" complaint. Give him one true line about why. It could come in the ch25 lockhouse, or in a ch31 cellar visit Rohan chooses to make: debt, a lordship promised for his wife's family, or contempt for a law that never made Rohan rich. One scene of Rohan hearing it would make the betrayal cost what Jassi's death deserves.

5. **Ch32: the hinge has a long exposition tail.** The jogini speaking through palms is a strong, eerie hinge. After it, Rohan gives three paragraphs of grandfather's gloves, the queen's motive, Ila's map and the Roll-not-pardon recap. That flattens the moment, and it re-explains ch31. Keep the folded gloves, which are a great image. Cut or compress the queen-and-map speculation to one line, and drop the pardon recap. Make the final image concrete (the two unequal hands, or Kiran) rather than "a place she had never been."

6. **Ch16: the evidence summation runs long.** Rohan's "He touched his collar. 'And the Teeth...'" packs the fallback route, the seal, the hitch and who he told into one speech. Split it into a back-and-forth so the deduction feels like the competence duet it is. Also cut "I am the man who must hand you to the rope. You are the woman I must hand over. And I am sitting here warm." It is the one moment of chemistry that is stated rather than felt, because it repeats what both have just said with warm hands.

7. **Ch30 and ch1: line clarity.** In ch30, "She laid it open on the page. Under Rohan's smear of red she wrote" leaves it unclear whether she is writing in her count or in the herald's book. The belief-change proof needs it unmistakably to be the crown's record. Also in ch30, "Thirty-two graves... I counted thirty-one" confuses on first read. Make it "thirty-one when I came, and Sunni's makes thirty-two." In ch30, "Law's a rope. Depends who ties it" said "to the page" lands as a thesis statement. Let it come out as part of what she says to the meadow. In ch1, Jassi (40) calls Meera (29) "half my age." Also flag for the owner: goats named Sheriff, Magistrate and Judge read American-Western in Hemkot. A local word for official, or crown titles, would keep the joke.

8. **Ch21 to ch23: the sag the critic flagged (3.12 to 3.38).** I didn't read these, but the outline shows the risk. Ch21 ends on an interrupted reveal (Kiran bolts mid-sentence), which follows ch15's gust-interrupts-confession. Ch22 and ch23 then hold the Kiran secret for several chapters while Meera goes quiet. Make sure each chapter adds a new pressure, such as the ash marks, the last doses or Ila's danger, rather than recycling "she can't tell him." Replace the bolting with a reason Kiran can't speak that comes from the world: the crossing death hangs, and Rohan is a warden standing there.

## Blurb

- The blurb promises the book I read on tropes (enemies to lovers, forced proximity, magical bond, slow burn) and on the rule. "Every lie costs body heat, and a great lie costs flesh" and "Sharing one fire is a hanging crime" are the selling lines. Keep both.
- Fix the second paragraph: "the only way over the Long Saddle is a guide who knows the mountain." Meera *is* the guide, so this sentence makes it sound as if she needs one. Rewrite it so she is the guide and the jogini's rope is the cost: "In winter, the only way over is bound to another soul by the jogini, the spirits of the pass."
- "He never breaks the law without first measuring the cost, and that is what makes him dangerous to her" is soft. A reader can't see the danger. Try: "He has hanged two smugglers on that pass, and his writ names her as the third."
- "Open-door ending worth the cold" undersells and misplaces the heat. The first open-door scene is in ch20, well before the end. Say "slow burn, open door" plainly so the pen name's heat promise is clear.
- On darkness, the book includes the deaths of children (Sunni, the graves at Ranu) and an amputation. "Ranu is counting its graves" hints at this, but romance-first readers who expect cozier fare from the tagline may feel ambushed. Add a short content note in the front matter or the A+ content.
