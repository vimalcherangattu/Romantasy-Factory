I read chapters 1, 2, 16, 30, 31 and 32, plus the blurb and the book-check. I'd keep reading after chapter 1 without hesitation. "Nine. Ten. Eleven." is a hook with a personality in it. The scraped crate lid and the brass button give me two small mysteries in the first page and a half. Then Rohan's "It is a goat, and it has not been charged with anything" makes him dangerous and interesting in one line. The midpoint changes the romance: confession, then complicity, then a kiss, then a promise with a body cost. The ending answers the book's question and the jogini hinge makes me want book 2. It isn't a 4.5 yet because of the fixable things below.

## Keep

- **Meera's counting.** It works as voice, as coping, and as plot. It pays off hard in chapter 31, where she counts the 206 people she carried through the amputation and then adds "Two hundred and seven." That was the best moment in what I read. Don't touch it.
- **"Guide."** Rohan names the work, not the woman, in chapter 1 ("Then you will do. Guide."). Chapter 30 then gives us "no *Guide* in it any more." The title is tracked the whole way.
- **Chapter 2's quiet kindnesses.** The Puja fine scene ("Especially not mine") gives Meera a kindness she can't explain. The pocketed grain of redbark gives the reader a gap. And Varun's button is a beautiful bit of fair-play irony: "A man who cared that much about buttons did not cut corners with lives."
- **Chemistry that works (chapter 16).** "I suspected and I chose not to know. That is not the same as ignorance, but it is not far from it." Then the kiss: "He kissed like a man reading a document he had waited a long time to sign," followed by "That was free." / "I know. I watched your hands." This is shown chemistry: specific to these two and to the rule, and funny under pressure.
- **The "Watch my hands" callback (chapter 30).** A threat in chapter 4 becomes a declaration. Then her three remaining left fingers stay warm. It is earned and it isn't signposted.
- **The felted glove cut for a thumb and three fingers (chapter 31).** It is a small act of care that proves he noticed a specific need. So is Kiran in the room for the "no one hangs for a death on a bound crossing" clause.
- **Chapter 32's walk past the eight spans and back** ("Every step of it she chose"). So is the final turn: "Nothing's watching them now… You'll have to take my word." / "I do." Belief with nothing to prove it is exactly the right last beat for this rule.
- **The heroine changes.** Writing her own name into the crown's record is a choice the chapter 1 Meera couldn't make. It is proof on the page, not a reveal of hidden awesomeness.

## Fix

1. **Chapter 31: the rope length is wrong.** Ila says "A bound rope is not 'about twelve arm-spans'" and Rohan replies "The jogini refuse anything longer." Every other chapter, the blurb and chapter 32 say eight. Fantasy-first readers will catch this on page one of the drafting scene. Change it to eight. If the licence deliberately allows a different length, have someone say why.

2. **Chapter 31 answers chapter 32's question a chapter early.** Rohan says "I will not hand you to anyone" and the rope hums low and level. By the mountain's own rule, that is a binding promise. So when Gopal raises the writ at the cairn, the reader already knows the answer, and the scene has no suspense. It also breaks Rohan's pattern from chapters 6, 20 and 23, where he refuses to promise what he can't guarantee. Make it an intent he won't dress up as a promise. Something like "I have not found how to make the paper say no. I am looking." with no hum. Meera should notice he didn't promise. Then chapter 32's public answer becomes the payoff.

3. **Chapter 30: the age line reads like a compliance box.** "We're twenty-nine and thirty-five, and I want to be very clear that I am not asking leave of anybody" is the one line in these chapters that made me wince. Their ages are already on the page (chapter 1 "Twenty-nine," "about thirty-five"; chapter 20 per the outline), so the rule is met. Cut the ages from this line and keep the defiance: "I'm not asking leave of anybody." Also check that "since you climbed out of that gully swearing in verse" matches a moment actually on the page. If he swore in verse climbing out of the crevasse in chapter 8, say "crevasse," not "gully."

4. **Chapter 30: the belief change is stated before she proves it.** *"A law is a rope, she thought. It depends who ties it"* comes in italics just before she steps forward. That tells the reader the meaning before the act delivers it. Move the line after the confession, when the herald writes her name. Or give it to her aloud, to Rohan, on the wall afterwards. The act should land first and the words second.

5. **Chapter 32: the bell is confusing.** Jassi held a "kid bell" in chapter 3. That bell is now buried with Sunni, so this is Badami's big bell. Then Bindu says "She's retired. She doesn't need it. He did." Then Meera tells Jassi the goat is "under your roof, which is the roof of a man who's looking after her." I had to read that three times to work out who "her" and "a man" are. Give one clean line: whose bell this is, where the small one went, and who keeps Badami now. The moment matters too much to blur.

6. **Chapter 32: the hinge is overloaded.** The jogini voice, the unequal hands, Asha's knot, the cut rope end, the grandfather's gloves, Kiran's silence, Ila's map and the queen all arrive in about a thousand words.
   - The jogini speaking through the cold is the hook. Keep it whole.
   - "I know what your father said" reads like a missing line. Either let Rohan say the gloves detail aloud, or cut it.
   - Meera's fear for Kiran is vague ("cold with a fear no mountain had put there"). Make it one concrete thought, such as: a bargain made on a rope, and her brother survived a rope that was cut. Otherwise cut it.
   
   Two sharp threads pull harder into book 2 than five gestured ones.

7. **Chapter 16: the deduction runs long, and Rohan's formality piles up.** The Varun reveal is a long back-and-forth of evidence: the sieve ring, the button, the gully, the iron ring, Lalit's number, the fallback, the seal. Then it ends on the told line "It's Varun. It was always Varun." Trim to the three clinching facts: the button, "three wardens," and the fallback only he and Varun drew. Cut "It was always Varun" and let the two twists side by side on the stone carry the silence.
   
   Rohan's no-contraction legal cadence is a good signature. But here "It would appear so," "I do not say it for pardon," "That is no small thing to share" stack up until he sounds like a deposition. Vary at least two of them.

8. **Chapter 1: the withholding feels coy.** "Meera did not think about the rest of what she carried, and she was good at not thinking about it" hides from the reader something her own POV knows: she is the Salt Fox. That is the "contrived delay" failure mode, and it's probably why chapter 1 scores lowest of the opening (3.62). Give her one line in chapter 1 that owns the crew name and its price. For example, at the hanging tree: she knows which name those pegs are waiting for. Then chapter 16's "I am the Salt Fox" plays as a confession readers have dreaded for fifteen chapters, not a reveal they half-guessed and were kept from.

## Blurb

- **Tropes and heat match the book.** Enemies to lovers, forced proximity and the magical bond are all delivered. Slow burn is honest, with the first kiss at the midpoint. "Open-door romance with moderate heat" fits chapters 20 and 30 (chapter 30 is closer to soft open-door; that's fine at "moderate"). The closing question is the right one, and the book answers it.
- **It under-promises the darkness.** Children die on the page (thirty-one graves, then Sunni), and the heroine pays two fingers for a lie. A reader expecting cosy-adjacent forced proximity will be blindsided. That hurts reviews more than it helps sales. Swap "a village is coughing" for something with teeth, such as "a village is burying its children." Let one clause hint that lies here cost flesh, not just warmth.
- **Raise the stakes in the hook.** "He hunts the crew behind her" undersells the conflict. "He hunts the smuggling crew she secretly runs" makes the danger personal in one phrase. It doesn't spoil chapter 16, because the confession scene's power is in saying it to him, not in the reader learning it.
- **Paragraph two is a rules dump.** Five rules arrive back to back: eight spans, lie-cold, free silence, evidence, a shared fire. Keep three of them: the eight spans, the heat cost of a lie, and "silence is the only thing that stays free." Move "a shared fire is a hanging crime" into paragraph three as the tension line. It is the sexiest rule and it is buried.
