## Keep

- **Chapter 1 hooks me.** I'd keep reading. The spoken count is a character in one move: "a number spoken was a number kept." Kiran's P.S. ("Nobody here will kiss me. I've decided it's the ear.") sets up the ear clue with a joke I'll remember. Rohan's entrance sold me on him: "It is a goat, and it has not been charged with anything." The name load in ch1 is heavy (crew, goats, Puja, Lata, Nimmi), but every name gets a behaviour, so I didn't get lost.
- **Rohan's voice.** He uses no contractions, gives the exact number and says the full sentence. It's consistent from ch2 to ch32, and it makes "I love you" in ch30 land as a statement of fact rather than a swoon.
- **Chemistry that's felt, not described (ch16).** "'That,' said Meera, 'was free.' / 'I know,' he said. 'I watched your hands.'" The world rule, the banter and the desire all sit in one exchange. Then comes the promise and the rope's level hum, turning tenderness into a contract with a clock on it. This is the book's best idea working at full strength.
- **The midpoint works.** Both confessions are verified by warm hands, the shared complicity ("We fed the same hand. Your silver. My faith.") follows, and then the kiss is chosen with every reason not to stated aloud. It changes the romance and rereads the first half.
- **Ch31 is the emotional peak.** The amputation counted out to "Two hundred and seven." The felted glove "cut for a thumb and two fingers." His veto given away ("It was never a good thing to be proud of."). Her line "Don't be kind in front of the council." These are the moments readers will quote.
- **Ch32's walk past eight spans and back.** He stays where she left him and she chooses every step back. It's the betrothal shown as action. The rope ceremony and "Look at my hands. Nothing's watching them now… You'll have to take my word" pay off the whole bond. The hinge (the cold writing in every palm, the unequal carved hands, the folded gloves) makes me want book 2.
- **Real losses that stay lost.** Jassi, Sunni and the fingers are all lost on the page, and the fingers keep mattering through the endgame (the glove, "all eight that were still hers").

## Fix

1. **Ch2: the button clue is too loud, so the midpoint becomes predictable.** Ch1 describes the token as "a shank so stiff it had never been sewn to anything." Ch2 then gives Varun "one of brass. Cheap, bright, wrong, its shank still stiff," followed by "A man who cared that much about buttons did not cut corners with lives." That is three signals in a row plus dramatic-irony signposting. Most genre readers will name Varun as the middleman by the end of ch2, and the ch16 deduction becomes confirmation instead of a turn. Cut "its shank still stiff" and the irony sentence. Keep the brass button as one gripe among others (the lost pewter one, the quartermaster) so the detail is present and retrievable but not glowing.

2. **Ch2 ending: the last beat explains the question it should leave open.** The outline's out-question is "Why hasn't he searched the bales?" The paragraph "By the letter of the Closing Law… If he followed the medicine, it might lead him to the hand behind it" answers it, and "Then he went to find his lieutenant, and said nothing" lands flat after it. Cut the explanation and end on the pocketed powder and his silence. Readers will hold the question until ch4, which is the point.

3. **Ch31: bond-rule contradiction.** Rohan says "The jogini allow twelve… Eight is what one guide can hold on a slide." Everything else in the book, including the blurb's "neither can stray beyond eight arm-spans," treats eight as the spirits' limit, and ch1 says the rope is "no longer than the spirits would stand." Fantasy-first readers will catch this. Either make Ila's quarrel purely about whose arm sets the measure, or plant the twelve-versus-eight gap earlier as deliberate, a crew safety margin set inside the spirits' limit.

4. **Ch30: the climax says its theme aloud, and the antagonist folds too easily.** "I used to think the law was only ever a noose for people like us… It's a rope. It depends who ties it." / "Then we will tie it well." This reads as the book's thesis recited, and the reply is greeting-card. Her public self-entry in the herald's book already proves the change. Cut the first sentence. Keep a shorter, sharper version of the rope line, or move it into the act itself (as she lays the count down). Also, Pritam's one real threat (the grain) collapses in a few lines to a crowd chorus ("I paid… And another. And another."), which edges into strawman. Give his leverage one more beat of genuine danger, such as a council man who hesitates, before the meadow turns.

5. **Ch30 heat vs the blurb's promise, plus a continuity slip.** The blurb promises "two open-door scenes." The ch30 scene fades at "After that there was not much talk," so the door is open only literally. If taste.md sets the series at explicit, write this scene on the page at the same heat as ch20. The dead hand, his ribs, her taking the lead, and "held without counting" are already the right ingredients, and the scene changes the relationship, as the content rules require. If the intended heat is lower, change the blurb instead. Separately, her declaration "since you climbed out of that gully on my count" mixes up the ch3 gully and the ch7–8 snow-bridge haul. Make it "off that ledge."

6. **Ch32: plant the bell (C25).** The planted small bell was buried with Sunni in ch19, so "Badami's big bell" arrives unplanted. So does Meera's knowledge that Badami is "with your sister Leela, in the apricot orchard, forty trees." Add a short beat in ch31's return crossing or at Kothi before the cairn: Meera collects the big bell from the salt house, or sends Badami to the orchard. That lets the ch32 offering pay off something readers have seen.

7. **Ch30–32: turn the legal tail into the hinge.** "Held pending the queen's judgement," Gopal's "The queen may say otherwise," and ch2's unanswered "why… delivered and not hanged" currently read as a second open question beside the Old Bargain. Have Rohan connect them aloud in ch32's final exchange: the queen wanted the Fox delivered, she will want Ila's map, and she may want both for the same reason. That gives one series question with the romance closed, and it answers the reader who otherwise asks "so is she pardoned?"

8. **Ch16 opening, and a pattern across ch16, ch30 and ch32.** "For one stupid breath, Meera thought the rope tied her to the door" is a confusing first line for the midpoint after ch15's woodpile cliffhanger. Open on the action instead (the sticks skidding, the shout). Also, "Silence was free" (three times in ch16, again in ch30 and ch32) and the scene-closing beat where Meera lies awake eyeing the deliver-at-thaw threat (end of ch16, end of ch30) are starting to repeat. Keep the motif, but vary the phrasing. Change ch30's final beat so it doesn't mirror ch16's. Ch31 already sets up that tension well.

## Blurb

- It promises the book I read: enemies to lovers, forced proximity, a lie-cost bond, one fire, a law that hangs warmth, a slow burn with a costly first kiss, and an HFN. The darkness matches too (dead children, an amputation, a coup). The tropes line in bold does its job.
- **Heat:** "two open-door scenes" must match the page (see Fix 5). As the sample stands, ch30 is closer to fade-to-black, and spice readers will review that mismatch.
- Cut the last italic line ("Will Meera get the medicine through…"). It reads as internal metadata, not a hook, and it repeats the stakes paragraph.
- The paragraph about Meera is the weakest. "Ten winters of crossings in her ledger, a crew to keep off the gallows, and no use for the crown's law" is three clauses at the same pitch. Consider leading with the ledger as a danger ("a ledger that could hang every name in it"), since the count is the book's signature.
- The opening bold sentence is excellent and passes the five-second test. Keep it.
- "Every book is a complete love story with its own happy ending" is accurate for this couple. If book 2 follows the same couple, make sure that line stays true.
