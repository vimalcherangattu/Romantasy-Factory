# Bound till the Thaw

## First look

- **Title:** Bound till the Thaw
- **Alternatives:** The Winter Oath; Rope and Snow
- **Series:** The Winter Passes (4 books, one couple per book)
- **Hook line:** A smuggler must guide the warden sworn to hang her over a closed winter pass, but the mountain spirits bind them till the thaw, and every lie to him freezes her.
- **Tropes:** enemies to lovers, forced proximity, magical bond, bodyguard, one fire
- **Comps (shelf only):** Quicksilver; A Court of Thorns and Roses
- **Cover concept:** Two gloved hands bound wrist to wrist by a red wool rope, dusted in snow, against a jagged blue-white ridge.

## Leads

**Meera, 29, smuggler-guide of the high pass.**
- Want: to get fever medicine to the far valley and keep her crew off the gallows.
- Need: to rely on someone else's strength instead of carrying everyone alone.
- False belief: "The law is only ever a noose for people like us."

**Rohan, 35, captain of the queen's pass-wardens.**
- Want: to deliver the queen's seal and stop a regent's coup in the far valley.
- Need: to admit the law he enforces has killed people he could have saved.
- False belief: "If I bend once, nothing holds."

## World rule

1. **Can:** the high-pass spirits let rope-bound pairs cross a closed pass alive.
2. **Never:** cross alone in winter, or separate beyond a rope's length before the thaw.
3. **Cost:** each lie to your partner costs body heat in proportion to its size; a great lie kills.
4. **Who benefits:** pairs who trust each other, and the smugglers who learned it.
5. **Who's excluded:** the lone and the deceitful; armies can't cross.
6. **Lovers:** a declaration made while bound is tested against the cold.
7. **Dangerous intimate act:** confession and promise.
8. **Recurring dilemma:** tell him a truth that convicts her crew, or lie and lose a finger.
9. **Loophole:** the one lied to can take the cold onto their own skin by touch.
10. **Loophole cost:** he knows at once that she lied, and pays for her lie himself.

## Folklore and respect notes

- **Drawn from:** Himachal beliefs in jogini spirits of high meadows and passes, and the winter closing of passes that cuts valleys off.
- **Setting:** an invented kingdom on a Kinnaur-like trade route of salt and wool.
- **Invented:** the spirits' rules and their cairn shrine. No real goddess or shrine is named, and the spirits are not romantic characters.
- **Kept out:** Tibetan Buddhist practice, monasteries and prayer rites, which are a living religion. Real border politics are kept out too.
- **Non-English terms:** about six.

## Book-by-book

1. **Bound till the Thaw** (Meera and Rohan).
   - Question: will she get the medicine through, and will he arrest her?
   - Climax: she loses two fingers to one chosen lie, protecting her brother.
   - Ending: they write a winter-crossing law together. HEA.
   - Hinge: the spirits warn the pass will close forever unless someone pays their old bargain.
2. **The Winter Oath** (Rohan's sister Ila, 31, a pass-surveyor; a rebel prince of the far valley, 33).
   - Question: map the pass for the queen or hide it?
   - Ending: HEA.
3. **Salt and Snow** (a widowed wool trader, 42; the regent's exiled general, 45).
   - Question: second chances across a war.
   - Ending: HEA.
4. **The Last Crossing.**
   - Question: who pays the spirits' old bargain?
   - Ending: the series couples unite and the final couple pays with a promise, not a life. HEA.

## Why readers will click

Enemies roped together in snow is instant forced proximity with one fire and a slow burn. "Every lie freezes her" is a five-second twist that forces truthful-adversary banter. Survival stakes keep the pages turning.

## Risks

- Travel plots sag in the middle: plant a pursuit and a rival smuggler crew on their trail.
- The lie-cost must not make dialogue stilted: use silences and evasions, which are free.
- Book 4's "one-way crossing" theme must stay HEA, so the bargain is paid with a promise.
