## Keep

- **Chapter 1's first page.** I'd keep reading on the strength of the counting alone. "Two." on its own line, then the cloth "warm from the brazier... rewarmed in the night by a man who said nothing about it." That is chemistry shown through a small act of care, and it tells a book-2 reader the marriage is real before anyone says so. The langur silence at the end ("It did not come as a hush. It came as a hole.") is a specific, earned hook.
- **The ch 1 goals argument.** "I need one page." / "All of them." / "Then we go together. And argue after." Two right people disagree about values, not over a misunderstanding. This is exactly the values-collision couple the market is short of. Don't soften it.
- **The ch 16 midpoint.** Neela covers the leaf with her palm and slides it down one line at a time so she can't read ahead. That turns the honesty bargain into a physical act, and it makes "Lookout," she read. "Seventeen." land like a blade. Devan's "Say what you did" and her plain answer (counting to three hundred and ten) are the best exchange in the book. "I need to walk," and the cloak left on the peg: keep every beat.
- **Devan's withdrawal is disciplined, not cruel.** "I know," said evenly, "not forgiveness, and not the opposite." This is the right side of the content line, and readers will trust him for it.
- **The ch 31 scene changes the relationship.** The shutters are opened on purpose, the shared bite welt ("You bit yourself through me") turns the world rule into an intimate joke, and her eyes stay open for the first time. Then come the new terms and "It's a terrible word. I'll keep it." The breath-counting coda, with her counting his to know he's still there, is the moment readers will quote.
- **Kandan's salute in ch 30** ("It was not for her. She knew that, and was glad of it") pays off with restraint.
- **The ch 32 hinge.** "Sold to the crown," thirty stubs matched by finger, and Vellan walking in without resin. Kamalakshi's "I haven't forgiven you" over the whole-beaked bird is a real grace note. The recall vote proves she means "no one's master."

## Fix

1. **Ch 2–3, the escort clue is solved too early.** In ch 2 alone we get dry packs, unscuffed boots, numbers instead of names, a single smooth seam, "never once saw them sleep" and a sash Devan "could not catch rising." Add Neela's late breath at "Which wrist?" and the ch 5 nightbark, and any genre reader has the ch 32 reveal by chapter 3. Neela is the expert on odiyan scars, so her not connecting it for 30 chapters reads as irrational behaviour to extend the plot. Ch 32's "she had let him" admits it. Cut two tells from ch 2 (the unrising sash and the never-sleeping line are the most on the nose). Then give Neela an on-page reason to rule it out in ch 3 or ch 5, for example: "Odiyans can't hold a road by day in shape; no guild sells to the crown." The ch 32 reveal then overturns a belief instead of confirming a guess.

2. **Ch 1, ch 30 and ch 31, a repeated narrated-chemistry tell.** Ch 1: "It was the most honest refusal she had ever been given, and it pleased her more than a promise." Ch 30 ends: "It was the most honest thing anyone had ever said to her." Ch 30 also has "He never lied to her" twice, and ch 31 echoes it. Each one tells us what the dialogue has already shown. Cut the ch 1 sentence, or replace it with a physical reaction. Rewrite the ch 30 final line so the fear shows in what she does (counting the exits and finding none, say). Keep "He never lied to her" once, at most, in the whole book.

3. **Ch 31, the Janu story explains its own callback.** After "one night she walked in," Devan's paragraph decodes it: "It was his father's rule in an old woman's mouth... waiting all along to tell him the opposite." The style rules say not to signpost callbacks. Cut that paragraph down to the image and Neela's hand under the table, and let the open shutters in the bedroom scene make the point. The Raiu apology in the same chapter is thin ("He never said much"). Either give it one specific memory or cut it, since Raiu also appears in ch 30.

4. **Ch 32, Vellan's exposition.** His speech answers the clues one by one, like a checklist: "Lime-and-ochre paste... a palace surgeon pares the old ridges smooth, so a slipped cuff shows one seam." It is a monologue doing the critic's work. Split it. Let Neela work out the wrist seam herself, aloud, from what Devan told her in ch 2, and have Vellan only confirm it. Keep his lines about the green road and being bought at nine; that's character, not explanation.

5. **Ch 30 vs ch 32, an echoed line across characters.** Neela to the garrison: "I'd think less of you if you didn't." Vellan to Kamalakshi: "I'd think less of you if you had." Two different people using the author's phrasing makes them sound alike. Rewrite Vellan's line in his own grumble ("Good. Don't.").

6. **Ch 30, the timeline and the "no way out" line.** Rema says the writ was "ready before your wife's name reached her desk." But in ch 2 Rema already knew the closing writ named Neela, so it reached Kodiyur before she left. Neela's sum ("before the closing writ... could have reached Kodiyur") needs one line that squares this. The chapter then ends "she had never before stood in one that had no way out at all", and two chapters later forty-two people leave by goat track. Make it "no road she could take forty people down in the rains," or make it clear the hill route is the dangerous one.

7. **Ch 16, ch 31 and ch 32, the green quire's handling.** In ch 16 Devan insists "Rema can break it. The high court can. Not us." In ch 31 we're told after the fact that Rema refused to break it and left it with them. Their choice to break it in ch 32 is a reversal of Devan's own principle, and it deserves one line on the page, not just "No court was left to keep it for." Either show Rema's refusal in ch 29 or 30, or give Devan a beat in ch 32 where he names that he's breaking his own evidence rule and why.

8. **Ch 30 and ch 32, the speechifying and a muddled beat.** Neela's "Not behind. Beside." caps a strong speech with a slogan. End on "Wherever he walks out of this hall, I walk out beside him" and cut the coda. In ch 32, "It was not what they had agreed in the dark" confuses: his public pledge matches the ch 31 terms ("at your back"). Say what's new, which is that he binds himself to the forty's vote. Also, the critic scored ch 12 and ch 24 (the first two explicit scenes) at 3.25. Ch 24 carries the Eravi reveal, Muthu's apology, the declaration and a scene, all in one chapter. Check that the scene there isn't squeezed into interchangeability.

## Blurb

- **Mislabelled hero.** "A morally grey hero who gives his power away." Devan is the most principled man in the book. Fantasy-first readers will call it bait and romance-first readers will feel cheated of the edge. Use "a warden who could cage her with one word and swore he never will" in the tag line, or "a hero who gives his power away" without the label.
- **Wrong first line.** "The first one she reads aloud names her at his sister's murder." In ch 16 she reads the Released index first. Make it "The page he needs names her as the lookout at his sister's murder." That is also stronger.
- **Unglossed term.** "Forty frightened odiyans" is used without explanation. First-look needs "shapeshifters" in plain English. "Forty frightened shapeshifters" works, and keep "odiyan" once at most.
- **Missing darkness signal.** The book has forced turnings, caging, children sold at six, an on-page execution and a sister's murder. Add one darkness signal so the heat and darkness promise matches, e.g. "a guild that cages its own".
- **What works.** The heist, "marriage under strain," "open-door romance with real consequences" and the closing question all match the book. Keep the series line; this book leans on book 1 heavily from chapter 1.
