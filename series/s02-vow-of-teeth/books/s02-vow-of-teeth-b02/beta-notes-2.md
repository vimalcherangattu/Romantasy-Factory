## Keep

- **Ch 1 opening.** "Two." is a perfect first beat. It sets the clock, the counting habit and the stakes in one word. The brazier-warmed cloth and "Habit," with the pebble shows the marriage without explaining it. I'd keep reading on the first page. The langurs going quiet "as a hole" is a great chapter-out, and the honest goal clash ("I need one page." / "All of them.") gives the alliance a fault line from the start.
- **Chemistry that works.** In ch 31 he counts her breathing and finds she has a hitch on the ninth count. She says "Nobody told me." He says "Nobody was listening." This is disclosure met with understanding, done through action. It's the moment readers will quote.
- **Ch 2 Devan's eye.** He notices the dry packs, the barely scuffed boots and the goat path that "took the skin off his knees" at nineteen. His competence is shown through what he sees. "Before you ask" is a good turn for the romance.
- **Ch 16 reading device.** Her palm slides down one line at a time so she can't read ahead. That turns honesty into a physical act, and "Lookout. Seventeen." lands like a blow. Devan walking past his cloak into the rain is the right size of reaction: disciplined, not cruel. The midpoint changes the romance.
- **Ch 30.** His "No" comes before Rema finishes. He steps out so the hall's eyes leave her. Kandan, who called her a witch, salutes first. This is a public declaration with a real loss attached.
- **Ch 31.** Janu finally tells the ending of the leopard story, and Devan owns the lie he told in her kitchen. The bite that marks her own shoulder is playful and specific to their shared wounds. The explicit scene changes the relationship: eyes open, both names said, new terms made.
- **Ch 32.** Vellan's carved bird has a whole beak, a quiet callback to the broken-beaked parrot newel. Kamalakshi: "I haven't forgiven you." / "Good. Don't." Devan folds a cloth beside his sister's killer and says nothing. This is earned moral complexity. The thirty pages in Kodiyur are a clean series hinge.
- **Heroine change.** She does change. She confesses without excuse, reads the lookout line unasked, and keeps no page. This is the "changed heroine" readers say they want.

## Fix

1. **Ch 2 (and the pattern through ch 3 and ch 5): the escort twist is over-signalled.** In one chapter we get dry packs, unworn boots, numbers for names, ropes strung in the dark, guards who never sleep, a smooth wrist seam, and "Her hem had dried to the knee." The hem is close to a supernatural tell. Add the whining hounds and the nightbark and most genre readers will have the escort as odiyans by ch 5. That makes ch 32 a confirmation rather than a reveal. Cut the dried hem and one other tell (the never-sleeping or the ropes). Give Neela a reasonable counter-reading she says aloud early, and keep the dramatic irony deliberate.
2. **Ch 32: the reveal is over-explained.** Vellan walks in and delivers the night-guard, the paring by a palace surgeon, the throat paint, the goat-path teaching and the watcher's log in one block. Neela then gives a self-blaming speech ("I told Devan in the rain that no guild sells to the crown… There was no rule"), then a recap paragraph about "Sell to the crown when spent." Let Neela put it together herself from the sheet, the stubs and her memory of First Guard's wrist, and have Vellan confirm it in two lines. Cut her speech to two sentences. Use the space to pay off C04 on the page: one line remembering the hounds that wouldn't go near the escort's quarters.
3. **Ch 31: POV slip.** The chapter is in Devan's POV, but it says "Neela thought it held the queen's own contracts with the Resin House… Whether it was ever opened was theirs to choose." Rewrite it as Devan recalling what she said ("She'd guessed it held…"), or cut it. The quire's meaning is better saved for ch 32 anyway.
4. **Ch 30: Neela's logic at the end is unclear.** "If I go to the forty, I'm a master with a vouched house. The crown writes my name beside forty others." She gave every page back in ch 28, so readers will ask what she would be vouched by. Spell out the actual risk in one line (for example, being recorded as head of an unvouched band). Also tidy Rema's "thirty years a clerk" (she's a justice) and the "board tucked under her arm" that was never set down.
5. **Ch 31 and ch 32: the terms read like policy.** The afterglow negotiation ("No one leads. Or both do. If you say stop, we stop… We tell each other what we're doing before we do it.") and Devan's vote speech in ch 32 ("I've been a wall most of my life. I'm not one now.") are on-the-nose, close to the "cheesy dialogue" complaint. Keep the image of her at his back ("Someone who could strike and doesn't"), because it's the best line in either scene. Cut the rest to half and let a gesture carry it.
6. **Pattern across ch 16, 30, 31 and 32: repeated physical and voice tics.** These recur often enough to notice: "His jaw worked" (ch 30 Devan, ch 32 Vellan), "His face worked" (Muthu), and "His jaw moved" (ch 16). Devan's voice is described as "the way he gave the guard rota" (ch 30) and "the voice he used for the guard rota" (ch 32). "Flat," "evenly" and "very evenly" appear in nearly every Devan beat. Vary them or replace them with action.
7. **Continuity slips.** In ch 16 she was "called through that door twelve times, once before each contract of her own," but she has eleven contracts. Ch 32 says "She had read eighty-two pages aloud in a day," while ch 30 says forty pages. Reconcile both numbers. Ch 16 also has a recap paragraph (the kindi, "Find me in daylight") that stalls the scene right after the reveal. Cut it to one line.
8. **Ch 21 and ch 24: soft clue payoffs flagged by the book check.** C14 (the milled crown silver) and C18 (Eravi's wood-road ride) only pay off by inference. Add one on-page line for each. In ch 21, have Devan lay a death-gift coin beside the scouts' coin. In ch 24, have Eravi name the "tapper I know" ride as the night he carried the news. Fantasy-first readers will audit these.

## Blurb

- **The blurb promises a danger the book doesn't have.** "The man who could cage her with one spoken word has sworn never to say it" describes book 1. In this book her page is ash (ch 1, ch 30) and she is uncageable. Replace it with this book's real danger, the shared wounds or the lookout secret. For example: "Every blow she takes opens on his skin, and the first contract she reads aloud will name the part she played in his sister's death."
- **The hook line needs a gloss.** "the ledger that owns every odiyan" in the bold hook asks readers to know the word before they're told. Use "every shapeshifter" in the hook and keep "leopard-shifting assassin" in the first paragraph.
- **Chathu is an unexplained name.** "the master Chathu was" uses a name new readers don't know. Write "the guild master who owned her."
- **Heat, darkness and ending are accurate.** Open-door heat (three scenes), dark edges and a hopeful HFN all match the book. Consider adding "found family" to the trope line, because the forty and the vote are a big part of the ending's appeal.
