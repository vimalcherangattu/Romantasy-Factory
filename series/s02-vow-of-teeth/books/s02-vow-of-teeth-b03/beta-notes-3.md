## Keep

- **Ch 1, the vote at the hearth.** The opening line about the black thread breaking is a hook. Devan asking the Company, not Neela, for leave to fight is the best character beat in the opening. I would keep reading on the strength of one moment: Neela lowers her own half-raised hand so the Company can speak first. That one gesture sets up her whole arc. Chalking her name last pays off cleanly against ch 28.
- **Ch 2, the welt explained to a stranger.** "Nicked his chin shaving three days back and I found out over my rice." This is chemistry shown, not described: dry, specific and costly. Devan then sees his own body as a lever on hers. That is the book's romance engine in one paragraph.
- **Ch 16, the midpoint.** "You carried away paper. You can't carry away what I saw." / "You are the page." This is a real reversal of the first half. Neela sitting with the thorn and letting him wait is a hard, adult choice. The queen is morally grey in a way readers will argue about, which is the goal.
- **Ch 30, Madhavi's amendment to clause five.** This is a values disagreement settled on the page, with the dead named. The naming scene told through sound only ("Names only") is the most original device in the ending. Janu's "I'm not squinting at you in the dark" is earned comedy.
- **Ch 30, the declarations.** Devan says "never in front of her like a wall", and Neela steps out from his shoulder "so that everyone could see her do it". That is a public HEA beat that pays off three books.
- **Ch 31.** Neela stops the queen burning the children's rolls. Haran's objection gives the confession clause a real price. Neela holds her tongue so the confession stays the queen's own act, which is heroine growth shown as restraint. The explicit scene changes the bargain around his changed body. "Ask me... Do it aloud" works, and the nape memory is handled as harm she moves past, not as spice.
- **Ch 32.** Muthu beats Devan one-handed and Devan "laughed at the sky". The welt from the drill that Neela rubs with her thumb. "He was in none of it" is a clean, chilling hinge. Neela and Devan leaning opposite ways on the vote, in public, is the right last image for this couple.

## Fix

1. **Ch 31 → 32: the dropped cliffhanger.** Ch 31 ends on Kuttu's horn: "Climber on the pass road! One! Nobody behind him!" Devan calls it "its first vote". Ch 32 then opens "Weeks after the charter" and never mentions the climber. The envoy who arrives is three people, weeks later, and the first vote is the pepper contract. Readers will notice. Do one of two things. Make the ch 31 climber the scarred rogue scouting ahead, and open ch 32 by naming him as the man they turned away or watched. Or replace the ch 31 ending with a quieter beat, such as the hitch-counting dawn, and let ch 32's horn be the first stranger.

2. **Ch 16 (and 22): the plain-ink twist is telegraphed.** In ch 16 Neela pictures Chathu's "dry grey-brown" fingertips and nearly asks "How, with a reed and a pot of grey ink?" She then "folded the question small and put it away", and ch 22 repeats the smell clue. An attentive reader solves ch 29's reveal thirteen chapters early. This is the predictability complaint. In ch 16, cut her forming the question. Keep one sensory snag (the sheaf feels dry, or doesn't smell of tamarind) and move on. Let ch 22's lamp-black detail be the only second clue.

3. **Ch 29–31: the queen confesses three times.** She says "Yes" in ch 29. She gives a full itemised admission in ch 30 ("I let the raids run, and I bought the thirty, and I paid to have you killed"). Then she reads the same facts as charter clauses in ch 31. By ch 31 the Nellimala clause, which should be the book's emotional peak for Neela, is a recap. In ch 30, cut her answer to two lines: "I sealed it. I knew what it bought." Save the raids, the thirty and Nellimala for the charter reading, so Haran's objection and Neela hearing her village named in a hall land for the first time.

4. **Ch 1: name load and opaque callbacks.** About fifteen named people appear in the first scene, plus Kirri, Ittan and Muthu. Several are tagged only with book-2 references ("Madhavi, who had once closed her teeth on Devan's thigh", Meenu "pressed against the wall as she had once been pressed against another"). Returning readers will cope, but a KU reader starting here stalls. Hold Thulasi, Madhavi and Meenu until the vote paragraph, and give each a present-tense trait rather than a past-book reference. Also fix the birds. In ch 1 Vellan turns "a lump of pale wood" and also points "with the wooden bird", while Kamalakshi has "a small carved bird turning over in her lap". Ch 16 calls it "Vellan's bird", and ch 23 has Kamalakshi carving him one for the first time. Decide whose bird exists when, because ch 27's death beat depends on it.

5. **Ch 32: the epilogue sprawls and the romance thins.** The chapter runs drill, Muthu's round, the boat letter, Ponni, Janu's tiffin, the contract vote, Koman's river report and the envoy. That is eight vignettes, and only the drill welt and the doorway vote belong to Neela and Devan. Merge Ponni and the tiffin into one scene. Trim the contract debate. Use the room you gain for one private beat between the leads before the envoy. It should show the "vigilance stops" bargain from ch 31 in daily life, for example Neela not checking the door when he's at her back. Then the doorway disagreement lands as a couple who are safe enough to disagree.

6. **Ch 28–31: guard arithmetic and the Sajan/Sanu reveal.** The queen's held pages are four in ch 28, "the four it keeps, the eleven it claims" in ch 30, then three leaves in ch 31 after Lata names herself. Ch 30 has Sajan refuse "not tonight". Ch 31 then reveals, in passing, that Sanu is Sajan's younger brother and is the one Sajan names, while Sanu is already bound to Kandan from ch 24. Each of these can work, but none is clear on the page. Have Neela tally the remaining pages aloud in ch 30. Plant the brothers earlier, ideally in ch 16 when Sanu's page is read, so ch 31 is a payoff rather than a surprise. Also fix the two continuity points book-check flagged: Lata riding with the queen in ch 22, and fourteen hundred versus twelve hundred Thurai.

7. **Ch 30–32: repeated tics and one cheesy exchange.** Janu has "both fists in her apron" twice in ch 30, and it recurs in ch 32. Muthu looks at the floor or ceiling as his only reaction in ch 30 and 31. "The slow dry laugh he spent so rarely" (ch 31) is echoed as "the slow dry one she knew" (ch 32). Give each reaction once. In ch 31, "Then we've kept each other alive since the wedding, on a bad habit." / "It's a habit worth having." is the one exchange that tips into greeting-card territory. Cut Devan's reply and let her line end the beat, or have him correct her count instead.

8. **Ch 16: sharpen why Neela doesn't prick.** The queen already knows about the wound-share; Usha carried it down the hill in ch 2. "The queen would see exactly where the lever bit" therefore reads as a reason she doesn't have. Make the secret the signal itself: the noon code, the timing, and the fact that Devan is waiting on it right now. That gives her withholding a precise cost and makes "she had made sure there was no thumb to prick" sharper.

## Blurb

- **It calls the book "the finale of The Odiyan Contracts", but the book ends on a series hinge.** The Thurai envoy, the unledgered rogue and the river shape are explicitly left open. Readers who buy a finale and get a teaser will dock a star. Either say "the finale of the Odiyan Contracts, and the first job of the Night Company", or drop "finale".
- **"Has spent three books refusing to be" breaks the frame.** Say it in-world: "Devan could have been her cage. He has spent every day of their marriage refusing to be."
- **"The cure has a price" doesn't say what is being cured.** Name it: "The only way to free them is self-naming."
- **The bold question is the outline's central question word for word, and it reads like a spec.** It uses "odiyan" and "Varanad" without explanation. Try: "Can she free every knife in the queen's chest, when freedom means bleeding for someone in the middle of a war?"
- **The heat label is accurate.** "Open-door, moderate heat" matches the three consensual explicit scenes. "Dark edges" is honest. Consider adding that a beloved character dies, since grief-led darkness is a selling point here, not just a warning. Tropes visible: married couple, secret mission, magical bond, morally grey queen. Good. "Forced proximity" isn't really this book; it's right that the blurb doesn't claim it.
