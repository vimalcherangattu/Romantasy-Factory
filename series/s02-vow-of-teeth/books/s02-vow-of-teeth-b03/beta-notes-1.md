## Keep

- **Chapter 1 earns the read-on.** The opening line hooks: "Thilak tipped the last flask over the horn cup and watched the black thread break in the middle." The resin vote sets the book's politics in one scene. Kamalakshi's goat "has *written a letter*" gets a real laugh. Devan asking the Company, not Neela, for leave is the best character beat in the opening, and her hand going up last ("She felt every eye in the room go to it") pays it off. Ending on Usha's pared seam is a clean hook. I'd keep reading. The only place a newer reader might stall is the Kirri/Pachan paragraph, which leans on book 2, but it's short.
- **Chemistry that's felt, not described.** Chapter 2: "He cuts himself, I get the welt... That's all of it. It's also everything." This is disclosure in public, and Devan's interior reaction (his body is now a lever on hers) makes it costly. Chapter 31: "Neela. May I put my hand here." The heroine asks him to ask, and the scene changes the bargain around his dead hand. This is the explicit scene doing exactly what content rules want.
- **The midpoint works.** Chapter 16's fresh page in tacky ink with the fly stuck in it is the image readers will remember. So is "You carried away paper. You can't carry away what I saw." The rat-eaten page and Kamalakshi's question click into place fairly. The plain-ink clue that "slid away" is good fair-play misdirection. "The only freedom I have ever had is the one that bleeds when he does" is the belief cracking on the page, and the lever on Devan's office gives the romance its second-half engine.
- **Chapter 30's self-naming with backs turned.** "A witness's knowledge with the witnessing taken out" is a clever, world-specific act of intimacy. Janu and Kamalakshi ("You've a face like a wall already") is the found-family payoff of the series. Devan's declaration ("never in front of her like a wall") is specific and earned.
- **Losses stay lost.** Left-handed drills and losing to Muthu, laughing, is exactly the right HEA tone for a man who couldn't let a joke show in chapter 1.
- **Nirmala.** A legitimate antagonist with real virtues. She is never strawmanned.

## Fix

1. **Ch 16 (and wherever Sanu's intake is dated): make Sanu unambiguously adult.** "Sold at seven... The date, six years back" reads as a thirteen-year-old palace guard. He then self-names in a bonding rite in ch 24. That rite isn't romantic, but it is intimate, and readers will flinch. Change the date to sixteen or more years back, or state his age outright. Kill "young Sanu" phrasing if it recurs.

2. **Ch 30 vs ch 2 vs ch 21: the writ's location.** In ch 2 Devan carried the oilcloth up the hill in his pack "rather than leave it in the shutter frame." In ch 21 it's in the shutter hollow. In ch 30 he lifts it from "the hollow behind the third slat." Pick one trail and make it consistent.
   - In the same pass, fix the number drift. He made the drill at twenty-five and is thirty-two, which is seven years (ch 2). Ch 32 says "eight years of a drill," "eight years deciding everything alone," and "one in eight years," while ch 27 says six years with no one at his back.
   - Ch 32 also calls the drill "a slow open-handed pattern... that needed both hands." In ch 2 it's a one-handed two-finger strike. Rework the ch 32 line so the left-hand drill is new, built from the old strike.

3. **Ch 31 end / ch 32 open: cut the fake cliffhanger.** Ch 31 ends on a stranger climbing at grey. Ch 32 deflates it in one line ("turned out to be a boy... with wet sandals"). Readers feel cheated, and it steals the envoy's thunder. End ch 31 on "Then she helped him find his sleeve." with Kuttu's horn calling the first vote instead, or cut the stranger entirely.

4. **Ch 32 final scene: don't let Neela cast her vote at the door.** She thinks "You do not put the question that matters to a stranger at your door. You put it to your own people first," and then does exactly that, with a full speech to the envoy. That undercuts the vote culture the whole book built, and reads like pulling the rank Devan refused in ch 1.
   - Keep "How do you vote?" / "You first." That's the romance payoff.
   - Have her answer him alone, low, with her lean and her reason (Nellimala).
   - Then turn to the envoy with only: "We'll hear you at the table. Leave your staff at the door. We vote at noon."
   - Also replace the narrated "the face of a man who had spent eight years deciding everything alone, and had chosen... to wait for her" with something we see him do.

5. **Chs 29–31: the queen confesses three times.** She says "Yes" in ch 29, gives the full public confession in ch 30, then reads the permitted-crimes clause in ch 31. By the charter the clause has no charge left. Trim ch 30's speech to the new part only: "It is only blind" and "I only found out this week I could do it standing." The ledger-style list can live in the charter, where reading it aloud is the act.
   - In the same speech, fix "twenty-six of my own knives knelt... bleeding for strangers." They bled for people they chose. Say that; it's the point.

6. **Ch 31 dawn count: clarify "Seven."** "Seven breaths from the first of the grey. Yours catches at the ninth count" is confusing right when the beat should land clean. Readers also already know from ch 2 that he counts her. Make it one simple line ("You hitch at nine. I've counted it every morning since the wedding.") and let her surprise carry it.

7. **Ch 30: plant or cut Asha's "Say it anyway" scrap.** In these chapters it appears from nowhere as the chapter's title idea ("a scrap he found under a camphor block... kept it from Neela for weeks"). If it isn't planted in an earlier chapter, plant it by ch 21. Otherwise it's an unearned motive, and it quietly contradicts the book's no-secrets term between them.
   - Also cut "I know the hand from a letter shown at a border school." It's a strange, unsupported aside at the climax of the evidence.

8. **Chs 16, 30, 31, 32: small repeat patterns.**
   - Kamalakshi's one-word quips ("About time," "Finally") land in back-to-back chapters. Give her one, and make the other a gesture.
   - "Did not look away" / "her eyes did not change" recurs heavily in ch 16.
   - "She was fifty-four. Neela knew the number as she knew the sum of a lock's pins" doesn't make sense, since how would she know? Cut it or source it.
   - Add the one-line river-shape echo in ch 32 that book-check asked for (C13), so the second series thread isn't dropped.

## Blurb

- Mostly promises the book I read: married couple, wound-share bond, found family, a queen with a warm voice and a terrible offer, open-door moderate heat. "Safe, unseen, and mine" is the best line in it; keep it italic and alone.
- **"Dark romantasy" over-promises.** The book is tense and costly, but tender. The cruelty is institutional, the love interest is gentle, and the ending is a charter and a fishing boat. Dark-romance readers expecting an antihero will rate it down. Call it romantasy and sell the danger through the invasion and the bleeding bond instead.
- **The opening bold line is too long and abstract** for a thumbnail scroll. Try: "Sixty ships on the beach. Thirty knives caged by a queen's memory. And the only way to free them is to bleed."
- **Cut jargon a returning reader still has to decode.** "Speak her night-shape" can go, and so can "whose silence keeps her uncageable." Say instead that he's the one person who knows her secret and has never used it.
- **Lead the trope line with what readers search.** Use "magical bond," "protective hero who asks before he acts," and "found family" before "married couple." Keep "finale" and the read-order line; read-through is the point.
