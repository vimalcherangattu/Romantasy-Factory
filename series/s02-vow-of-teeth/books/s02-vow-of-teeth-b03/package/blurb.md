<p><b>Sixty ships. One pass. And a queen who keeps thirty knives in a chest.</b></p>
<p>Neela was sold to a warden to kill him. Now she is his wife, a leopard by night, and the first name on a company of free assassins. Her husband Devan has given up his fort, his office and the old habit of holding power alone. But the queen's hidden night-guard are still caged by paper, and Neela is the only one who knows how to cut the leash.</p>
<p>The cure has a price. Every guard who frees herself must bind to one chosen person, and every pair will feel each other's wounds for life. Neela and Devan already know that cost. Every blow he takes opens on her skin, and every fight he chooses is chosen for her body too.</p>
<p>Devan is the man who could have been her cage, and has spent three books refusing to be. Now the Thurai fleet has landed to stay. The queen offers Neela a bargain: command of a page-free night-guard, <i>safe, unseen and mine</i>, with Devan's fort handed back. The pass will not hold without the knives. The knives will not stay free if they must bleed for each other in a war.</p>
<p><b>Can Neela free every odiyan, even the queen's guard, if it leaves Varanad without its night-guard as the raiders land?</b></p>
<p><i>Crown of Night</i> is the finale of The Odiyan Contracts: a married-couple romantasy with a secret mission, a shared-wound magical bond and a morally grey queen. Open-door, moderate heat. Dark edges and a hard-won happy ever after. Best read after <i>Vow of Teeth</i> and <i>Oath of Claws</i>.</p>
