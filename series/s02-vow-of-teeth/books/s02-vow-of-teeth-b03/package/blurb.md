<p><b>Every knife she frees will bleed the one it loves.</b></p>
<p>Neela was a guild assassin who turns into a leopard at night. Now she has one goal left: free every odiyan in Varanad, including the thirty hidden knives of the queen's own night-guard. The queen has offered her a quiet alternative. Take command of a guard with no pages and no cages. <i>Safe, unseen, and mine.</i></p>
<p>Devan gave up his fort for Neela. He is also the one man who could cage her with a single spoken word, and since the night they bound themselves, every cut he takes opens on her skin. He holds a mountain pass without office, rank or a garrison he can command. She is learning to trust a husband who finally asks before he acts.</p>
<p>Sixty Thurai sails have anchored off the coast, and they have come to stay. The only way to give the queen's guard true freedom is to bind each one to a chosen person for life, which makes every freed knife a wound waiting to happen. Freedom may leave the realm with no night-guard just as the raiders climb the pass.</p>
<p><i>Crown of Night</i> is the closing book of <b>The Odiyan Contracts</b>. It follows an arranged marriage that became a true one, with a protective, morally grey hero, found family, war and a slow-earned happy ever after. Open-door romance, moderate heat.</p>
<p><b>Can they free every odiyan, even the queen's guard, if it leaves Varanad defenceless when the raiders land?</b></p>
