## Keep

- **Ch 1, the vote.** Devan asks the Company for leave to fight and never looks at Neela. He waits for the hands, and her hand goes up last. This is the book's chemistry in one scene: admiration for a choice, not for a body. I would keep reading on the strength of it. The reveal of the sixty sails, with Neela counting them and her body knowing the curve of Nellimala beach, is a strong first-chapter hook.
- **Ch 2, Neela to Usha:** "Nicked his chin shaving three days back and I found out over my rice." This is chemistry I can feel. The bond is told to a stranger as plain fact, and Devan's POV turns it into a lever the queen now holds.
- **Ch 16, the midpoint.** "You carried away paper. You can't carry away what I saw." Chathu's rat-eaten page and Kamalakshi's question both snap into place, so the twist is fair. Neela leaving her thumb unpricked so the queen can't see the lever is a real choice with a real cost. The reveal that the queen spent her whole noon on the crate is a great cold beat. The queen is not a strawman: she is kind and dangerous in the same breath.
- **Ch 30, Madhavi's objection to clause five** ("If the crown can hire us to hold a pass, it can hire us to die on one"). Devan amends the clause on the spot. The vote is a real negotiation, not a rubber stamp.
- **Ch 30, Janu's self-naming.** "Me? I'm sixty-one," followed by the complaint about the cairn-fire rice. This is the best found-family moment in the ending.
- **Ch 31, Haran's plea to strike the clause, and Neela closing her mouth on "Keep it."** This is the heroine changed: she won't make the queen's confession her own demand.
- **Ch 31, the explicit scene.** It earns its place. The hand at the nape of her neck, "the place for *wait*", becomes only his. The bargain is remade: she is his right hand, and he is the place her vigilance stops. This is the moment that works best in the book.
- **Ch 32, Muthu's left-handed round, and Ponni at the door board** ("That's the girl I carried round the bend"). These earn the HEA without sugar.
- **Reader-complaint check.** The heroine clearly changes a belief, from chalking her name last in ch 1 to writing it first in ch 28. The love interest's danger points outward. Cruelty is never a policy toward her. The book mostly avoids cheesy dialogue.

## Fix

1. **Ch 30 and ch 31: "That's all of them" isn't true.** In ch 30 Neela narrates Sajan stopping at the edge and not stepping out, then says "That's all of them." Under the book's own witness rule, the queen can still write Sajan again after ch 31. Change: in ch 30, make her line "That's all who'll come tonight." In ch 31, after Sajan burns his leaf, have him name himself to Sanu at the gate basket, with his brother's sleeve touching his. That closes the rule gap and pays off the brothers.

2. **Ch 2 vs ch 30: where is the payment writ?** In ch 2 Devan "had carried [it] up the hill rather than leave it in the hollow behind the third slat," and checks it in his pack. In ch 30 he "had lifted it out of the hollow behind the third slat at dawn." Fantasy-first readers will catch this. Change: in ch 21, show him put the oilcloth back in the hollow when they open it. Alternatively, change the ch 30 line to "it had ridden against his ribs since the hill."

3. **Ch 31 ending and ch 32: the cliffhanger deflates.** Ch 31 ends on "Lone climber! ... This is our first vote, Neela." Ch 32 then explains in one subordinate clause that the climber was a barefoot boy with a satchel. This reads as a false alarm, and it spends the "first vote" twice. Change: end ch 31 on the sleeve ("Ask." / "My sleeve. If you would.") and cut the horn. Let the pepper factors' letter arrive fresh in ch 32.

4. **Ch 2 close and ch 32 close: narration explains what the scene already showed.** In ch 2: "It was the truth. It was the only kind of answer he had ever given her, and she took it, as she always did." In ch 32: the paragraph starting "Neela understood why she had asked in the open..." walks through the diplomatic logic of the lean-vote. Both flatten strong endings, and ch 32 is the lowest-scoring chapter after the outline's own ch 3 and 5. Change: cut the ch 32 paragraph entirely and go straight from the murmur in the doorway to her nod. In ch 2, end on "I can't tell," / and her cold hand in his, with no summary. Also trim the ch 32 shutter callback ("Once, at the foot of a stair...") to the image alone, since the style guide says not to signpost callbacks.

5. **Ch 30: the queen dodges the writ.** Devan reads aloud the paper that paid for his death. Her answer is "I held that a realm is safe by what it has not seen. It is only blind." That is a thesis, not an admission. The reader wants her to say the thing to his face before the full record in ch 31. Change: give her one plain line before the thesis, such as "I sealed it. Seven weeks before your wedding. I knew what it bought." Let the hall react, and let the charter clause in ch 31 carry the rest.

6. **Pattern, ch 1, 2, 16, 30, 31 and 32: "flat" and "good hand".** Delivery is "flat" again and again: "His voice was flat", "her dry flat voice", "Her flat voice cracked", "said it flatly", "voice... was flat", plus a "flat green case". In ch 30 to 32, "his good hand / the good one" appears more than ten times. Change: keep "flat" for at most one character's signature register, and vary the hand with action instead ("his left", or just the gesture). Neela's counting is character and should stay, but cut one or two in ch 31, for example "counted the road four times" repeated in dialogue.

7. **Ch 16: small staging slips in the midpoint.** "He would be at the cistern shed now, or on a wall": Devan is at the shelf in the lower narrows. "You climbed in by the spray slot" contradicts ch 15's rope up the sea stair, unless ch 15 shows the slot. "The fading ache where a hook had not been" reads as a typo for the hook that was. Change: fix all three so the reveal chapter is clean.

8. **Ch 1 and ch 24: count and loose-page drift.** Ch 1 gives "Forty-two odiyans", "divided the cup into forty-two", "forty-one names" on the plank after she has added hers, and everyone still says "the forty". Change: make the plank forty-two, and add one line that "the forty" is the name they kept after the number stopped being true. Also show, in ch 28 or ch 31, the eleven Resin House pages Neela promised in ch 24 to hand back at first light being handed back. One sentence is enough.

## Blurb

- **"The closing book of The Odiyan Contracts" fights the ending.** Ch 32 opens a clear new question: a rogue odiyan in no ledger, and the sea lords hiring the Company. If more books are planned, call this "Book 3" and drop "closing". If it truly closes the series, add a line in the back matter pointing to a Night Company spinoff, so hinge readers aren't left feeling cheated.
- **Paragraph two leans on series knowledge.** "Pages and no cages" means nothing to a new browser. Try instead: "Take command of a night-guard the crown no longer owns on paper, and let the queen keep the only thing that matters: she watched each of them turn."
- **"The one man who could cage her with a single spoken word"** describes the earlier books. In this book his danger is that every wound he takes opens on her skin. Lead with that, because it is the hook.
- **Paragraph four explains the rule twice, and the bold closing question repeats the hook.** Replace the closing question with the queen's offer and its price: his fort back if she says yes.
- **The promise is mostly accurate.** It covers the arranged marriage turned real, the protective morally grey hero, found family, war, open door and moderate heat. That matches three explicit scenes that each change the bargain. Consider signalling the darkness honestly with something like "not everyone walks off the shelf," so that Vellan's death and Devan's lost hand don't blindside romance-first readers.
