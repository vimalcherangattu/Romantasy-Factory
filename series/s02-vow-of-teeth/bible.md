# The Odiyan Contracts: Series Bible

Pen name: Anya Kalloor

## Series overview

- **Series title:** The Odiyan Contracts
- **Books:** 1. Vow of Teeth. 2. Oath of Claws. 3. Crown of Night.
- **Structure:** one couple across the trilogy (Neela and Devan).
- **Primary engine:** rule. The witness rule (see World rules) drives every book. Court intrigue and border war are pressure, not the engine.
- **Subgenre:** romantasy (core), dark edges. Romance and fantasy carry equal weight.
- **Tropes on the tin:** arranged marriage, enemies to lovers, secret mission, morally grey love interest, slow burn. Supporting tropes: forced proximity (the monsoon closes the passes) and "touch her and die", with restraint built in.
- **Heat:** open door, moderate. Two to three explicit scenes per book. Each one changes the bargain between them. All participants are adults, stated on the page before any intimate beat.
- **Darkness:** dark edges, not grimdark. The series includes contract killing, a guild that owns its members, and caged odiyans whose minds fade. Neela's guild-sister Ammu dies in book 1 and stays dead. There is no sexual violence on or off the page. Devan's danger points outward. When he holds power over Neela, the story treats it as harm he names and gives up.
- **Ending promise:**
  - Book 1: HFN. Married in truth.
  - Book 2: HFN. Together, homeless and hunted, with forty freed odiyans.
  - Book 3: HEA. The guild is remade as a sworn company that owns itself. Neela and Devan stay together and lead it.
- **POV plan:** dual close third person, past tense. Neela holds about 65% of chapters and Devan about 35%. There is one POV per scene, and every switch is marked with a scene break. Devan's POV supplies the dramatic irony: the reader knows from chapter 2 that he saw her turn.
- **Setting:** the invented kingdom of Varanad. It is built on Malabar hill country and its pepper coast: laterite forts, pepper vines, kalari halls, and monsoon-closed passes. No real places are named as fantasy kingdoms, and there are no deities. Odiyan lore is adapted as follows:
  - An invented bark resin replaces the folklore's preparation.
  - The guild recruits orphans of any birth.
  - Caste is not coded onto the guild or onto anyone.
- **Non-English terms:** at most about 10 per book, each clear from context on first use and not italicised. The core set is odiyan, kalari, marma, mundu, kasavu, chechi, kindi and padippura.

## World rules

Rules are never suspended. If a scene needs a rule bent, the scene changes, not the rule.

### Rule 1: The Turning

- **What people can do:** an odiyan who smears nightbark resin on the skin of the throat and wrists after sunset turns into one animal within about a minute. She keeps her human mind and memory. Her senses and hungers belong to the animal. She turns back at first light, whether she wants to or not.
- **What they can never do:**
  - Choose the shape. It is fixed at the first turning and never changes.
  - Turn by day.
  - Turn back before dawn.
  - Speak while in shape.
- **Cost:**
  - The resin burns. Every odiyan carries pale ridged scars at the throat and inner wrists.
  - The morning after a turning brings a hollow, shaking hunger.
  - An odiyan who goes a full moon without resin gets the ache: fever, night-sweats, and nails that split in sleep. The ache is never fatal, but it cripples.
- **Who benefits:** the Resin House. It owns the only groves of nightbark in the Blackbark Wood and rations the resin to members. Supply is the guild's second leash.
- **Who is excluded:**
  - Anyone without a master. Rogue odiyans must steal resin or buy it cut with lamp oil, which turns badly.
  - Non-odiyans. The resin does nothing to them except burn.
- **What happens to lovers:** if she takes resin she cannot stay the night in human form. On any night she wants to be a woman in his bed, she skips the resin, and the ache counts that night against her.
- **Which intimate act becomes dangerous:** staying in a lover's room past sunset or before first light on a resin night. That is the turning, and it can be watched.
- **Recurring dilemma:** take the resin and do the guild's work, or skip it and stay human beside someone.
- **Loophole:** the resin can be forced. Smeared on an unwilling odiyan, it still turns her at dusk. This is how the guild makes a disobedient member cageable.
- **Loophole's cost:** a forced turning tears. The odiyan bleeds from the resin scars and is weak for days. It is a loophole only for the guild, and it is a cruelty the story names as such.

### Rule 2: The Witness Word

- **What people can do:** anyone who has seen an odiyan's turning (dusk or dawn) with their own eyes becomes her witness. A witness who speaks her night-shape aloud, in her hearing, while she is in shape and before dawn, cages her.
- **What they can never do:**
  - Undo a caging. A caged odiyan stays an animal for life. Her human mind fades over about a year.
  - Cage by naming the animal without having seen a turning. A non-witness can shout "leopard" all night and nothing happens.
  - Cage by day.
- **Cost:**
  - Intent does not matter. A witness who says the word by accident, in a fever or a joke, cages her just the same.
  - Every witness carries a live weapon in their mouth after dark, and every odiyan knows exactly who her witnesses are.
- **Who benefits:**
  - The guild masters, who witness every first turning.
  - Anyone who learns an odiyan's secret by watching.
- **Who is excluded:** odiyans, who cannot refuse to be witnessed once seen.
- **What happens to lovers:** a lover who watches her turn becomes a witness for life. After dark, she depends on his silence every night they share a roof. He must never let the word slip in anger, sleep or drink.
- **Which intimate act becomes dangerous:** letting someone watch you change, sleeping beside them through dusk, and talking in the dark.
- **Recurring dilemma:**
  - Hide, and keep lying to the people she loves.
  - Or be seen, and hand them the key to a cage.
- **Loophole: self-naming.** An odiyan speaks her own night-shape aloud, in human voice, during the minute of a dusk turning, to one chosen person who is looking at her. That act does three things:
  - It makes the witness bond mutual with that one person.
  - It voids every other witness: masters, sisters and strangers.
  - From then on, a caging needs both voices together. Hers is one of them, so in practice no one can cage her without her consent.
- **Loophole's cost:**
  - The pair feel each other's wounds for life. A cut on one opens as a shallow welt on the other, and a mortal wound drags the other toward death.
  - Her witness entry in the guild ledger burns where it lies. The guild then knows she has self-named, she is marked rogue, and she is hunted.
  - She can self-name only once, to one person, ever.
  - The guild has hidden that this loophole exists. Neela learns it from Ammu in book 1.

### Rule 3: The Ledger

- **What people can do:** a witness can write a sworn entry in resin-ink. The entry records the odiyan's ledger number and night-shape and is signed by the witness. Whoever holds that page and reads the entry aloud at night, in the odiyan's hearing, has a witness's power to cage. The power transfers with the page.
- **What they can never do:**
  - Forge an entry. Only a true witness's hand takes in resin-ink.
  - Read an entry to cage someone by day or out of her hearing.
- **Cost:**
  - A page is a life. Whoever holds it is a master, whether they want to be or not.
  - Burning another odiyan's page frees her from it, but also marks her rogue, because the guild cannot vouch for her and the crown hunts unvouched odiyans.
- **Who benefits:**
  - Master Chathu of the Resin House, who holds every member's page.
  - The crown, which holds a separate "crown leaf" of pages for odiyans sold into its night-guard (revealed at the end of book 2).
- **Who is excluded:**
  - Every member. No odiyan has ever held her own page.
  - Rogues, who are hunted because no page vouches for them.
- **What happens to lovers:** an odiyan cannot promise a lover safety while anyone else holds her page.
- **Which intimate act becomes dangerous:** writing. Keeping a record of someone you love, even a tender one, can become a leash.
- **Recurring dilemma:**
  - Hold a page and protect someone by owning them.
  - Or give it up and lose the power to protect.
- **Loophole:** a page handed to its own subject, and kept by her, cannot be read against her by anyone else. She holds her own leash.
- **Loophole's cost:**
  - The handover has to be read aloud in full by the current holder, by daylight, before the subject. Everyone present hears the night-shape, so the odiyan gains new living witnesses.
  - A subject who burns her own page also burns the intake roll fastened to it: her birth name and birth village, gone for good.

### Non-magic texture (no rule, but fixed)

- **Kalari:** the fighting art, taught in earthen pits.
- **Marma:** strikes to vital points that can heal or kill. Marma is skill, not magic. A marma strike can still kill in human form.
- **Monsoon:** the rains close the Monkey Pass and the hill roads for about four months.

## Places

- **Kettamala, the hill fort.** Laterite walls above the Monkey Pass, with pepper vines on the inner terraces and a carved west stair whose newel post is a parrot with a broken beak. Sensory signature: the clank of the iron cistern chain at every hour and the smell of wet red laterite.
- **The warden's chamber at Kettamala.** A high room with teak shutters facing west and a window ledge broad enough for a crouching body. Sensory signature: the creak of the third shutter slat, which Devan has never fixed.
- **The fort kalari.** A sunken earthen pit below the east wall where Devan trains alone before dawn. Sensory signature: sesame oil in the lamps and red earth that comes off on bare feet.
- **The Resin House.** The guild compound deep in the Blackbark Wood, a day's hard ride from Kettamala. It holds tapping sheds, training yards, and the kennels where caged odiyans are kept. Sensory signature: the bitter smell of nightbark resin, like scorched tamarind and pine sap.
- **Kodiyur, the royal port and capital.** A pepper harbour with the palace on a sea cliff. Sensory signature: drying fish and split pepper sacks on the quays, and the noon gun from the harbour tower.
- **The Monkey Pass.** The only cart road from the coast into the pepper hills, closed each monsoon. Sensory signature: the bark of langurs echoing off the cliff walls, which goes silent when strangers are on the road.
- **Neela's burned village (book 2 onward).** Stone house-footings on the coast side of the Monkey Pass, overgrown with lantana. Sensory signature: the smell of crushed lantana leaves, which Neela has always hated without knowing why.

## Cast

### Neela

- **Age:** 27.
- **Role:** heroine. Odiyan of the Resin House, ledger number Seventeen, sent to Kettamala as a contract bride.
- **Cover identity:** ward of the Chirayil pepper house of Kodiyur.
- **Night-shape:** leopard.
- **Want:** to finish her twelfth contract and kill Devan before the monsoon closes the pass. Twelve contracts earns release: her page burned and her birth name returned.
- **Need:** to be known by someone and not owned by them.
- **False belief:** "Anyone who truly sees me will put a leash on me."
- **Contradiction habit:** she learns and uses the name of every servant, guard and goat-boy in any house she enters, though a woman who wants to stay unseen should stay unremarkable. The guild gave her a number. She cannot stand anyone going unnamed.
- **Voice notes:**
  - Short sentences and dry, under-the-breath humour.
  - She counts exits, steps and breaths when frightened.
  - She never says "leopard" and calls her night-self "the other one".
  - She asks questions to avoid answering them.
  - She is precise about bodies and blades and vague about feelings until pushed.
- **What she knows at series start:**
  - Her contract terms: kill the warden in his chamber so it looks like a leopard kill, before the pass closes.
  - Her deadline: about 40 days.
  - Her witnesses are Master Chathu and Ammu.
  - Her page is in the ledger.
  - She does not know who bought the contract, because the guild never tells.
  - She does not know her birth name.
  - Six years ago she was lookout on a hill-fort job where a young woman died. She was moved there blindfolded and never learned which fort.
  - She does not know about self-naming.

### Devan

- **Age:** 32.
- **Role:** love interest. Warden of Kettamala and the queen's border commander for the Monkey Pass.
- **Want:** to find which royal councillor is selling forged pass-tokens to the Thurai raiders, before the raids reach the pepper villages.
- **Need:** to let someone guard his back. He has trusted no one since his sister was killed under contract.
- **False belief:** "Power is only safe in my hands if I never use it."
- **Contradiction habit:** he drills kalari alone every dawn, faster and deadlier than anyone in the fort, yet he has not sparred a living person in eight years. He commands men to fight and refuses to strike first himself.
- **Voice notes:**
  - Few words, and literal.
  - He answers questions with exact facts and no comfort, then does something kind without mentioning it.
  - He uses dry, slow humour and is rarely the first to laugh.
  - He never lies to Neela. When he can't tell the truth, he says "I won't answer that."
- **What he knows at series start:**
  - The witness rule. Border wardens are taught it to deal with rogues.
  - His sister Asha died six years ago on the west stair. Her wounds were a bear's, inside walls no bear could climb, so he believes an odiyan did it under contract.
  - Pass-tokens bearing real council wax are reaching Thurai raiders.
  - The queen's council pressed this marriage on him, and he distrusts the bride from the start.

### Ammu

- **Age:** 25.
- **Role:** Neela's guild-sister, raised beside her since age 5. She is Neela's contact during the contract.
- **Night-shape:** jackal.
- **Status:** dies in book 1, chapter 27. She stays dead.
- **Want:** to see Neela released, because then release is real and she might earn it too.
- **Need:** to believe her life is more than the guild's use of it.
- **False belief:** "We're safe as long as nobody but us has seen."
- **Contradiction habit:** she preaches the guild's caution, then takes reckless dares for Neela without hesitating.
- **Voice notes:**
  - Quick, teasing, a talker.
  - She calls Neela "chechi" (elder sister).
  - She hums the same invented fishing song when nervous.
- **What she knows at series start:**
  - She witnessed Neela's first turning at 14 and has never spoken the word.
  - She knows the self-naming loophole from an old master's whisper and has told no one.
  - She writes to Kirri, a "released" sister, and never gets an answer.

### Master Chathu

- **Age:** 61.
- **Role:** master of the Resin House and holder of the ledger. He is not an odiyan.
- **Want:** to keep the guild paid and the ledger whole.
- **Need:** to be more than the keeper of other people's cages. He will not admit this.
- **False belief:** "Without a leash they'd be hunted dogs. The ledger is mercy."
- **Contradiction habit:** he keeps a tidy accounts book of what each orphan cost to feed, and he weeps when a member dies, then sells her page to the crown.
- **Voice notes:** a soft, patient, fatherly tone. He talks in debts and balances.
- **What he knows at series start:**
  - Every member's shape and page.
  - That release is a lie: "released" members are caged in the kennels or sold to the crown.
  - The buyer of Devan's contract, and the queen's seal on it.

### Vellan

- **Age:** 39.
- **Role:** the guild's senior odiyan and enforcer.
- **Night-shape:** sloth bear.
- **Want:** release, which he has earned three times over and never received.
- **Need:** to face what he has done.
- **False belief:** "The blade isn't guilty, only the hand that throws it."
- **Contradiction habit:** he kills without hesitation, but he carves small wooden birds for the youngest orphans and leaves them on their sleeping mats.
- **Voice notes:** blunt, sparing, a low grumble, and fatalistic jokes.
- **What he knows at series start:**
  - He killed Asha on the west stair six years ago.
  - Neela was the lookout.
  - He suspects release is a lie but has no proof.

### Councillor Ittan

- **Age:** 49.
- **Role:** Keeper of the Passes on the queen's council, and the pass-seller.
- **Status:** executed by the crown in book 2, chapter 5.
- **Want:** wealth, and to keep the queen's secret treaty running so he stays indispensable.
- **Need:** none he will act on.
- **False belief:** "Every border has a price; I only collect it."
- **Contradiction habit:** he is genuinely devoted to his invalid wife, and buys deaths to pay her physicians.
- **Voice notes:** genial and wordy. He calls everyone "my friend".
- **What he knows at series start:**
  - He proposed the bride and bought the contract through the guild.
  - He sells extra pass-tokens on top of the queen's controlled passages.
  - If Devan died, his widow would hold Kettamala's pass-seal until a new warden came, and Ittan planned to take that seal from her.

### Queen Nirmala of Varanad

- **Age:** 54.
- **Role:** ruler. She is a legitimate antagonist, never a strawman.
- **Want:** to keep Varanad unburned by Thurai, by any quiet means.
- **Need:** to trust her people with the truth.
- **False belief:** "A realm is kept safe by what it never has to see."
- **Contradiction habit:** she personally answers every petition from widows of border soldiers, and she signs death writs for loyal servants who get in the way.
- **Voice notes:** warm, unhurried and courteous. She never raises her voice and never says a cruel word, only cruel facts.
- **What she knows at series start:**
  - She keeps a secret treaty with the Thurai sea lords: limited raids through controlled passes in exchange for no fleet landing.
  - She sealed the writ that paid for Devan's death after he got close to Ittan.
  - She keeps the crown leaf: the pages of her odiyan night-guard.

### Captain Muthu

- **Age:** 35.
- **Role:** Devan's second-in-command at Kettamala, and secretly the queen's informant.
- **Want:** to serve the crown and keep Devan alive. He believes those are the same thing.
- **Need:** to choose a loyalty and stop living two.
- **False belief:** "If I report everything, nobody I love gets hurt."
- **Contradiction habit:** he is the most honest-seeming man in the fort and writes a monthly letter to the palace that he never mentions.
- **Voice notes:** cheerful and loud, with a big laugh and army slang. He gets very quiet when lying.
- **What he knows at series start:** he reports on Devan to the queen's household. He knows nothing of the contract or the treaty.

### Janu

- **Age:** 61.
- **Role:** steward of Kettamala. She raised Devan and Asha after their mother died.
- **Want:** to see Devan married, rested and fed.
- **Need:** to forgive herself for not being at the west stair the night Asha died.
- **False belief:** "If I keep the house in order, nothing else will break."
- **Contradiction habit:** she complains about every guest and then overfeeds them.
- **Voice notes:**
  - Scolding and affectionate.
  - She tells leopard stories at supper, which Devan quietly asks her to stop after the wedding.
- **What she knows at series start:** the fort's every corner and the hour of Asha's death. She knows nothing of odiyans beyond old tales.

### Asha

- **Age:** died at 22, six years before book 1.
- **Role:** Devan's younger sister. She was his second at the fort and found the first forged pass-token.
- **Want (in life):** to prove the passes were being sold.
- **Need, false belief and contradiction habit:** shown in memory only. She believed "the truth protects whoever finds it". She was fearless with soldiers and terrified of the dark.
- **Voice notes (in memory and letters):** fast and funny. She signed every note with a drawn parrot.
- **What is known of her:**
  - She was killed on the west stair by Vellan in sloth-bear shape.
  - Neela kept watch at the stair foot.
  - Her murder was bought through Ittan.

### Kirri

- **Age:** 31.
- **Role:** a guild-sister "released" two years ago.
- **Night-shape:** civet.
- **Status:** in fact caged in the Resin House kennels, her mind fading.
- **Want and need:** she is now beyond wanting. Before her caging she wanted a fishing boat of her own.
- **False belief and contradiction habit:** shown in Ammu's memories. She trusted Chathu completely and was the guild's best liar.
- **Voice notes:** none on the page in her own voice.
- **What is known of her:** Neela finds her in the kennels in book 1, chapter 26, wearing her old silver anklet.

## Relationship worksheet

Couple: Neela and Devan.

1. **What does each consciously want, visible in chapter 1?**
   - Neela wants Devan dead before the monsoon closes the Monkey Pass, so she can earn release and her birth name. In chapter 1 she maps his chamber, his guards and his habits during their own wedding feast.
   - Devan wants the name of the councillor selling pass-tokens. In chapter 1 he studies the bride the council chose, because the match is the first move anyone on that council has made toward his fort.

2. **What do they secretly need?**
   - Neela needs to be fully seen, by someone with the power to cage her who chooses not to, and to be left free.
   - Devan needs to stop being the only wall, and to let someone at his back who could strike him and doesn't.

3. **What belief about love or self holds them back?**
   - Neela: "Anyone who truly sees me will put a leash on me."
   - Devan: "Power is only safe in my hands if I never use it."

4. **What do they misunderstand about the other?**
   - Neela reads Devan's silence as a hunter's patience: he is saving the word until she is most useful caged. This is a reasonable inference, because every witness she has known (Chathu, the crown) treats the word as a leash.
   - Devan reads Neela as an instrument without a will. He thinks she will do whatever the guild orders, so the only lever is the one in his mouth. This is also reasonable: the odiyan who killed Asha did exactly that.

5. **What can each offer that nobody else can?**
   - Neela can name his buyer. She can hunt the forged-pass couriers at night as a leopard, and nobody suspects a beast. She also knows the guild from inside.
   - Devan can be a witness who won't speak. After self-naming, he is the one person whose bond makes her uncageable. He holds the pass-seal, the fort and the queen's ear, which is everything the guild and Ittan want.

6. **What makes loving the other costly, in world-rule terms?**
   - For her, every night beside him risks the word slipping out in his sleep or anger, and every human night costs resin she must skip and ache she must bear.
   - Failing the contract means the guild forces resin on her and Chathu reads her page.
   - After self-naming, his wounds are hers. She is rogue, and the crown is entitled to hunt her.
   - For him, loving her means harbouring the guild that killed his sister, and defying a council (and later a queen) that wants him dead.
   - Every battle he fights from book 1, chapter 27 on puts her body at risk too.

7. **What would make trust rational? Three observable choices each.**
   - Devan:
     - He closes her shutters at every dusk from the second night, so no servant can witness her.
     - He calls the leopard "the cat" every night, even with a fever (book 1, chapter 13).
     - He withdraws the silence bargain unconditionally at the midpoint, before she gives him anything.
   - Neela:
     - She has a clean killing marma strike on him during his dawn drill (chapter 11) and pulls it.
     - As a leopard she kills the raider scouts set to ambush his patrol (chapter 14), though his death would end her contract.
     - She gives him Ittan's name freely (chapter 23), after the bargain is gone and he has stopped asking.

8. **What changes because they fall in love?**
   - In them: Neela lets herself be known and later chooses belonging. Devan learns to use power openly and answer for it.
   - In the world: self-naming becomes public knowledge, which breaks the guild's monopoly. The ledger is handed back page by page (book 2). The crown leaf is undone and the guild is remade as a company that owns itself (book 3).

9. **Why are these two uniquely suited to challenge each other?**
   - She has survived by hiding herself. He has survived by hiding his strength. Each recognises the other's discipline as a cage dressed as safety.
   - She can see that his restraint is fear, because hers looks the same. He can see the person in her, because she insists on learning every servant's name.

10. **What does each refuse to give up, even for the other?**
    - Neela will not give up her guild-sisters. She won't betray Ammu, Kirri, or even Vellan's right to his own page.
    - Devan will not lie to her. He will not give up the border: he will keep the pass held even when the queen strips him of his office.

11. **What does the reader know before either character does?**
    - From book 1, chapter 2, that Devan witnessed her turning. Neela doesn't learn this until chapter 15.
    - That the west stair where Asha died is the stair Neela kept watch at. The reader connects Neela's unease at the parrot newel (chapter 10) with Devan's memory of Asha's parrot drawings (chapter 12) long before either character connects them in book 2.
    - That Captain Muthu writes to the palace (book 1, chapter 12). The reader learns the reason only in book 2.

## Book arcs

### Book 1: Vow of Teeth

- **Central question:** will Neela kill the husband she was sold to?
- **Romantic phase completed:** enemies to allies to married in truth. Meeting, barrier, attraction, declaration and betrothal (a remade vow) all land here.
- **Love interest's own goal:** unmask the pass-seller. Resolved when Devan arrests Ittan (chapter 29).
- **Act 1 (chapters 1 to 8):**
  - Wedding at sunset. Neela waits for Devan to sleep, smears resin at the window and turns. Devan, feigning sleep, sees it all and stays silent (chapter 2, his POV).
  - The monsoon deadline is set at about 40 days.
  - Devan's quiet protections begin. Neela reads them as indifference.
- **Midpoint reversal (chapters 15 to 16):**
  - At the first storm of the monsoon, Neela comes back at dawn half-turned and finds Devan waiting.
  - He tells her he has seen her since their wedding night. He offers the bargain once: his silence for the name of whoever bought his death. She refuses.
  - In chapter 16 he names the bargain as what it is, the guild's leash in his mouth, and gives his silence freely with no terms.
  - Every closed shutter in the first half now rereads as protection.
- **Act 2b (chapters 17 to 24):**
  - **First kiss (chapter 19):** at dusk on the rampart, with resin on her wrists. The world rule makes it dangerous three ways:
    - The turning will begin in front of him if she stays.
    - Ammu is watching from the tree line and will report.
    - One careless word from him in passion could cage her.
    She breaks away at the first burn.
  - **First explicit scene (chapter 22):** a night she skips resin. She lets him see the scars at her throat and wrists, and asks him to stay till morning. The ache starts counting, and Ammu covers her missed signal.
  - **Disclosure ladder:**
    - Chapter 21: Devan tells her about the surrendering raider captain he killed with one marma strike at 24.
    - Chapter 23: she gives him Ittan's name unasked.
- **Point of ritual death (chapters 25 to 27):**
  - Vellan comes to finish the job and leaves Devan poisoned.
  - Neela rides to the Resin House for the antidote. She is taken and dragged to the kennels, where she finds Kirri caged.
  - Chathu forces resin on her and waits for dusk with her page.
- **Climax (chapter 27):**
  - Ammu frees her and tells her the self-naming loophole.
  - Devan arrives, half-dead, with Muthu.
  - At dusk in the yard, Neela turns in front of him and says "Leopard" to him.
  - Chathu reads her page aloud and nothing happens. The page burns in his hands.
  - Ammu, as a jackal, draws Vellan and the hunters away, and is killed. Real loss: she stays dead.
- **Chapters 28 to 32:**
  - Neela feels Devan's poison wound open in her own side.
  - Ittan arrives to seal the pass for the monsoon and is arrested with forged tokens (chapter 29).
  - Recognition and declaration (chapter 30).
  - They remake their vows in daylight before the fort (chapter 31). Second explicit scene: she turns back at dawn in his arms, the first time she lets anyone see the return.
- **Heroine's belief change:**
  - From "Anyone who truly sees me will put a leash on me."
  - To "Being seen by the right person is the only thing that makes me unleashable."
  - **Proving choice:** she turns, by choice, in front of the man she was paid to kill, and gives him the word.
- **Devan's movement:** from withholding power to giving it away. He strikes Vellan to save her (chapter 27), the first blow he has struck at a living person in eight years.
- **Series hinge (chapter 32):** among Ittan's effects is the guild's contract chit, and the payment writ bears the queen's own seal.

### Book 2: Oath of Claws

- **Central question:** can they steal the guild's ledger without becoming its new masters?
- **Romantic phase completed:** commitment tested by the past. This book is about repair after a betrayal neither of them chose.
- **Love interest's own goal:** prove to the crown's high court that Asha's murder and the pass sales were one conspiracy, and keep his garrison loyal.
- **Opening:**
  - The queen executes Ittan before he can testify (chapter 5).
  - Neela is rogue and hunted, and her resin supply is gone.
- **Midpoint reversal (chapter 16):**
  - They take the ledger in a monsoon-night raid.
  - Devan cannot read guild cipher, so Neela translates. She reads the contract for Asha aloud honestly, including "lookout: Seventeen". She finds out at the same moment he does.
  - The west stair from book 1 rereads as the scene of her crime.
- **Point of ritual death (chapters 25 to 27):**
  - Chathu and Vellan retake the Resin House.
  - Devan holds Vellan's page before dawn while Vellan is in bear shape. He could cage his sister's killer with one sentence.
  - Neela holds the rest of the ledger, and the guild's odiyans will follow whoever holds it.
- **Heroine's belief change:**
  - From "What the guild made me do isn't mine to answer for."
  - To "I owe repair even for what I was made to do."
  - **Proving choices:**
    - She confesses her part in Asha's death to Janu at the west stair.
    - She stops Devan from reading Vellan's page. Instead she reads every page aloud by daylight, including Vellan's, and gives each odiyan their own. She makes herself no one's master.
  - Devan lets Vellan go. This is the hardest act of power he has made: refusing it.
- **Real loss:** the queen's writ strips Devan of Kettamala for harbouring a rogue. He never returns as warden.
- **Ending (HFN):** they ride into the hills as husband and wife with forty odiyans who each hold their own page.
- **Series hinge:** the ledger's last section lists odiyans "sold to the crown". Their pages are missing, held in the queen's crown leaf. Vellan confirms that the queen's own guard are odiyans.

### Book 3: Crown of Night

- **Central question:** can they free every odiyan, even the queen's guard, if it leaves Varanad without its night-guard as Thurai raiders land?
- **Romantic phase completed:** partnership as equals in public. This brings the HEA: love that survives power used openly.
- **Love interest's own goal:** hold the Monkey Pass against the Thurai landing, without office or crown sanction.
- **Midpoint reversal (chapter 16):**
  - Neela steals the crown leaf, but finds that handing pages back is not enough. The queen herself witnessed every guard's turning and is a live witness.
  - Full freedom for the thirty guards means each must self-name to a chosen person. Every pair will then share wounds, so the night-guard can't fight without bleeding their partners.
  - Meanwhile the queen offers Neela command of a page-free night-guard, "safe, unseen, and mine".
- **Point of ritual death (chapters 25 to 27):**
  - Devan takes a raider spear at the Monkey Pass, covering newly self-named odiyans.
  - The shared wound drags Neela down beside him, and both nearly die by dawn.
- **Heroine's belief change:**
  - From "Freedom means belonging to no one."
  - To "Freedom is choosing who I'm bound to."
  - **Proving choice:** she refuses the queen's command. At dawn after the battle she writes her own name first on the roll of the sworn company, by daylight, in front of every witness, and binds herself to it by choice.
- **Real loss:**
  - Devan's right hand never recovers its strength. His killing marma strike is gone.
  - Vellan dies holding the pass. Before he dies he gives Devan Asha's last words.
- **Devan's arc completed:** he uses power openly, under the company's vote, and accepts what it costs him.
- **Ending (HEA):**
  - The Night Company is chartered, owning itself and contracting by vote.
  - Queen Nirmala signs the charter and burns the crown leaf by her own hand. She keeps her throne and loses her hidden knives.
  - Neela and Devan lead the Company from a new hall above the pass.
- **Series hinge (teaser only, everything resolved):** a Thurai envoy asks to hire the Company, and brings a rogue odiyan who was never in any ledger.

## Theme

**Is a life safer unseen, or freer known?**

- **Answer one: safety lies in being unseen.**
  - Ammu holds it with love. Never being witnessed has kept her and Neela alive for twenty years, and she is right that it has.
  - Queen Nirmala holds it at the scale of the realm. A kingdom kept safe by knives nobody sees has, in fact, not burned.
  - Master Chathu holds a corrupted version: unseen means owned, and owned means safe.
- **Answer two: freedom lies in being known.**
  - Devan holds it, though he spends book 1 hiding his own strength.
  - Neela moves from the first answer to the second across the trilogy and pays for the move every time.
- **The ending does not mock the first answer.** The Company still works by night, and Devan loses his hand to the openness. Both answers cost something.

## Cover style

- **Series look:** close-cropped, intimate, dangerous. Each cover shows a detail of bodies or objects, never a full face. No real person's likeness and no trademarked imagery.
- **Palette:** deep black, turmeric gold, laterite red, ivory.
- **Central motif per book:**
  - **Book 1, Vow of Teeth:** a woman's hand wearing a plain gold wedding bangle lies flat on a man's bare chest. Her fingertips are turning into leopard claws, catching gold light.
  - **Book 2, Oath of Claws:** a single ivory ledger page torn down the middle by four claw marks, its edges just catching gold fire.
  - **Book 3, Crown of Night:** a circlet made of gold leopard claws resting on black cloth, with a thin red cord threaded through it like a vow-binding.
- **Typography:**
  - Title: a tall, high-contrast serif in all caps, with one letter's terminal drawn out into a fine claw hook. It is foil-gold on black and set large across the top third.
  - Pen name (ANYA KALLOOR): a clean geometric sans in widely spaced small caps, in ivory, along the bottom edge.
  - Series line: "The Odiyan Contracts" in small spaced laterite-red caps above the title.
- **Composition:** the motif sits just below centre on a black field, lit from one side as if by a single oil lamp. A faint leopard-rosette pattern shows in gloss black on matte black in the background. All three books use the same placement so they read as a set.
- **Mood:** hushed, dangerous, sensual. The feeling is the moment before a word is spoken.

## Glossary

- **Odiyan:** a guild-trained assassin who takes one animal shape by night using nightbark resin.
- **Nightbark resin:** bitter black sap from trees grown only by the Resin House. Smeared on the skin after sunset, it triggers the turning.
- **The turning:** an odiyan's change into her night-shape at dusk, or back at first light. Seeing it makes you a witness.
- **Night-shape:** the one animal an odiyan becomes, fixed at her first turning. Saying it is the dangerous word.
- **Witness:** anyone who has seen an odiyan turn with their own eyes.
- **Caging:** a witness speaking the night-shape aloud, in the odiyan's hearing, before dawn. It locks her in animal form for life.
- **Self-naming:** an odiyan speaking her own night-shape to one chosen person during a dusk turning. It makes the bond mutual and voids all other witnesses.
- **Wound-share:** the cost of self-naming. The bonded pair feel each other's injuries for life.
- **The ledger:** the Resin House's book of witness pages and intake rolls. Holding a page gives a witness's power over its subject.
- **Page:** one odiyan's sworn witness entry in resin-ink.
- **Intake roll:** the record of an odiyan's birth name and village, fastened to her page.
- **Release:** the guild's promise to burn a member's page after twelve contracts. In fact it is a lie.
- **Rogue:** an odiyan with no page vouched by a master, hunted by guild and crown.
- **The ache:** the fever of an odiyan who goes a moon without resin.
- **The kennels:** the Resin House cages where caged odiyans are kept.
- **Crown leaf:** the queen's secret set of pages for odiyans sold into her night-guard.
- **Night-guard:** the queen's hidden corps of odiyans.
- **Pass-token:** a wax-sealed tally that lets a cart or company through the Monkey Pass.
- **The Night Company:** the sworn company of free odiyans chartered in book 3.
- **Kalari:** the earthen training pit and the fighting art taught in it.
- **Marma:** strikes to the body's vital points, which can heal or kill.
- **Mundu:** a wrapped cloth garment worn at the waist.
- **Kasavu:** ivory cloth with a gold border, worn at Neela's wedding.
- **Chechi:** elder sister; Ammu's name for Neela.
- **Kindi:** a spouted brass water vessel kept by the warden's bed.
- **Padippura:** the roofed gatehouse at the fort's entrance.
