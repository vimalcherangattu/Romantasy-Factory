# A Name Held in Trust: Series Bible

Pen name: Mara Vellacourt

## Series overview

- **Series title:** A Name Held in Trust
- **Books:** 1. *A Name Held in Trust*; 2. *The River Remembers*; 3. *Unnamed*
- **Structure:** one couple, Elin Marrowgate and Aurek Sall, across the whole trilogy
- **Primary engine:** court. The power is in information gaps: who holds which name, who knows which order, which law covers which act. One costly metaphysical rule, Name-Ownership, sits underneath every move at court.
- **Subgenre:** romantasy (core). Romance and fantasy carry equal weight, with dark edges.
- **Heat:** open door, moderate. Two or three explicit scenes per book. Each one changes the relationship or the plot. Intimacy waits until both leads choose it freely, and the page shows both choices.
- **Darkness:** dark edges, not grimdark. Real losses stay lost. There is no sexual violence on the page. Aurek's capacity for harm points outward and he keeps it disciplined. The empire's cruelty is institutional and specific: dams, ledgers, held names. It is never cruelty for flavour.
- **Ending promise per book:**
  - Book 1: HFN. Married, in love, and the flood is stopped. Then the hinge: the Emperor holds Elin's name.
  - Book 2: HFN. Still two people inside one name, chosen again. Then the hinge: the Rolls pass to Aurek.
  - Book 3: HEA. Every held name is given to the river, the split name included. They remarry by river custom with nothing held.
- **POV plan:** dual close third person, past tense. Elin carries about two thirds of the chapters and Aurek about one third. Each scene has one POV, and a POV switch only happens at a scene break (`* * *`). Aurek's chapters exist to create dramatic irony. The reader learns his orders and his mutiny before Elin does. The reader never learns what the Emperor holds before Elin does.
- **Naming convention on the page:** no true name is ever printed in the text. Characters think of a true name as "the word", "his name", or "the given name". Use-names (Elin, Aurek) are ordinary names with no power.
- **Trope stack:** arranged marriage plus secret mission supplies proximity. Conqueror and conquered enemies to lovers supplies opposition, and the enmity is rooted in the dams, not in rudeness. Forbidden love comes from the treaty and the charter. The slow burn runs to a first kiss with a cost.
- **Convention inverted:** in the arranged marriage, the wife holds total power over the husband.
- **Convention honoured:** the arranged-marriage slow burn, honoured in full, with a wedding night that does not include sex.

## World rules

### Rule 1: Name-Ownership

Every person has a true name, separate from their use-name. A true name is a single spoken word. In the empire, a mother whispers it to the infant at birth and then writes it into the House book. The named person knows it.

- **What people can do:** whoever holds a person's given true name can command them. The holder speaks the true name, then an imperative, in the named person's hearing. The named person cannot refuse. A command binds until it is fulfilled.
  - A *standing command* ("come when I call") is possible. Each word of it costs the speaker as if it were a separate command.
  - Holders can transfer a held name by speaking it to a new holder with the words of gift. Names are property. The Crown records them in the Rolls, and they pass by will or inheritance like land.
- **What people can never do:**
  - A name cannot be taken back once given. The named person can never reclaim it.
  - A name cannot be given whole to its own owner. The Rolls call this "void" and the name stays with the giver.
  - An overheard name has no power. Only a *given* name commands.
  - A command does not carry beyond earshot. It cannot be written, relayed, or shouted through a door the named person cannot hear through.
- **The cost:** every command scars the speaker's own voice. The court calls the scar a *rasp*. The voice roughens, drops, cracks, and narrows. Enough commands leave the speaker mute. Scars are permanent.
  - Emperor Casimir has commanded for thirty years. He can now speak only a few words a day, and an officer called the Voice speaks for him in public.
  - A rasped voice is a visible ledger of how much someone has commanded.
- **Who benefits:** the imperial Great Houses and the Crown, who hold their servants' and clerks' names.
  - Holding is how the empire guarantees loyalty without armies.
  - Court service at rank requires a name given to a House or to the Crown.
- **Who is excluded:** the river peoples of Lissane. A River Mother speaks a newborn's name once into the delta water and it is never spoken again. Even the named person never learns it.
  - Water-named people cannot be held, so they cannot be commanded.
  - For the same reason, they cannot give a name. That bars them from imperial office, from marriage under imperial law (the wife customarily gives her name too), and from any guild that requires a held name.
  - Imperial law calls them "unpledged" and treats them as untrustworthy by definition.
- **What happens to lovers:** trust is made literal. In an imperial marriage the husband gives his name to the wife at the wedding. He whispers it so that only she hears it. The wife may give hers in return. For the holder, every request ("stay", "come to bed", "don't go") is one word away from an order. For the held, every kindness from the holder might be a choice, or might be a word held back.
- **The intimate act that becomes dangerous:** naming. A holder who speaks the true name in bed is one breath from command. If she says it and then "don't stop", consent becomes unprovable for him and a weapon for her.
  - The safe intimacy is the use-name.
  - The dangerous, trusting intimacy is the true name spoken alone, with nothing after it.
- **The recurring dilemma:** use the name to win something that matters (a people, a life, a war), or never use it and prove the held person can be trusted unforced.
- **The loophole:** a holder may split a name between two holders.
  - Each holder holds a half.
  - A command then requires both halves spoken together, by both holders, in the named person's hearing.
  - The other holder can be anyone, including the named person. A name given half back to its owner is not void, because it is not whole. Nobody has done this in recorded memory. Elin finds it through river trust-law in Book 1.
- **The loophole's cost:** each holder becomes partly the other.
  - It begins with dreams of the other's memories, then waking memories that arrive unbidden, then shared wounds. A cut on one opens as a welt on the other, and a broken bone shows as deep bruising.
  - Both voices pay the rasp for a joint command.
  - The merge cannot be undone. Giving the halves away later ends the command, but the shared memories stay.

### Rule 2: The river holds without speaking

This rule is an expansion of Rule 1, not a second magic.

- **What people can do:** a name given into living, moving river water is held by the river. The river never commands. A water-named person is free.
- **What they can never do:** retrieve a name from moving water by any ordinary means.
- **The cost:** names settle with the silt. Where water is slowed or trapped, names can be sieved out of the silt by trained *weir-clerks*. A sieved name can be given like any other, with the sieve-master as first giver.
- **Who benefits:** whoever controls the weirs.
- **Who is excluded:** the river peoples, who do not know this is happening until Book 2.
- **What happens to lovers:** two people who give a name to an open river can never hold each other by it.
- **The intimate act that becomes dangerous:** the naming of a child at the Mother Pools. Under the dams it has become an act of surrender.
- **The recurring dilemma:** keep the dams, which feed the drought-struck imperial heartland, or open them and make every river name safe.
- **The loophole:** any holder may give a held name to open water. That frees it permanently.
- **The loophole's cost:** a name given to the river can never be given to a lover. The imperial marriage-gift dies with it.

### Rule 3: The Wardenry Charter

This is law, not magic, but the rules lean on it.

- **What it allows:** a Warden-General's name may be held only by a spouse. The clause is three hundred years old and was written so that no emperor could make the army one man's puppet. A Warden who acts under a spouse's command is not culpable for that act.
- **What it never allows:** the clause never lets the Crown hold a Warden's name directly.
- **The cost:** the *spouse* answers for any act done under her command as if she had done it herself. If that act is treason, she hangs. Under the Treaty of Lissane, treason by the bride voids the peace and frees the garrisons to "pacify" the delta.
- **The loophole:** under a split holding, liability is joint. A Warden who co-holds his own name shares the charge.

## Places

- **Velmarch, the imperial capital.** A white city on dry chalk heights above the drought-cracked heartland, the Dry. Sensory signature: chalk dust in the teeth, and the constant creak and slosh of the bucket-chains that carry canal water uphill day and night.
- **The Hall of Rolls.** A vaulted archive where every held name is entered in cipher. Sensory signature: cold beeswax and iron-gall ink, and a hush so complete that pen nibs sound like insects.
- **The Warden's House.** Elin and Aurek's marital residence, a narrow lime-washed house inside the barracks walls. Sensory signature: the grit of whetstones on the sill, and bitter orange peel that Aurek dries on the stove to cover the smell of armour oil.
- **The Map Room of the War Council.** A long chamber with a brass relief map of the empire and the delta. Sensory signature: candle smoke, and the tick of cooling brass as the room empties.
- **The Lissane delta.** Reed islands, brackish channels, stilt-houses. Sensory signature: salt mud that sucks at boots, and the dry rattle of reeds in wind.
- **The Mother Pools at Saltreach.** Spring-fed naming pools at the delta's heart. Sensory signature: frogs falling silent all at once when a River Mother speaks, and water so clear it looks like air.
- **The Saltreach Locks.** Imperial silt-locks at the delta mouth, officially there to "prevent sea incursion". Sensory signature: the scrape of brass sieves in the dark, and the sour smell of dredged silt in sealed clay jars.
- **Hollin Weir.** The great upriver dam and its reservoir, completed four years before Book 1. Sensory signature: a roar felt in the molars before it is heard, and cold spray that never stops falling on the gate-walk.
- **Sedge Ferry.** A delta village of two hundred people below Hollin. It is drowned in Book 1. Sensory signature: eel smoke and the ferry-bell's flat clank.

## Cast

Every romantic lead is an adult, with their age stated on the page before any intimate beat.

### Elin Marrowgate, 30. Heroine.

- **Role:** envoy of Lissane and river-law advocate. She becomes the Warden-General's wife.
  - She is famous for arguing the Weir Case before the imperial Circuit Court three years ago. She lost on appeal but won a water-share that kept the inner delta alive.
  - She is water-named and has never known her true name.
- **Want:** to free Lissane from the empire's dams, which are slowly starving its delta. In Chapter 1 she is sent to use Aurek's name to do it.
- **Need:** to believe she is worth more than her usefulness.
- **False belief:** "Love is leverage that hasn't been spent yet."
- **Contradiction habit:** she memorises everyone's kindnesses and never uses them. She keeps a mental ledger of every favour ever done for her, and in court she never calls in a single one.
- **Voice notes:**
  - Precise and conditional: "If that's so, then".
  - River idioms: "upstream of that", "that's silt, not bottom".
  - She asks questions she already knows the answers to, to watch how people answer.
  - She goes quieter, not louder, when angry.
  - After Book 1's climax her voice carries a slight rasp, which she hates and does not hide.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - Lissane's Delta Council has sent her to use the name to break the dams.
  - The treaty asked for "the advocate Marrowgate" by name. She thinks this is because of the Weir Case.
  - She knows the Wardenry Charter exists but has not yet read the spouse clause.
  - She does not know she is held.

### Aurek Sall, 35. Love interest.

- **Role:** Warden-General of the Corvantine Empire, its most decorated soldier.
  - He is a former rebel. At 21 he fought in the Saltmarch Rising, a frontier rebellion that was crushed fourteen years ago, and was pardoned into imperial service.
  - Until his wedding his name has never been held.
- **Want:** to stop the Emperor's plan to flood the delta entirely. His own goal, unrelated to Elin, is a slow mutiny inside the war council. He wants to break General Vey's majority and end the Dry Wars, the campaigns of conquest fought for water.
- **Need:** to be chosen rather than obeyed.
- **False belief:** "No one keeps a promise they're not forced to."
- **Contradiction habit:** he insists nobody keeps unforced promises, and he keeps every small one he makes himself, to the minute. If he says he will be home at the tenth bell, he walks in on the tenth bell, and he will cross the city on a broken ankle to do it.
- **Voice notes:**
  - Short declaratives and a soldier's economy.
  - His humour is so dry that people miss it.
  - He never says "please". It sounds like begging to a man who once begged.
  - He uses her full name, "Elin Marrowgate", until the first kiss.
  - He goes very still instead of raising his voice.
- **Private face:** in Book 1 he tends a row of dying orange seedlings on the barracks roof. They are the only living thing he has ever owned.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - He holds a sealed order, in blue wax, to lead the Sluice: opening Hollin at Thaw Moon high water.
  - Lissane sent Elin to use his name. His informant on the Delta Council told him.
  - He knows the Charter's spouse clause exactly.
  - He leads the Quiet Table, his mutiny network.
  - He executed Tamsin Orrey with his own hand. It was the price of his pardon.
  - He does not know the Emperor holds Elin's name.

### Emperor Casimir Orlenne, 61. Antagonist.

- **Role:** Emperor of the Corvantine Empire for thirty-one years.
- **Want:** water for the Dry, where the heartland's famine is real, and a dynasty that cannot be challenged.
- **Need:** to be trusted without having to compel it. He will not meet this need.
- **False belief:** "Every loyalty is a debt waiting to default."
- **Contradiction habit:** he spends his last words on small, private courtesies, such as thanking servants. Meanwhile he spends other people's lives without comment.
- **Voice notes:**
  - His own voice is a ruined whisper, and he speaks through the Voice in public.
  - When he speaks for himself, it matters.
  - He watches mouths.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - He holds Elin's name, sieved from Saltreach silt.
  - He arranged the marriage so that Aurek's name would sit with a woman he can command.
  - The flood's real purpose is to drown the delta into a held lake, so that every name already in it and every future naming is caught.
  - Three thousand river names sit on his private Rolls.

### Princess Ysolde Orlenne, 27. Heir and court player.

- **Want:** the throne without a civil war.
- **Need:** to act without permission.
- **False belief:** "Patience is the only weapon a held woman has."
- **Contradiction habit:** she gambles recklessly at cards and nowhere else.
- **Voice notes:** bright, social, with every sentence carrying a second meaning. She laughs at the wrong moment on purpose.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - Her father holds her name.
  - She is a silent member of the Quiet Table.
  - She does not know about the river names.

### Pell Dorran, 28. Aurek's adjutant.

- **Role:** Aurek's closest friend and runner for the Quiet Table. Pell dies in Book 1 holding the Sedge Ferry sluice. The death stays.
- **Want:** to see Aurek happy and to keep his own sister's farm watered in the Dry.
- **Need:** to be more than useful. He mirrors Elin's need.
- **False belief:** "Good men are the ones who stay in the second rank."
- **Contradiction habit:** he jokes constantly and never jokes about Aurek.
- **Voice notes:** fast, warm, with heartland slang ("dry as a tax-man").
- **Knows at series start:** the Sluice order and the Quiet Table's membership.

### General Brannoc Vey, 52. Rival general.

- **Role:** leader of the war-faction majority in the war council and backup commander for the Sluice.
- **Want:** the Dry fed by any means, and Aurek's office.
- **Need:** to be respected rather than feared. He is a capable logistician whom everyone treats as a thug.
- **False belief:** "Mercy is a delay that costs more later."
- **Contradiction habit:** he is a meticulous gardener of a flowerless herb plot.
- **Voice notes:** blunt, numerical, and he quotes ration figures from memory. He has a heavy rasp from commanding his House servants.
- **Knows at series start:** the Sluice order. He also knows Aurek executed Tamsin Orrey and resents him for it, because Vey commanded the Saltmarch campaign and the pardon went over his head.

### Maud Tessary, 58. Speaker of Lissane's Delta Council.

- **Role:** Elin's patron, who sent her.
- **Want:** Lissane surviving as a people, at whatever price.
- **Need:** to confess.
- **False belief:** "A leader's sins are the people's savings."
- **Contradiction habit:** she gives away her own food at council dinners and keeps every document.
- **Voice notes:** maternal and practical, with a fondness for proverbs.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - At the surrender twelve years ago she gave the empire the Mother Pools naming registers.
  - Since the Emperor's envoy asked about Elin specifically four years ago, she has suspected that Elin's name was taken.
  - She proposed Elin as bride anyway.
- **Tell:** she gave Elin a river-glass vial "for luck".

### Ressa Hale, 71. River Mother.

- **Role:** the River Mother who named Elin at the Mother Pools thirty years ago. She dies at Saltreach in Book 2. The death stays.
- **Want:** to keep naming children in the old way.
- **Need:** to be believed.
- **False belief:** "The water forgives everything given to it."
- **Contradiction habit:** she names everything (boats, dogs, pots) except the children.
- **Voice notes:** slow, sparing, and she never finishes a sentence she thinks the listener can finish.
- **Knows at series start:** names are "going quiet in the water". When she sings at the Pools, some named people no longer answer the old way. She calls them *quiet ones*.

### Corin Marrowgate, 24. Elin's brother.

- **Role:** ferryman and smuggler with the delta resistance.
- **Want:** the dams blown, now.
- **Need:** to trust his sister's slower way.
- **False belief:** "Talking is what people do instead of fighting."
- **Contradiction habit:** he is a coward about letters. He can't write to Elin and he rows forty miles to say things instead.
- **Voice notes:** blunt, funny, delta dialect, curses by the tide.
- **Knows at series start:** the resistance's cache sites. He does not know Elin's mission.

### Lady Sabeth Quill, 49. Rollkeeper.

- **Role:** keeper of the Hall of Rolls.
- **Want:** an accurate record.
- **Need:** to stop being complicit.
- **False belief:** "A ledger has no conscience, so its keeper needn't."
- **Contradiction habit:** she corrects the grammar of death warrants.
- **Voice notes:** a fine dry whisper (a rasp from commanding archive clerks), exact, and fond of footnotes.
- **Knows at series start:**
  - The Emperor's private Roll lists about three thousand names entered in river-script, Elin's among them.
  - Hesper Dunn's name is held by the Crown.

### Hesper Dunn, 33. Elin's court-assigned maid.

- **Role:** maid. Her name is held by the Crown and she reports under command.
- **Want:** to buy back her younger sister's indenture.
- **Need:** forgiveness without excuse.
- **False belief:** "Nobody's sorry for a held woman."
- **Contradiction habit:** she leaves small comforts (warmed slippers, a lit lamp) for the woman she spies on.
- **Voice notes:** quiet, formal, and a half-second late on every answer.
- **Knows at series start:** she has a standing command to report everything she hears in the Warden's House.

### Dov Ashcombe, 46. Weir-master of Hollin Weir.

- **Role:** engineer. He dies in the managed breach in Book 3. The death stays.
- **Want:** a dam that doesn't kill anyone.
- **Need:** to be told that he isn't the dam.
- **False belief:** "Engineers build. Others choose."
- **Contradiction habit:** he refuses to look at the reservoir he made.
- **Voice notes:** technical and gentle. He explains water like a doting uncle.
- **Knows at series start:** how the Sluice works and that the Saltreach Locks are silt-traps, not sea-defences. He does not know what the silt holds.

### Anselm Rooke, 44. The Emperor's Voice.

- **Role:** officer who speaks the Emperor's words in public.
- **Want:** to be forgotten when this is over.
- **Need:** a voice of his own.
- **False belief:** "I only repeat."
- **Contradiction habit:** he changes small words when repeating, softening them, and believes no one notices.
- **Voice notes:** a trained, beautiful, emotionless voice.
- **Knows at series start:** the Emperor's will names the Warden-General as heir to the Rolls.

### Tamsin Orrey. Died aged 34, fourteen years before Book 1.

- **Role:** leader of the Saltmarch Rising.
- **History:** she promised her rebels an amnesty negotiation and instead sold their positions for her own pardon. She was hanged anyway, and Aurek, then 21, was the executioner. She appears only in memory, including Elin's borrowed memory in Book 2.

## Relationship worksheet

Couple: Elin Marrowgate (30) and Aurek Sall (35).

1. **What does each consciously want, visible in Chapter 1?**
   - **Elin:** to break the dams. At the wedding she carries Maud's instruction in her sleeve: once his name is given, use it before the Thaw Moon.
   - **Aurek:** to stop the delta flood and win the war council. In Chapter 1 he tells her, before the vows, "I know why you came," and gives her the name anyway. The reader sees the blue-wax order on his desk the same night.

2. **What do they secretly need?**
   - **Elin:** to be valued when she has nothing left to trade.
   - **Aurek:** to be chosen by someone who could simply order him, and doesn't.

3. **What belief about love or self holds them back, as a sentence they'd say?**
   - **Elin:** "Love is leverage that hasn't been spent yet."
   - **Aurek:** "No one keeps a promise they're not forced to."

4. **What does each misunderstand about the other, as a reasonable inference from what they know?**
   - **Elin** infers that he gave her his name as a gesture of imperial contempt. She reads it as: "Command me, little envoy, and hang for it." She knows the spouse clause makes her answerable. Before the midpoint she cannot see that he needs her to command him.
   - **Aurek** infers that she will command him the moment it profits Lissane, because everyone does, and that every kindness from her is groundwork. He has seen Tamsin Orrey spend loyalty, so he reads Elin's restraint as patience.

5. **What can each offer that nobody else can, tied to plot and world?**
   - **Elin** can lawfully command a Warden-General. She is the only person in the empire who can make his refusal of the Sluice legal. She also knows river trust-law, which no imperial jurist reads. That is where the split-name loophole lives.
   - **Aurek** can move the army, and he holds the Quiet Table's votes. He knows the charter, the Map Room, Hollin Weir's defences, and the war council's arithmetic. He is the only imperial officer who has ever been on the losing side of an empire.

6. **What makes loving the other morally or practically costly, in world-rule terms?**
   - For Elin, every request she makes might be an order. Every kiss might be her spending his consent.
   - If she ever commands him to an act of treason, she hangs and the treaty voids.
   - To love him without using the name is to fail Lissane's instruction while the delta dies.
   - For Aurek, loving her means trusting someone he cannot refuse.
   - Both are adults, and the marriage is real, consented to by her and given by him. Even so, the court treats any intimacy between holder and held as an instrument of state.
   - After the split, loving her means becoming partly her. Any wound she takes, he takes.

7. **What would make trust rational? Three observable choices.**
   - Ch. 10, Book 1: Hesper is commanded to report the Quiet Table meeting. Elin catches her and does not tell Aurek's people, who would kill her. Instead she covers for Hesper with a false meeting time. Aurek learns of this later and checks it. It is true.
   - Ch. 14, Book 1: Elin has the name and Aurek is about to walk into Vey's trap at the Map Room. She shouts his *use-name* instead of the command that would have stopped him. She proves she will spend danger before she spends him.
   - Ch. 21, Book 1: Aurek hands her the blue-wax Sluice order unopened. She could take it straight to Lissane's resistance and win her mission alone. She reads it in front of him and gives it back.

8. **What changes, in them and the world, because they fall in love?**
   - **Elin** stops treating love as an account and gives up sole command for good.
   - **Aurek** learns that a promise can hold without force. In Book 3 he gives away the most power anyone in the empire has ever held.
   - **The world:** the split name is the first recorded half-return of a name to its owner. It is legal precedent that a name can be held in trust. Over the series this leads to the end of the Rolls, the opening of the weirs, and the River Compact, a third institution that shares water between the Dry and the delta.

9. **Why are these two uniquely suited to challenge each other?**
   - They agree on what matters: no one should drown for someone else's thirst. They disagree on how.
   - She wants every dam down. He knows the Dry's children will starve without Hollin.
   - She is an advocate who wins by precision. He is a soldier who wins by economy.
   - Each is the only person who can see through the other's restraint: hers is strategic, and his is penitential.
   - She was conquered by the empire he serves. He once rebelled against it, as she wants to.

10. **What does each refuse to give up, even for the other?**
    - **Elin** will not give up the delta. She will never accept a plan that keeps the Saltreach Locks.
    - **Aurek** will not give up the Dry's water. He will not open Hollin in a way that starves the heartland, and he will not abandon the Quiet Table's men to save himself.

11. **What does the reader know before either character does?**
    - Through Aurek's POV, the reader learns the Sluice order (Book 1, ch. 5) before Elin does (ch. 16).
    - Through Elin's POV, the reader learns that Hesper reports under command (ch. 6) before Aurek does.
    - From planted clues the reader can suspect, before either lead, that the Emperor holds a river name: the treaty naming her, the Emperor watching her mouth, the river-script page Quill hides.
    - In Book 2 the reader knows Maud's bargain through Elin's borrowed fragments before Elin understands them.

## Book arcs

Every book has 32 chapters. Midpoint at ch. 16, point of ritual death at chs. 25–27, finale at chs. 28–32.

### Book 1: *A Name Held in Trust*

- **Central question:** will Elin use Aurek's name to stop the flooding of the delta?
- **Romantic phase completed:** meeting through declaration and first commitment. They go from enemies to allies to lovers and end married in fact. HFN.
- **Opening:**
  - Chapter 1: the wedding in Velmarch's chalk temple. He says "I know why you came," and then gives her the word.
  - The wedding night without sex, in which he sleeps on the floor by the door.
- **Regis elements:**
  - Society: Velmarch, the Rolls, the Dry.
  - Meeting: ch. 1.
  - Barrier: the treaty, the charter, and the dams.
  - Attraction: chs. 4–8, through competence.
  - Declaration: ch. 30.
  - Ritual death: chs. 25–27.
  - Recognition: ch. 29.
  - Betrothal: ch. 32, the remarriage by choice.
- **First kiss, ch. 14:**
  - It comes after she has shouted his use-name instead of the command.
  - Its cost: Hesper hears it and is commanded to report it. Vey learns the marriage is real, which makes Elin a target.
  - First explicit scene, ch. 22: she speaks his true name, alone, with nothing after it, and stops.
  - Second explicit scene, ch. 31: after the split, they feel each other's pleasure for the first time through the merge. It changes how they plan the hinge confrontation.
- **Midpoint reversal (ch. 16):**
  - Elin learns that Aurek has been ordered to lead the flood. He gave her his name so that she could stop *him*.
  - Under the spouse clause, her command is the only way his refusal is not treason.
  - The first half recasts. Every time he provoked her to command him, it was an invitation. The court's view of her as his weapon was his plan.
  - He also expected her to hang for it. He believed his mutiny would carry the council the same day and protect her, and he never asked her. The romance breaks on that: he chose for her.
- **Rising cost (chs. 17–24):**
  - Repair requires that he acknowledge he gambled her life without consent, and that he change. From ch. 20 he hands her the Quiet Table's plans and votes.
  - Hesper's commanded betrayal exposes the meeting house.
  - Aurek discloses Tamsin Orrey (ch. 21).
  - At Hollin, Elin sees the brass silt-sieves (ch. 23).
- **Point of ritual death (chs. 25–27):**
  - Vey releases Hollin early to force the issue. Sedge Ferry drowns.
  - Pell Dorran dies holding the Sedge Ferry sluice half-closed so that people can reach the stilts.
  - Aurek is taken to Hollin under Vey's guard to open the main gates at high water.
  - Elin is alone with the name. Commanding him alone would stop the flood, and she could add "and break the dams", which is her whole mission. She would hang, and the treaty would void.
  - Ressa's letter reaches her: "your name has gone quiet in the water."
- **Recognition and confrontation (chs. 28–30):**
  - Elin finds the loophole in river trust-law: a name may be held *for* its owner.
  - At the Hollin gate-walk, she splits the name and gives him half of his own. Together they speak the full name and the command to refuse the Sluice.
  - Both voices take a first rasp.
  - The Quiet Table's officers follow him, and Vey is disgraced for Sedge Ferry. The flood is stopped. The dams remain.
- **Heroine's belief change:** from "Love is leverage that hasn't been spent yet" to "Leverage spent on someone you love is love spent."
- **The choice that proves it:** at the gate she gives up sole command for ever, in the one moment when it would win her original mission whole. She could not have made this choice in ch. 1.
- **Real losses:** Pell Dorran and Sedge Ferry. Both stay lost.
- **Series hinge (ch. 32):**
  - At the victory audience the Emperor dismisses his Voice and speaks for himself. He says a word Elin has never heard, and her body kneels.
  - He holds her name, given to the river at her birth and taken from it.
  - Through the merge, Aurek feels her knees strike the stone.

### Book 2: *The River Remembers*

- **Central question:** can two people stay themselves inside one name?
- **Romantic phase completed:** deepening and testing. They go from lovers to partners who choose each other while partly merged. HFN.
- **Engine of the book:**
  - The Emperor's standing command from Book 1's hinge: "Come when I call, and answer me truly." It cost him most of his remaining voice.
  - Elin cannot lie to the Emperor about Aurek's plans.
  - Aurek must keep plans from the one person who dreams his memories.
  - Their shared wounds make every court duel and every dark street a risk for two.
  - This is planted betrayal as a clock on every tender scene, and both of them know the clock is there.
- **Explicit scenes:**
  - Ch. 11: they choose intimacy specifically in order to tell their own sensations apart from each other's.
  - Ch. 29: after the anchoring, they take turns, each saying aloud what is theirs.
- **Midpoint reversal (ch. 16):**
  - Corin finds the clay jars at the Saltreach Locks. Elin learns the weirs sieve names from river silt.
  - Her own name came out of Saltreach silt four years ago.
  - The Book 1 flood was never only about the Dry's water. A drowned delta would be a held lake, and every future child named in it would be caught.
  - This recasts Book 1, the Emperor, and Aurek's victory. The man he obeyed was building a cage for her people.
  - It also recasts the romance. Aurek's position, keep Hollin and stop only the flood, now keeps the sieves working.
- **Point of ritual death (chs. 25–27):**
  - The Emperor calls Elin and asks where the Quiet Table will meet. She must answer truly.
  - Ressa Hale dies when weir-clerks dredge the Mother Pools.
  - Maud's bargain is revealed through Elin's borrowed memory of Aurek reading the surrender documents.
  - Elin is stabbed in the Saltreach raid, and Aurek bleeds in Velmarch.
  - The merge peaks. She wakes thinking his thoughts, and cannot find which grief is hers.
- **Heroine's belief change:** from "If I'm partly him, I'm no longer anyone he chose" to "I choose who I am each morning. Being changed by him isn't being erased."
- **The choice that proves it:** in the war council she speaks in her own rasped voice *against* Aurek's position. Her vote, for the Locks to close and the delta to be restored, defeats his. She proves there are two people in the name. Aurek, having sworn nothing, stands and keeps his promise to back her vote anyway.
- **Real loss:** Ressa Hale. The Mother Pools are dredged and cannot be restored.
- **Series hinge (ch. 32):**
  - The Emperor dies of lung-fever.
  - His will leaves the Rolls to the Warden-General. Aurek now holds three thousand river names, four hundred court names including Ysolde's, and Elin's own name.

### Book 3: *Unnamed*

- **Central question:** will Elin free every held name in the empire, including the one that binds them?
- **Romantic phase completed:** final commitment without leverage. HEA, with a remarriage by river custom and no names exchanged.
- **Engine of the book:** a power seesaw.
  - Aurek holds Elin's name and has sworn never to speak it.
  - Elin holds half of his.
  - The Great Houses demand the Rolls. Ysolde wants her own name back and the throne.
- **Values collision:**
  - Elin wants every name given to the river now.
  - Aurek wants to hold them in trust until the Houses sign a peace, because freeing them overnight starts a civil war.
  - Both are right.
- **Explicit scenes:**
  - Ch. 13: he gives her the choice of whether he will ever speak her name. She chooses never, and it is the first choice anyone has ever given her about herself.
  - Ch. 31: the first time they are together with nothing held.
- **Midpoint reversal (ch. 16):**
  - A name given to a dammed river is sieved again. Freeing anyone means opening Hollin, which is what Aurek has refused to do for three books.
  - The value he would not give up becomes the obstacle to her freedom.
- **Point of ritual death (chs. 25–27):**
  - Ysolde, acting for the first time without permission, seizes the Map Room.
  - The Houses march on the Hall of Rolls.
  - Aurek, to stop the fighting, nearly breaks his vow and speaks Elin's name to command her out of the burning Hall. Through the merge, she feels him nearly do it. He does not, and she stays to save the Rolls.
- **Climax:**
  - Dov Ashcombe's managed breach opens Hollin in stages, and Dov dies doing it.
  - The River Compact is signed, the third institution: water shared between the Dry and the delta, with names belonging to the river.
  - At Saltreach, name after name, Aurek gives the Rolls to open water. Elin's name goes last.
  - Then the two of them give their split name to the river.
  - The command dies. The shared memories stay.
- **Heroine's belief change:** from "What I love, I must keep hold of" (the bond is now her safety) to "What's held in trust is held for the other's sake, then given back."
- **The choice that proves it:** she gives her half of his name to the river, giving up the last bond between them.
- **Aurek's matching proof:** he gives away more power than any emperor ever held. Elin's name, his last hold on her, is given away too.
- **Real loss:** Dov Ashcombe. Aurek also loses the Wardenry for ever, by his own resignation.
- **Series close:** no open hinge. The river runs unheld, the Compact's first season begins, and two people with rasped voices marry in reed-light with nothing given but their word.

## Theme

**Is trust real if betrayal is impossible?**

- **Answer 1: no.** Trust only exists where betrayal is possible, so a bond that can't be broken is obedience, not trust.
  - Elin holds this answer, earning it at Book 1's climax and acting on it fully in Book 3.
  - Corin holds a rougher version: "a chain's a chain."
- **Answer 2: yes, and binding is the more honest trust.**
  - The argument: promises enforced by law or name protect people who cannot afford to be betrayed. The Saltmarch rebels died because Tamsin Orrey's promise was unbound.
  - Aurek holds this answer at the start of the series, and it is legitimate. He bled for it.
  - Ysolde holds a pragmatic version: the Houses need held names, or they go to war.
  - The Emperor holds its corrupted form, everyone bound and no one trusted.
- **How the series resolves it:**
  - Neither answer wins outright.
  - The River Compact binds *institutions*, water shares and courts, and frees *persons*.
  - Elin and Aurek end choosing each other with nothing held.

## Cover style

- **Series look:** one style across all three books so they read as a set.
- **Palette (four colours):**
  - deep river teal-black for the field
  - chalk white
  - tarnished gold
  - one thin line of oxblood per cover, used only on the central motif
- **Central motif per book:**
  - **Book 1:** a single gold wedding band resting on dark still water, with three ripple rings spreading outward and one oxblood thread trailing from the band into the depth.
  - **Book 2:** one reed split cleanly down its length, the two halves bound back together with gold thread and casting a doubled reflection in water.
  - **Book 3:** a stone sluice gate cracked open, with white water pouring through and the gold band carried away in the current, the oxblood thread unwound and floating free.
- **Typography:**
  - **Title:** tall, high-contrast serif capitals with an inscribed, chiselled feel and generous letter-spacing, in chalk white with a faint gold edge.
  - **Pen name:** small, widely spaced geometric sans-serif capitals in tarnished gold, along the bottom.
  - **Series line:** "A Name Held in Trust, Book One" (or Two, Three) in small italic serif above the title.
- **Composition:**
  - Strictly symmetrical, with the motif dead centre in the lower-middle third.
  - The title sits across the upper third and the pen name on the bottom edge.
  - A fine gold border of stylised reeds frames all four edges, broken at the top centre by one gap.
  - There are no figures and no faces.
- **Mood:** hushed, intimate, dangerous. The cover should feel like something held under water.
- **Restrictions:** no real person's likeness and no trademarked imagery.

## Glossary

- **True name:** the single word that, once given, lets its holder command a person.
- **Use-name:** an everyday name with no power (Elin, Aurek).
- **The word:** how characters refer to a true name without speaking it. True names are never printed on the page.
- **Holder:** the person to whom a true name has been given.
- **Given name:** a true name that has been handed to a holder. Only a given name commands.
- **Command:** a holder speaking the true name followed by an imperative, in the named person's hearing.
- **Standing command:** a command that binds until a condition recurs or ends. Each word costs the speaker as if it were a separate command.
- **Rasp:** the permanent scar a command leaves on the speaker's voice.
- **The Voice:** the officer who speaks the Emperor's words in public.
- **The Rolls:** the Crown's ciphered register of held names, kept in the Hall of Rolls.
- **Rollkeeper:** the official in charge of the Rolls.
- **Void:** a name given whole to its owner. It has no effect and stays with the giver.
- **Split, or halving:** dividing a held name between two holders. Commands then need both halves spoken together.
- **Merge:** the shared memories and wounds of two holders of a split name.
- **Water-named:** a river person whose name was given into the delta at birth and is unknown even to them.
- **Unpledged:** the imperial legal term for the water-named.
- **River Mother:** a Lissane elder who names infants at the Mother Pools.
- **Quiet ones:** water-named people whose names no longer answer in the water because they have been sieved.
- **Weir-clerk:** an imperial clerk who sieves names from trapped river silt.
- **Silt-jar:** a sealed clay jar of dredged silt awaiting sieving.
- **The Sluice:** the planned opening of Hollin Weir at Thaw Moon high water to drown the delta.
- **The Dry:** the drought-struck imperial heartland.
- **The Dry Wars:** the empire's campaigns of conquest for water.
- **Wardenry Charter:** the three-hundred-year-old law of the army's high command.
- **Spouse clause:** the charter rule that a Warden's name may be held only by a spouse, who answers for acts commanded through it.
- **The Quiet Table:** Aurek's mutiny network inside the war council.
- **Treaty of Lissane:** the peace that ended the conquest. It set the marriage as a term, named the bride, and voids the peace if the bride commits treason.
- **River Compact:** the third institution of Book 3, which shares water between the Dry and the delta and gives held names to the river.
- **Reed Moon, Thaw Moon:** months of the imperial calendar. Reed Moon is early spring and Thaw Moon is high water.
- **YC (Year of Casimir):** imperial year reckoning by the Emperor's reign.
