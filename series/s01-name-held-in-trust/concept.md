# A Name Held in Trust

## Hook

To seal the peace, the conquered river province of Lissane sends its envoy, Elin Marrowgate, to
marry the empire's Warden-General. At the wedding, by imperial custom, a husband gives his wife
his true name: the one word that commands him completely. Her people have sent her to use it.
Aurek Sall knows exactly why she came. He gives her his name anyway.

## Leads

- **Elin Marrowgate, 30.** Envoy and river-law advocate. *Wants* to free Lissane from the
  empire's dams, which are slowly starving its delta. *Needs* to believe she's worth more than
  her usefulness. *False belief:* "Love is leverage that hasn't been spent yet." *Contradictory
  habit:* she memorises everyone's kindnesses and never uses them.
- **Aurek Sall, 35.** Warden-General, the empire's most decorated soldier, with a defeated
  rebellion in his past. *Wants* to stop the Emperor's plan to flood the delta entirely. *Needs*
  to be chosen rather than obeyed. *False belief:* "No one keeps a promise they're not forced
  to." He has his own goal: a slow mutiny inside the war council.

## World rule: Name-Ownership

1. **Can:** whoever holds a person's true name can command them, and they can't refuse.
2. **Never:** a name can't be taken back once given.
3. **Cost:** every command spoken through the name scars the speaker's own voice. Command him
   enough and she will be mute.
4. **Who benefits:** the imperial houses, who hold their servants' names.
5. **Who is excluded:** river peoples, whose names are given to the water at birth.
6. **Lovers:** trust is made literal. Every request she makes could be an order.
7. **Dangerous intimate act:** naming. Even saying his name aloud in bed is a weapon.
8. **Recurring dilemma:** use the name to save her people, or never use it and prove he can be
   trusted.
9. **Loophole:** a name can be split between two holders.
10. **Cost of the loophole:** each holder becomes partly the other, sharing memories and wounds.

## Book-by-book

- **Book 1, *A Name Held in Trust*.** Will Elin use Aurek's name to stop the flooding of the
  delta? Midpoint: she learns he gave her his name so that she could stop *him*. He has been
  ordered to lead the flood. Ending: she splits the name instead of commanding him, and they
  stop the flood together. Hinge: the Emperor holds a name too, and it is Elin's, given to the
  river at her birth and taken from it.
- **Book 2, *The River Remembers*.** Bound halves of one name, with shared wounds and shared
  memories. Can two people stay themselves inside one name?
- **Book 3, *Unnamed*.** Will Elin free every held name in the empire, including the one that
  binds them?

Structure: one couple across a trilogy.

## Why readers will want it

- **Being chosen:** he gives her total power over him, knowing why she came.
- **Safety inside danger:** a lethal man who disciplines his power.
- **Dramatic irony:** each lead holds half the secret.
- **Tropes:** arranged marriage plus secret mission, enemies to lovers (conqueror and conquered),
  forbidden.

## Originality scorecard

| Test | Pass | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Swap the magic | Yes | Without Name-Ownership there's no power gift and no dilemma |
| 2. Theme as a two-sided question | Yes | Is trust real if betrayal is impossible? |
| 3. Love interest's own goal | Yes | The mutiny in the war council |
| 4. Not solved by killing a villain | Yes | Killing the Emperor doesn't free the names |
| 5. Heroine changes a belief | Yes | From "love is leverage" to a gift she never spends |
| 6. One convention inverted, one honoured | Yes | Inverted: the wife holds the power. Honoured: the arranged-marriage slow burn |
| 7. Describable without comps | Yes | "She's given her husband's true name and sent to use it" |
| 8. Intimate act made political | Yes | Naming is an act of state |

## Risks

- Readers may be wary of arranged-marriage consent. Show his consent to the name, and hers to
  the marriage, explicitly. Intimacy waits until both choose it freely.
- The empire must stay specific, not generically evil.
