# The Oracle Cannot Lie

## First look

- **Title:** The Oracle Cannot Lie
- **Alternatives:** A God in Her Mouth; The Truthful Rival
- **Series:** The Gathering of Gods (4 books, one couple per book)
- **Hook line:** A mountain oracle who cannot lie must win her god's case at the great gathering, but her rival speaks for the enemy god, and loving him would silence hers.
- **Tropes:** rivals to lovers, forbidden love, slow burn, touch her and die protector
- **Comps (shelf only):** The Cruel Prince; The Jasmine Throne
- **Cover concept:** Two silver ritual masks, one ram-horned and one snow-white, face each other nose to nose over a snowy ridge, their breath mingling.

## Leads

**Tara, 30, oracle of the Grey Ram of Sarol valley.**
- Want: to win back the stream that Dhura's dam has cut off before her village's crops fail.
- Need: to stop treating herself as only a vessel and claim a voice of her own.
- False belief: "Justice has one right answer, and my god already knows it."

**Vikram, 34, oracle of the Snow-Eyed Lady of Dhura, former mercenary captain.**
- Want: to find who poisoned his predecessor at the last gathering.
- Need: to believe his violent past doesn't disqualify him from being loved.
- False belief: "A man who has killed for pay can only be useful, never trusted."

## World rule

1. **Can:** gods speak and vote through oracles at the gathering; oracles know when others lie.
2. **Never:** an oracle can never speak a lie, possessed or not.
3. **Cost:** a god withdraws from an oracle who takes a lover, and the valley loses its vote and protection.
4. **Who benefits:** the gathering's council and valleys with steady oracles.
5. **Who's excluded:** valleys whose oracles die or fall, which become vassals.
6. **Lovers:** any direct question about her heart forces a true answer.
7. **Dangerous intimate act:** being asked; a kiss also ends the office.
8. **Recurring dilemma:** stay silent (forfeiting a point in court) or answer and expose themselves.
9. **Loophole:** two gods may share one pair of oracles if both consent before the gathering.
10. **Loophole cost:** the two valleys merge their vote and lose independence forever.

## Folklore and respect notes

- **Drawn from:** Himachal devta courts, where village gods rule through oracles, hold court, travel in palanquin processions and meet at a great festival. Set in invented Kullu- and Mandi-like valleys.
- **Invented:** all gods (the Grey Ram, the Snow-Eyed Lady) and the gathering itself. No real devta, temple or festival is named.
- **Respect:** this is a living practice, so possession scenes are written with dignity and never romantic or sexual. Romance happens only between the two adult humans, outside possession.
- **Respect:** the office of oracle traditionally falls to men in many villages; Tara's valley is an invented exception, stated as such.
- **Kept out:** major deities, caste hierarchy among valleys, and any mockery of the rite.

## Book-by-book

1. **The Oracle Cannot Lie** (Tara and Vikram).
   - Question: who gets the stream?
   - Ending: they invoke the shared-oracle loophole, merging the valleys at the cost of both valleys' independence and Tara's sole office. HEA.
   - Hinge: the High Seat's god has been silent for years, and someone is faking its voice.
2. **The Silent God** (Nisha, 33, gathering scribe; Arun, 36, bodyguard to the suspected forger).
   - Question: who speaks for the silent god?
   - Ending: HEA.
3. **The Fallen Oracle** (Kamla, 44, an oracle her god left twenty years ago; Hari, 47, the man she left it for).
   - Question: can a second chance be worth a valley?
   - Ending: HEA.
4. **The Last Procession.**
   - Question: should gods keep their votes at all? The finale couple is drawn from book 3's world.
   - Ending: HEA, and the gathering is rebuilt.

## Why readers will click

"Cannot lie" is a five-second hook with built-in banter and danger. Rival advocates in a trial of gods give a court-intrigue shelf with fresh mountain specifics. A protective ex-soldier hero hits "touch her and die."

## Risks

- Living practice: needs the owner's cultural review first, and the gods must stay clearly invented.
- Court plots can bog down: keep the case to one stream and one deadline.
- The shared-oracle loophole must be planted early so the ending feels earned, not convenient.
