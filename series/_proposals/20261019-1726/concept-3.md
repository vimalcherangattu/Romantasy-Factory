# The Iron Nail

## First look

- **Title:** The Iron Nail
- **Alternatives:** Bound in Iron; Her Hunger, His Oath
- **Series:** Iron and Hunger (3 books, one couple)
- **Hook line:** A bound night-spirit must win her freedom from the man who holds her iron nail, but every kiss feeds her on his life, and freeing her unleashes her hunger.
- **Tropes:** captive and captor, forbidden love, monster romance, slow burn, morally grey love interest
- **Comps (shelf only):** Uprooted; Kingdom of the Wicked
- **Cover concept:** A single rusted iron nail driven through a white pala blossom, a red-bordered sari trailing into black lagoon water below.

## Leads

**Malli, yakshi, died at 26 and adult in mind and body, bound eighty years.**
- Want: freedom from the nail, and the house's ruin.
- Need: to be desired with her hunger, not in spite of it, and to grieve the life she lost.
- False belief: "No one born to that house can be anything but my jailer."

**Kiran, 34, eldest man and keeper of a debt-ruined house.**
- Want: to keep his niece's inherited land from the creditor-lord Raghavan.
- Need: to repair a crime he didn't commit but profits from.
- False belief: "The past is paid by keeping the house standing."

## World rule

1. **Can:** a nailed yakshi wakes for her nail-holder and can strike his enemies at night.
2. **Never:** leave the tree's shadow, or feed on anyone but the keeper while bound.
3. **Cost:** each feeding, by breath or blood, costs the keeper a season of life.
4. **Who benefits:** the nail-holding house, which uses her as a guardian and weapon.
5. **Who's excluded:** the yakshi herself, who has no will over the nail.
6. **Lovers:** a kiss is a feeding; desire spends his life.
7. **Dangerous intimate act:** touch.
8. **Recurring dilemma:** feed and age him, or starve and fade into the tree.
9. **Loophole:** if the keeper gives her the nail freely, she owns her binding and can choose whom to feed on.
10. **Loophole cost:** she is free to feed on anyone, the house loses its guardian, and Kiran becomes the target of every enemy she kept at bay.

## Folklore and respect notes

- **Drawn from:** Kerala yakshi lore (a beautiful, dangerous night-spirit of the pala tree, bound by an iron nail). Tharavad matriliny means the land belongs to his niece and he manages it.
- **Setting:** invented lagoon country inspired by Kuttanad.
- **Kept out:** named real yakshi legends and shrines, and mantravadam as a real practice. Binding is an invented craft of iron-wrights.
- **Respect:** her murder is referred to, never eroticised. Caste does not appear as backstory spice; the crime is a land grab.
- **Content:** the first explicit scene happens only after he has given her the nail, so she chooses with full power.

## Book-by-book

1. **The Iron Nail.**
   - Question: will Kiran keep her bound or free her?
   - Midpoint: he gives her the nail.
   - Climax: she protects the niece's deed by choice. Kiran has lost eleven years of life, a loss that stays lost.
   - Ending: HFN.
   - Hinge: his grandfather nailed six more spirits across the lagoon.
2. **Seven Nails.**
   - Question: free the others, who want revenge on the families who now hold them?
   - Ending: HFN.
   - Hinge: Raghavan's family holds the oldest nail.
3. **Hunger's End.**
   - Question: should a yakshi become mortal for love?
   - Ending: she refuses, and he becomes the grove's sworn keeper; love that keeps her hunger. HEA.

## Why readers will click

Monster romance with the heroine as the monster is a hot, underused angle. A nail-through-blossom cover reads as gothic at thumbnail size. Captive-and-captor with a rule that makes every kiss cost life is high-stakes yearning.

## Risks

- Captor power imbalance: he must be shown to acknowledge it, and the power transfer must come before any intimacy.
- A grandson-of-killer love interest can turn readers off: he must earn repair through costly acts.
- "Yakshi" stays out of the title; the cover and the hook carry the concept.
