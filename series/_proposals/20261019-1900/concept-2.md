# Debt of Warmth

## Hook

In the frozen city of Velmar, heat is money, and it passes only by touch. Sorrel Ashdown audits
warmth debts for the Ledger Office: she collects what the poor owe by taking it from their
skin. Her newest case is Kestrel Vane, a smuggler who runs an illegal hearth that gives warmth
away for free. When a killing frost traps them together in his hidden hearth-house, she learns
the one thing the Ledger hides: two people who touch with full consent, while telling each
other only the truth, create heat out of nothing.

## Leads

- **Sorrel Ashdown, 28.** Senior auditor of the Ledger Office. *Wants* to pay off her family's
  inherited warmth-debt before winter takes her younger brother. *Needs* to accept warmth
  without keeping a ledger of it. *False belief:* "Everything given is owed back, with interest."
  *Contradictory habit:* she secretly overpays the debts of the people she audits.
- **Kestrel Vane, 33.** Former Ledger enforcer turned smuggler. *Wants* to keep the free hearths
  running through the Long Dark. *Needs* to forgive himself for the families he froze as an
  enforcer. *False belief:* "Honesty is a weapon people use on you." He has his own goal: find out
  who is buying up the city's coal reserves.

## World rule: the Warmth Economy

1. **Can:** body heat can be transferred by touch and stored in ledger-glass.
2. **Never:** heat can't be created, only moved. Or so the Ledger teaches.
3. **Cost:** every transfer leaves the giver colder, and the debt is recorded on the skin as
   frost-marks.
4. **Who benefits:** the Ledger houses that own the glass.
5. **Who is excluded:** the debtors of the lower city.
6. **Lovers:** every touch impoverishes one partner, unless the loophole holds.
7. **Dangerous intimate act:** touch, and honesty.
8. **Recurring dilemma:** take what's owed and keep her brother alive, or give and freeze.
9. **Loophole:** contact with mutual consent creates surplus heat, but only while both are
   completely honest. One lie, and the heat reverses.
10. **Cost of the loophole:** intimacy becomes interrogation, and every secret she keeps for the
    Office can kill him.

## Book-by-book

- **Book 1, *Debt of Warmth*.** Will Sorrel expose the loophole, which would collapse the economy
  that keeps her brother's debt from being called in? Midpoint: the surplus fails during their
  first kiss, because she is still filing reports on him. Ending: she publishes the loophole, and
  the Ledger falls. Hinge: the coal reserves were bought to fuel something under the city that
  is waking up.
- **Book 2, *The Long Dark*.** A new couple: Sorrel's brother, Wren (27), and the Ledger heir
  who now owes the city everything. Can a city run on trust survive a winter without end?
- **Book 3, *Thaw*.** The thing beneath Velmar wants warmth too. Who does the city owe first?

Structure: one couple per book, in the same city, with Sorrel and Kestrel returning.

## Why readers will want it

- **Rest:** a hearth where vigilance can stop.
- **Desired unchanged:** honesty is the price of heat, so they have to be fully seen.
- **Moral intensity:** order against mercy.
- **Sensual fulfilment:** touch has stakes in every scene, and heat literally changes the plot.
- **Tropes:** enemies to lovers (auditor and smuggler), forced proximity (the storm shelter),
  forbidden. A slow burn with a costed first kiss.

## Originality scorecard

| Test | Pass | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Swap the magic | Yes | Without the Warmth Economy, touch carries no cost and no surplus |
| 2. Theme as a two-sided question | Yes | Is a world of debts fairer than a world of gifts? |
| 3. Love interest's own goal | Yes | Finding the coal buyer and keeping the hearths alive |
| 4. Not solved by killing a villain | Yes | The problem is an economic system |
| 5. Heroine changes a belief | Yes | From "everything is owed" to accepting a gift |
| 6. One convention inverted, one honoured | Yes | Inverted: she holds the institutional power. Honoured: forced proximity |
| 7. Describable without comps | Yes | "In a frozen city where heat is currency passed by touch…" |
| 8. Intimate act made political | Yes | Touch and honesty are economic acts |

## Risks

- The economics must stay simple. One rule, shown in chapter 1 at a cost.
- The honesty loophole must not turn into miscommunication. Every lie must be a choice with a
  reason.
